In 1998, the aftershocks of the Asian financial crisis still lingered, and the global semiconductor industry had fallen into its coldest winter in nearly three decades. Rivals were cutting staff, closing plants, retreating. One seventy-year-old man moved the other way. He doubled down on research. He expanded into the downturn. "A recession," he said, "is the best time to hire talent." His name is Morris Chang. His company is TSMC. This is the story of his second act.
