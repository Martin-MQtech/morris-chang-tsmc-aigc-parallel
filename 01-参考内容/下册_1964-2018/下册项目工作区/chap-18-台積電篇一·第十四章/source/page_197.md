## 五月台湾访问与七月决定

五月，我实践对徐贤修的答应，到台湾访问共十一天。在一九八一、八二年两次访问台湾时，我对工研院已稍有了解。这次，听了副院长（胡定华）以及各所（工研院当时共五所）所长的详细报告；十一天内，与徐贤修每天见面，与行政院院长俞国华见面两次，与李国鼎也见面好几次。

除了鼓励我接受工研院院长职，李国鼎重提他三年前（一九八二年）对我提过的创投基金，这三年，他的创投计划已有稍进展，他与在Hambrecht & Quist（H&Q）任职的徐大麟君谈在台湾设创投，于是李国鼎又重萌我亦可在创投扮演一个角色的念头，并且立刻介绍那时也在台湾的徐大麟给我，希望我和徐大麟可以合作。

十一天访问结束时，我是否接受工研院院长职尚无结论。我的条件是：我不计较待遇，可以接受任何俞院长及徐董事长认为合理的待遇，但我只答应「半时」工作，因为我要保留另一半时给李国鼎的创投，或其他在美国的工作（例如天使投资人）。

六月二十四日，徐贤修来纽约访我，告诉我俞院长已答应我的条件，只要我承诺：（一）不要对外说我只是半时；（二）创投与工研院应分开。徐也告诉我俞院长开出来的薪资。如考虑「半时」工作，这薪资相当合理。

六月二十六、七日，我去了旧金山访问H&Q的负责人，询问他们「台湾创投基金」的详细，结果是：「台湾创投基金」无论规模及我与徐大麟君的附带利益，都相当使我失望；更有甚者，徐大麟君本来预备独自经营「台湾创投基金」，现在如果要和我一起经营，而且分享「附带利益」，明显有点失望。所以李政委要我在创投扮演一个角色的主意，并不实际。

纽约的春天快快地过去，现在已到七月，我也已「沉潜」了三个多月。几乎整个七月，我陷入「此后做什么事」的沉思中。经济方面我已无后顾之忧，最重要的考量是：来的工作是否具足够挑战性，能使我再度振作；比较次要，但仍很重要的考量是工作地点和环境。我能否适应？

美国，尤其纽约、德克萨斯、或硅谷，是我最能适应的地点。但挑战性工作机会？在一九八五年七月看来，恐怕只有创业投资（如一九八四年，见第十三章）或继续做「经营顾问」及天使投资人。

台湾呢？无论工作或居住环境，对我都很陌生。但工研院职位看来颇具挑战性。至于徐贤修和俞院长对我「半时」的应承，明显地非常勉强；况且我原本要求「半时」的原意——参加李国鼎的「台湾创投基金」以及终结现存的「经营顾问」及天使投资人工作——一半已不存在（我已发现李政委对我参加「台湾创投基金」的期望并不实际）。我只要结束在纽约的工作（大约一年内）就可以全时在台湾；即使未来一年，也可以至少「四分之三时」在台湾。

七月二十四日，我决定了，我打电话给徐贤修，接受工研院院长职位。

五十四岁，再不闯天下以后也不会了。

<p class="en-para"><b>The May Taiwan Visit and the July Decision</b></p>
<p class="en-para">In May, I kept my promise to Hsu and visited Taiwan for eleven days. During my 1981 and 1982 visits, I had gained some understanding of ITRI. This time, I heard detailed reports from the Vice President (Hu Dinghua) and the directors of each institute (ITRI then had five institutes). Over the eleven days, I met with Hsu daily, saw Premier Yu Guohua twice, and met with Li Guoding several times.</p>
<p class="en-para">Beyond encouraging me to accept the ITRI presidency, Li Guoding revisited the venture capital fund he had proposed three years earlier (1982). In the intervening years, his VC plans had made some progress — he had discussed setting up a VC fund in Taiwan with Mr. Hsu Ta-lin at Hambrecht & Quist (H&Q). So Li rekindled the idea that I could play a role in the VC fund and immediately introduced me to Hsu Ta-lin, who was also in Taiwan at the time, hoping that Hsu and I could collaborate.</p>
<p class="en-para">At the end of the eleven-day visit, I had not yet decided whether to accept the ITRI presidency. My terms: I did not care about compensation and would accept whatever Premier Yu and Chairman Hsu deemed reasonable. But I would only agree to work "half-time," because I wanted to reserve the other half for Li Guoding's VC fund or other U.S.-based work (such as angel investing).</p>
<p class="en-para">On June 24, Hsu came to New York to see me. He told me that Premier Yu had agreed to my conditions, provided I promised: (1) not to tell outsiders I was only half-time; (2) to keep the VC fund separate from ITRI. Hsu also told me the salary Premier Yu was offering. Given the "half-time" arrangement, the compensation was quite reasonable.</p>
<p class="en-para">On June 26-7, I visited San Francisco to meet with H&Q's principals and inquired about the details of the "Taiwan VC Fund." The result: whether in terms of scale or the carried interest for Hsu Ta-lin and me, the Taiwan VC Fund was deeply disappointing. Worse, Hsu Ta-lin had originally planned to run the fund alone; now having to co-run it and share carried interest with me was clearly unwelcome. So Minister Li's idea of my playing a role in the VC fund was simply impractical.</p>
<p class="en-para">New York's spring quickly passed; it was now July, and I had been "submerged" in reflection for nearly three months. Throughout most of July, I was lost in contemplation about "what to do next." Financially, I had no worries. The most important consideration: would the work be challenging enough to excite me again? A secondary but still important consideration: the work location and environment. Could I adapt?</p>
<p class="en-para">The United States — especially New York, Texas, or Silicon Valley — was where I could adapt most easily. But challenging job opportunities? As of July 1985, the options seemed limited to venture capital (as in 1984, see Chapter 13) or continuing as a "management consultant" and angel investor.</p>
<p class="en-para">Taiwan? Both the work and living environment were quite foreign to me. But the ITRI position seemed genuinely challenging. As for Hsu and Premier Yu's accommodation of my "half-time" request, it was clearly reluctant. Moreover, the original rationale for "half-time" — participating in Li Guoding's "Taiwan VC Fund" while winding down my "management consulting" and angel investing — was half gone (I had realized that Minister Li's expectations about my VC involvement were unrealistic). I could go full-time in Taiwan within about a year of winding down my New York work; even during that year, I could spend at least "three-quarters time" in Taiwan.</p>
<p class="en-para">On July 24, I decided. I called Hsu Hsiu-hsien and accepted the ITRI presidency.</p>
<p class="en-para">Fifty-four years old — if I didn't venture forth now, I never would.</p>

### Cheat Sheet · 商业语汇

1. **Carried interest (附带利益/绩效分成)**
**中文解释**：创投基金中普通合伙人（GP）从基金利润中分得的报酬，通常为利润的20%；这是创投行业最核心的利益激励机制。
**商业造句**：*The carried interest structure aligns the GP's incentives with the LPs' — both profit when portfolio companies succeed, creating a powerful motivation for value creation.*

2. **Angel investor (天使投资人)**
**中文解释**：以个人资金投资初创公司的早期投资者；张忠谋在离开通用器材后短暂从事天使投资，这是他接触初创企业生态的起点。
**商业造句**：*Chang's brief stint as an angel investor gave him firsthand exposure to the startup world — experience that would later inform his approach to building TSMC's customer ecosystem.*

3. **"If I didn't venture forth now, I never would" (再不闯天下以后也不会了)**
**中文解释**：张忠谋在五十四岁做出重大职业转折时的自我激励；放弃美国优渥生活、赴陌生台湾，是他人生中最关键的一次「闯」。
**商业造句**：*Chang's decision to leave New York for Taiwan at 54 was a rare act of entrepreneurial courage — trading the comforts of a known world for the uncertainty of building something entirely new.*
