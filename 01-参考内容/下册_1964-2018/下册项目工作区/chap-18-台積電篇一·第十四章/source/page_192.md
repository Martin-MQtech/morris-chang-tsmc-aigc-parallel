## 海格底与一九八一年台湾访问

此后李政委结识了刚自德仪董事长职位退休的海格底。一年后（一九七八年），海格底应李政委邀请，专程访问台湾一周，而且受李政委邀请组织行政院科技顾问团，此事已详述在本书第十章。

不幸海格底在一九八〇年患胰脏癌，英年早逝。自发现癌症至过世前后只不过几个月左右。在海格底病危时，德仪认为这是海格底个人的「隐私」，所以只有极少数人知道他病危的消息，我那时已在德仪「下山」途中，只知道海格底生病，不知道他病危。倒是在海格底病危期间再度访问德仪，没见到海格底，却被告知海格底病危。于是，在与我聚餐时，倒是他——一个外人——告诉我海格底病危。从我一脸惊异的表情，他也猜到我应已不在德仪核心圈内，并且直爽地问：「你不在经营委员会里了？」我虽痛心，但不否认。

此后两年李政委开始征求我到台湾工作。第一步在一九八一年初，他要求德仪董事长夏伯特「借用」我两星期，要我参观台湾电子工业并对他及行政院院长孙运璿做建议。他第一次做此要求被夏伯特拒绝，夏推说我刚有新任务（品质总监），不肯借；第二次再要时，夏伯特有点不胜其烦，答应了。我在「品质总监」职务内，本就预备在一九八一的三、四月到欧亚各德仪工厂走一遍，现在腾出四月十二至二十二的十天，专门为李政委为台湾电子工业做建议。

这十天中我看了十三家工厂和两个实验室（工研院电子所及交通部电信所），与李政委及工研院方贤齐院长吃了好几次晚餐；从这次起，以后我访问台湾，李政委常邀请当时的经济部部长和财政部部长与我一起晚餐，甚至有一次他说：「我把张博士交给你们了。」

这次离台前，我拜会行政院院长孙运璿，口头报告此行的建议。回到达拉斯后写了一份九页的报告给李政委，这份报告我至今还保留，现在把它纲要摘录如下：

**日期：一九八一年五月四日**

**致：李国鼎政委**

台湾电子业在最近十年有爆发性的成长，但几乎所有成长都基于台湾的低工资。当别的比台湾工资更低的地区开始开放，政府的目标——要电子工业从「劳力密集」升级到「技术密集」，非常合时。

「技术密集」与「劳力密集」工业需要很不一样的经济及社会基础设施，也许更重要的，它们需要很不一样的经营者心态。要务不是开发几个个别的技术，而是建立一个奖励创新的社会及经济环境。

<p class="en-para"><b>Haggerty and the 1981 Taiwan Visit</b></p>
<p class="en-para">Minister Li subsequently became acquainted with Eric Haggerty, who had recently retired as TI's Chairman. A year later (1978), at Minister Li's invitation, Haggerty made a week-long visit to Taiwan and, at Minister Li's request, organized the Executive Yuan's Science and Technology Advisory Group (STAG), as detailed in Chapter 10 of this book.</p>
<p class="en-para">Sadly, Haggerty was diagnosed with pancreatic cancer in 1980 and died young. Only a few months passed between diagnosis and death. When Haggerty was critically ill, TI considered it his "private matter" and very few people knew of his condition. I was already on my "downhill" path at TI by then and only knew that Haggerty was ill, not that he was critical. Ironically, during Haggerty's final illness, Minister Li visited TI again. Not meeting Haggerty, he was told Haggerty was critical. At dinner with me, it was Minister Li — an outsider — who informed me of Haggerty's condition. From my visible shock, he surmised I was no longer in TI's inner circle and bluntly asked: "You're no longer on the Operations Committee?" Though pained, I did not deny it.</p>
<p class="en-para">Over the next two years, Minister Li began recruiting me to work in Taiwan. The first step came in early 1981, when he asked TI Chairman Shepherd to "borrow" me for two weeks to visit Taiwan's electronics industry and advise him and Premier Sun Yun-suan. The first time he made this request, Shepherd refused, excusing that I had just taken on a new assignment (Quality Director) and could not be spared. The second time, Shepherd, somewhat annoyed, agreed. Within my Quality Director role, I had already planned to visit TI factories across Europe and Asia in March-April 1981. Now I carved out ten days (April 12–22) specifically for Minister Li to advise Taiwan's electronics industry.</p>
<p class="en-para">During those ten days, I visited thirteen factories and two laboratories (ITRI's Electronics Research Institute and the Ministry of Transportation's Telecommunications Institute). I had several dinners with Minister Li and ITRI President Fang Xianqi. From this trip onward, whenever I visited Taiwan, Minister Li would often invite the current Minister of Economic Affairs and Minister of Finance to join me for dinner. Once he even said: "I'm entrusting Dr. Chang to you."</p>
<p class="en-para">Before leaving Taiwan, I paid a courtesy call on Premier Sun Yun-suan to give an oral summary of my recommendations. After returning to Dallas, I wrote a nine-page report to Minister Li, which I still preserve today. Its outline follows:</p>
<p class="en-para"><b>Date: May 4, 1981</b></p>
<p class="en-para"><b>To: Minister K.T. Li</b></p>
<p class="en-para">Taiwan's electronics industry has experienced explosive growth in the past decade, but nearly all of that growth has been predicated on Taiwan's low wages. As other regions with even lower wages begin to open up, the government's goal — to upgrade the electronics industry from "labor-intensive" to "technology-intensive" — is most timely.</p>
<p class="en-para">"Technology-intensive" and "labor-intensive" industries require very different economic and social infrastructure. Perhaps more importantly, they require very different managerial mindsets. The task is not to develop a few individual technologies, but to build a social and economic environment that rewards innovation.</p>

### Cheat Sheet · 商业语汇

1. **Downhill path (下山途中)**
**中文解释**：张忠谋自况在德仪职业生涯的下行阶段；「下山」隐喻失去核心圈地位、权力逐步被削弱的处境。
**商业造句**：*Chang's self-described "downhill path" at TI was a rare moment of self-awareness — recognizing that his influence had peaked and the only dignified option was to leave.*

2. **Operations Committee (经营委员会)**
**中文解释**：德仪最高决策机构，由董事长、总裁及核心高阶主管组成；被排除在经营委员会之外，意味着失去对重大决策的参与权。
**商业造句**：*Exclusion from the Operations Committee was the clearest signal that Chang had fallen from TI's inner circle — a political demotion more devastating than any title change.*

3. **Labor-intensive → Technology-intensive (劳力密集→技术密集)**
**中文解释**：产业升级的经典路径——从依赖低成本劳动力转向依赖技术知识；这是台湾在一九八〇年代经济转型的核心命题。
**商业造句**：*Taiwan's push from labor-intensive to technology-intensive industry in the 1980s mirrored the earlier trajectories of Japan and Korea — but with a unique emphasis on the semiconductor sector.*
