## 次微米计划与投标

工研院的"次微米计画"在九零年七月正式开始。史钦泰副院长深知开发记忆体需要设计及制程人才密切配合，他亲到美国求才，找到了卢志远、卢超群两兄弟。哥哥卢志远在贝尔实验室开发制程，弟弟卢超群在IBM设计——两人在各自领域都相当杰出，把他们两人找进"次微米计画"的确会是一个不错的安排。

但是问题发生了。志远愿意受聘为电子所副所长，并主持"次微米计画"，但超群志在创业，要在台湾开一家记忆体设计公司（即是后来的钰创科技），所以只愿意做"次微米计画"的设计承办者。史副院长在没有更好选择情形下，也同意了这安排。

这安排在第一个产品的开发相当成功，四年后（九四年中期），电子所和钰创团队已成功开发4M DRAM。但这安排的另一任务却被忽略了。4M DRAM的设计可以由钰创承办，但是下一个产品呢？本来要在"次微米计画"下建立自己的设计团队，但是等到真的要做，就发现有基本的矛盾和困难。台湾的IC设计人才本来就少，遇到好的，谁雇他（她）？是电子所还是钰创？何况竞争雇设计人才的公司还不只钰创，九三年又另一家记忆体设计公司从"钰创"衍生出去。

总之，直至九四年，在世界先进衍生的前夕，隶属工研院"次微米计画"的DRAM设计工程师只有两、三位。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">ITRI's "Submicron Program" officially began in July 1990. Vice President Shi Qin-tai, keenly aware that memory development required close cooperation between design and process talent, personally went to the U.S. to recruit, finding the brothers Lu Chih-yuan and Lu Chao-chun. The elder brother Lu Chih-yuan developed processes at Bell Labs; the younger brother Lu Chao-chun designed at IBM—both were quite outstanding in their respective fields. Recruiting both into the "Submicron Program" was indeed a good arrangement.</p>
<p class="en-para">But a problem emerged. Chih-yuan was willing to serve as deputy director of the Electronics Research Institute and lead the "Submicron Program." But Chao-chun aspired to start his own business—he wanted to open a memory design company in Taiwan (which later became ProMOS Technologies)—so he was only willing to be the design contractor for the "Submicron Program." Vice President Shi, lacking a better alternative, also agreed to this arrangement.</p>
<p class="en-para">This arrangement was quite successful in developing the first product. Four years later (mid-1994), the ERI and ProMOS teams had successfully developed 4M DRAM. But another mission of this arrangement was neglected. The 4M DRAM design could be contracted to ProMOS, but what about the next product? The original intent was to build the program's own design team under the "Submicron Program," but when it came time to actually do it, fundamental contradictions and difficulties emerged. Taiwan's IC design talent was already scarce. When you found good talent, who hired them—ERI or ProMOS? Moreover, ProMOS wasn't the only company competing to hire design talent; in 1993 another memory design company "spun off" from ProMOS.</p>
<p class="en-para">In any case, by 1994, on the eve of Vanguard International's spin-off, the DRAM design engineers belonging to ITRI's "Submicron Program" numbered only two or three.</p>

## "无法拒绝的机会"

更巧的，九四年秋，台积电在台湾上市，上市几天后股价涨到142.5元，是原始投资成本的十几倍（见第二十二章）。不少台积电的原始团队都发了一笔不大不小的财，被"次微米计画"的团队羡慕。"他们能，为什么我们不能？"

赶快衍一家公司！赶快量！赶快上市！赶快发财！

在"次微米计画"团队急著要量4M DRAM、工研院电子所绝对看好衍生公司的情绪下，经济部在九三年底决定以"招标"方式决定谁主导衍生公司。

台积电呢？如我在本章开端说，我离开德仪后，已不想碰记忆体。九四年台积电的总经理是布鲁克；布鲁克是我德仪属下手当初他也不想再碰记忆体。但是，当时台积电第三号人物——营运副总，也是原来核心团队之首繁城，虽不太了解记忆体业，却十分看好"次微米计画"团队，认为我们必须"竞标"。

即使我和布鲁克能够说服曾繁城的热忱，我也认为如不投标，对台积电和我都会是很大的负数。原因有二：第一，自八九年至九四年，我是工研院董事长，虽然在"次微米计画"酝酿时未被征询，但在此计画执行时我也自以为要尽董事长责任，充分支持此计画，现在又怎么能在即将产生成果时撒手？

第二，当初我替工研院的VLSI计画找出路，衍生了台积电，而台积电又非常成功。现在工研院又要衍生一个半导体公司，难道我拒绝参与？正因为是台积电成功，大家才会有这样的期待。

现在，又一个"无法拒绝的机会"出现。还是去吧！不负大家的期望。再募一次资，再办一个公司。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">Coincidentally, in the fall of 1994, TSMC listed in Taiwan. Within a few days of listing, the stock price rose to NT$142.5, more than ten times the original investment cost (see Chapter 22). Many of TSMC's founding team members made a modest fortune, which was envied by the "Submicron Program" team. "They can do it, why can't we?"</p>
<p class="en-para">Spin off a company quickly! Ramp production quickly! Go public quickly! Get rich quickly!</p>
<p class="en-para">Amid the "Submicron Program" team's eagerness to ramp 4M DRAM production, and ERI's unwavering optimism about the spin-off company, the Ministry of Economic Affairs decided at year-end 1993 to determine who would lead the spin-off company through a "bidding" process.</p>
<p class="en-para">As for TSMC? As I said at the beginning of this chapter, after leaving TI I had no desire to touch memory again. In 1994, TSMC's general manager was Brook; Brook was my former TI subordinate who had experienced the ups and downs of memory with me, and he didn't want to touch memory again either. But the then-third-ranking figure at TSMC—COO and also leader of the original core team Fan-cheng—though not very knowledgeable about the memory business, was extremely bullish on the "Submicron Program" team and believed we must "bid."</p>
<p class="en-para">Even if Brook and I could have convinced Fan-cheng to temper his enthusiasm, I also believed that not bidding would be a significant negative for both TSMC and me. There were two reasons: First, from 1989 to 1994, I was ITRI chairman. Although I wasn't consulted when the "Submicron Program" was brewing, during the program's execution I felt it my duty as chairman to fully support it. How could I now abandon it when results were about to bear fruit?</p>
<p class="en-para">Second, originally I had found an outlet for ITRI's VLSI program by spinning off TSMC, and TSMC had become hugely successful. Now ITRI wanted to spin off another semiconductor company—could I refuse to participate? Precisely because TSMC had succeeded, people had these expectations of me.</p>
<p class="en-para">Now, another "offer I couldn't refuse" appeared. Let's just go! Live up to everyone's expectations. Raise funds again, build another company.</p>
