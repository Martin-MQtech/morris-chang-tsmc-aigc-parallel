# 德仪篇·第五章｜四个上司

海格底和夏伯特——

讲完彪希，我不得不提海格底和夏伯特。我从来没有直接对海或夏报告，但海格底影响了我早、中期在德仪的「升」，而夏伯特影响了我中、后期的「升」和「降」。

海格底聪颖过人，而且非常有创新力。他工程出身，有工程学士学位。二次大战时他年三十左右，在美国海军部做采购。当时德仪的前身地质测验公司（GSI）是海军部的小供应商。采购与小供应商互相赏识。战后不久，德仪就聘海格底为总经理。几年后，美国电话电讯公司（AT&T，晶体管发明者）公开授权晶体管制造，许多大公司例如 RCA、奇异（GE）都去买「专利授权」以及「技术移转」；与他们比，德仪只是一个小虾米。但在关键时刻，海格底显示了远见和勇气——德仪不但勇敢地付出两万五千美元授权金，而且全力投入半导体的制造与后续研发。不到十年，德仪已在半导体领域击败当时与它一起获得授权的大公司。我第一个工作的希凡尼亚就是败将之一。德仪半导体霸主地位足足维持了三十多年——海格底是创始功臣。

<p class="en-para">Haggerty and Shepherd —</p>
<p class="en-para">Having discussed Shep, I must now mention Haggerty and Shepherd. I never reported directly to either of them, but Haggerty influenced my "rise" in the early and middle years at TI, while Shepherd influenced my "rise" and "fall" in the middle and later years.</p>
<p class="en-para">Haggerty was brilliant and immensely innovative. He was an engineering graduate with a bachelor's degree. During World War II, at about age thirty, he worked in procurement for the U.S. Navy. At that time, TI's predecessor — Geophysical Service Inc. (GSI) — was a small Navy supplier. The procurement officer and the small supplier recognized each other's worth. Shortly after the war, TI hired Haggerty as general manager. A few years later, AT&T (the inventor of the transistor) offered non-exclusive licenses for transistor manufacturing. Many large companies — RCA, GE, and others — rushed to buy patent licenses and technology transfers. Compared to them, TI was a small shrimp. But at this critical moment, Haggerty demonstrated vision and courage — TI boldly paid $25,000 in licensing fees and fully committed to semiconductor manufacturing and subsequent R&D. Within a decade, TI had defeated the larger companies that had received licenses alongside TI. My first employer, Sylvania, was one of the defeated. TI's dominance in semiconductors lasted more than thirty years. Haggerty was the founding hero.</p>
</div>

### Cheat Sheet · 商业语汇

1. **Small shrimp**
**中文解释**：小虾米，指小企业或市场新进入者；在商业竞争中，「小虾米」挑战「大鲸鱼」的故事是企业家精神的核心叙事。
**商业造句">In the semiconductor business, TI was a small shrimp in 1952, but within a decade, it had toppled the whales — a reminder that in technology, size is no guarantee of survival.*

2. **Founding hero**
**中文解释**：创始功臣，指在企业创建或关键转折点上做出决定性贡献的人；在组织叙事中，「创始功臣」的地位往往比现任 CEO 更具传奇色彩。
**商业造句">The founding hero was not the one who ran the company for twenty years, but the one who bet the company on semiconductors when it was still a seismic instruments maker.*

3. **Vision and courage**
**中文解释**：远见与勇气，指最高管理者在不确定性中做出正确决策的能力；在战略学中，远见是「看到别人看不到的东西」，勇气是「做别人不敢做的事」。
**商业造句">The $25,000 licensing fee required vision — the ability to see transistors not as a laboratory curiosity but as the future of electronics — and courage, because the board of a $20 million company did not typically make bets that could bankrupt it.*

### 修辞赏析

<span class="rhetoric-note">
<span class="zh">「德仪只是一个小虾米」——张忠谋用「小虾米」这个口语化的比喻，把AT&T、RCA、GE等巨头与德仪之间的实力对比写得栩栩如生。他随后用了「远见和勇气」两个词来形容海格底的决定——这是一种克制的赞美：他没有说海格底是天才，没有说是先知，而是说自己看到了一个普通品质（远见和勇气）如何创造了不普通的成就。这种「平凡品质、非凡结果」的叙事策略，使它的传记超越了「英雄神话」模式，给读者留下了更多思考空间。</span>
<span class="en">"TI was a small shrimp." — Chang uses the colloquial metaphor "small shrimp" to vividly render the power imbalance between AT&T, RCA, GE, and tiny TI. He then uses the words "vision and courage" to describe Haggerty's decision — a restrained praise: he does not call Haggerty a genius or a prophet, but shows how ordinary qualities (vision and courage) created extraordinary results. This "ordinary qualities, extraordinary outcomes" narrative strategy elevates his biography beyond the "hero myth" model and leaves the reader more to ponder.</span>
</span>

### 背景知识延伸

**海格底与成长委员会**：张忠谋主掌全球半导体集团后不久，海格底在总部成立了「成长委员会」，任张忠谋为主席，而彪希和夏伯特都是委员。张忠谋说：「老实说，在当时德仪体制及企业文化下，要我主持我的上司及我上司的上司都出席的会议，我并不舒服，但還是尽力而为，也算是报答海格底知遇之恩吧。」这段话再次展现了张忠谋感恩的一面——海格底当年不仅决定送他去史丹佛，还在多年后为他提供了一个在最高层发挥影响力的平台。
