# 德仪篇·第五章｜四个上司

我加入德仪几个月后立了功，海格底就认识我。在德仪的黄金时期，上下沟通即使隔好几个阶层也相当顺畅。好几个非正式场合，海格底与我有简短的谈话。事实上，简短的谈话往往可以发现对方对一个问题了解多深、对方思路多清晰。我自己做了总经理、董事长后，也常常以简短谈话对下属做初步的评估。

总之，几年后送我去史丹佛是海格底的決定。我想他原来的意思是要我走技术主管路线，但后来吕斯把我放在经营管理位置上，而且我做得很好，所以他也就不坚持了。

对我，海格底极其照顾。我主掌全球半导体集团后不久，海格底在总部成立了「成长委员会」，任我为主席，而彪希和夏伯特都是委员。老实说，在当时德仪体制及企业文化下，要我主持我的上司及我的上司的上司都出席的会议，我并不舒服，但還是尽力而为，也算是报答海格底知遇之恩吧。

海格底一生健康，但选择在六十三岁（一九七六年）退休。我在德仪的「黄金晨潮」也大约在此时结束。海格底的退休与我德仪黄金时期的结束，我想有点关系。海格底六十七岁（一九八〇年）突然患胰脏癌，两三个月后就过世了。纪念典礼中，达拉斯交响乐团演奏贝多芬第三交响曲第二乐章。纪念典礼后我去他家，他的女儿特地找我，告诉我：「爸爸一直非常重视你，你知道吗？」一九八〇年时，我在德仪的地位已无可挽救地下滑。对这位美丽女士这句话，虽然万千感慨，也只好留在心里。

<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">A few months after I joined TI and distinguished myself, Haggerty came to know me. During TI's golden era, communication between levels was remarkably smooth, even across several ranks. On several informal occasions, Haggerty and I had short conversations. In fact, brief conversations often reveal how deeply someone understands a problem and how clear their thinking is. After I became a GM and Chairman, I too often used short conversations to make initial assessments of my subordinates.</p>
<p class="en-para">In any case, the decision to send me to Stanford a few years later was Haggerty's. I believe his original intent was for me to pursue a technical leadership track, but after Lewis placed me in an operational management role and I performed well, he did not insist.</p>
<p class="en-para">Toward me, Haggerty was extremely caring. Not long after I took over as head of the Worldwide Semiconductor Group, Haggerty established a "Growth Committee" at headquarters, with me as its chairman and Shep and Shepherd both as members. Honestly, within TI's structure and corporate culture at the time, I was not comfortable chairing a meeting attended by my own boss and my boss's boss. But I did my best — partly as a way of repaying Haggerty's recognition of my worth.</p><p class="en-para">Haggerty enjoyed good health all his life but chose to retire at sixty-three (1976). My "golden morning" at TI also ended around this time. I believe Haggerty's retirement had something to do with the end of my golden era at TI. At sixty-seven (1980), Haggerty was suddenly diagnosed with pancreatic cancer and passed away within two or three months. At his memorial service, the Dallas Symphony Orchestra played the second movement of Beethoven's Third Symphony. After the service, I visited his home. His daughter sought me out specially and told me: "Dad always thought very highly of you, did you know that?" In 1980, my standing at TI had already begun its irreversible decline. Hearing those words from this beautiful young woman, though a thousand emotions surged within me, I could only keep them in my heart.</p>

### Cheat Sheet · 商业语汇

1. **Brief conversations as assessment**
**中文解释**：简短谈话评估法，指通过简短的、非正式的对话来评估一个人的理解深度和思路清晰度；这是一种高效的人才评估技巧，也是高层管理者最常用的「快筛」工具。
**商业造句">Fifteen minutes of conversation revealed more about the candidate's thinking than three hours of case interview — proof that brief conversations, conducted well, are the most underrated assessment tool.*

2. **Irreversible decline**
**中文解释**：无可挽救地下滑，指地位或处境已经恶化到无法逆转的地步；在职业叙事中，「下滑」往往比「失败」更难书写——因为它不是一次性的事件，而是一个缓慢的过程。
**商业造句">My influence at the company had entered an irreversible decline — not because of any single mistake, but because the center of gravity had quietly shifted to a younger generation that did not owe me its loyalty.*

3. **Recognition of my worth**
**中文解释**：知遇之恩，指上级对下属的赏识和提拔；在中国传统文化中，「知遇」是一种需要用忠诚和表现来回报的道德债务。
**商业造句">The chairman's recognition of my worth — I carried that debt for twenty years, repaying it not with flattery but with results.*

### 修辞赏析

<span class="rhetoric-note">
<span class="zh">「爸爸一直非常重视你，你知道吗？」——这是张忠谋自传中最为温柔的一句话。在海格底的追思仪式上，他的女儿对已经「无可挽救地下滑」的张忠谋说出了这句迟到多年的认可。张忠谋写道：「虽然万千感慨，也只好留在心里。」这种克制——把万千感慨留在心里而不是写在纸上——是他作为传记作者最特出的品质。他不煽情、不渲染、不利用读者的情感。他只是把一个珍贵的瞬间呈现给读者，然后让它自己发光。</span>
<span class="en">"Dad always thought very highly of you, did you know that?" — This is the gentlest line in Chang's autobiography. At Haggerty's memorial, his daughter spoke these words of overdue recognition to Chang, whose standing at TI was already in irreversible decline. Chang writes: "Though a thousand emotions surged within me, I could only keep them in my heart." This restraint — keeping the surge of emotion in the heart rather than on the page — is what most distinguishes Chang as a biographical writer. He does not sentimentalize, does not dramatize, does not exploit the reader's emotions. He simply presents a precious moment and lets it shine on its own.</span>
</span>
