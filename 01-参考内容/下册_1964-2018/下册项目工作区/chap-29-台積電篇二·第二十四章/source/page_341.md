## 一九九九年九二一大地震迅速恢复

一九九九年九月二十一日凌晨一时余，我在台北寓所睡梦中被地震惊醒，直觉认为楼下较安全，狼狈下楼，在楼梯中间遇到第二震，比第一震更厉害。拼命抓扶手时，听见楼上楼下啪啦啪啦东西掉在地板上的声音，真的以为这是末日了。但是过了一会，倒又不震了——这还不是末日。

二十一日上午我得知凌晨的地震强度达芮氏规模7.3，是台湾百年来最强地震。我也接到初步报告：（1）台积电和世界先进两家公司的人员都安全；（2）地震後两家公司的每个工厂都有许多该厂员工立刻回工厂看他们能做什么。听到这点，我非常感动。曾经在德仪长达二十五年的我，觉得德仪的工程师和经理人员，在同样情形下，不会这么热心；（3）全部工厂和办公室都断电，所以在停顿状态。

二十二日我到新竹，又有新的坏消息：美国纳斯达克（NASDAQ）股价大跌，原因是害怕台湾地震会使美国科技公司的半导体供应断链。此後几天我与同仁在没有电力、当然也没有冷气的闷热会议室商量对策。第一要解决的问题是恢复电力。

谢天谢地，二十四日我们获得近六成需求的稳定电力，二十七日五成生产设备恢复正常运作并逐步出货，三十日九成以上产能恢复正常。纳斯达克股市也很快涨回。

九二一芮氏规模7.3的大地震，起先引起客户的大恐慌。但是，我们在两星期内几乎完全复原，客户对我们的信任增加不少。政府的高效率恢复供电固然是重要因素，但同样重要的是全体同仁对危机的反应：各就其位，不辞辛劳，镇定应付。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">At around 1 a.m. on September 21, 1999, I was jolted awake by an earthquake in my Taipei residence. My instinct told me the ground floor was safer. I hurried downstairs in disarray, and midway down the stairs encountered the second aftershock—even stronger than the first. Clutching the railing for dear life, I heard things crashing to the floor from upstairs and downstairs—I truly thought it was doomsday. But after a while, the shaking stopped—it wasn't doomsday after all.</p>
<p class="en-para">On the morning of the 21st I learned the overnight earthquake measured 7.3 on the Richter scale—the strongest in Taiwan in a hundred years. I also received initial reports: (1) All employees of TSMC and Vanguard were safe. (2) After the earthquake, many employees at each fab of both companies immediately returned their fabs to see what they could do. Hearing this, I was deeply moved. Having spent 25 years at TI, I felt TI's engineers and managers wouldn't have been so enthusiastic under the same circumstances. (3) All factories and offices had lost power, so operations were at a standstill.</p>
<p class="en-para">On the 22nd I went to Hsinchu, where there was new bad news: U.S. NASDAQ stock prices had fallen sharply, for fear that the Taiwan earthquake would disrupt semiconductor supply to American Tech companies. Over the following days, my colleagues and I met in meeting rooms without electricity—and of course without air conditioning—to discuss countermeasures. The first priority was restoring power.</p>
<p class="en-para">Thank God, on the 24th we obtained stable power meeting nearly 60% of demand; on the 27th, 50% of production equipment resumed normal operation and we gradually began shipping; by the 30th, over 90% of capacity was back to normal. NASDAQ stock prices also bounced back quickly.</p>
<p class="en-para">The 921 earthquake, magnitude 7.3 on the Richter scale, initially caused great panic among customers. But we recovered almost completely within two weeks, and customer trust in us increased considerably. The government's efficient power restoration was certainly an important factor, but equally important was all colleagues' response to the crisis: everyone in their positions, tirelessly and calmly handling the situation.</p>

## 客户"实质上的"晶圆厂

我在德仪的经验告诉自己：自己拥有晶圆厂的IDM公司，其设计部门与晶圆制造部门因互不隶属，常常各玩"公司政治"，争风吃醋，鲜少合作。倒是台积电，作为纯粹晶圆制造公司，可以与IDM客户的设计部门合作无间。当然，无晶圆厂的设计公司更是我们真诚合作的客户暨夥伴。

在九零年代中期，我们对所有客户进一步宣示要成为大家"实质上的"晶圆厂（Virtual Fab）。既然是"实质上的"晶圆厂，那么与客户，尤其客户的设计部门应该沟通无碍。就在九零年代中后期，我对重要客户以及公司内部提倡与客户"多层面"沟通。所谓"多层面"沟通，就是除了我及行销部门外，我亦鼓励客户的设计部门与台积电为他们晶圆的部门沟通。

虽然我们自九零年代中期就有一百家以上客户，二零零零年後更有数百家客户，但在每一时期，最大的十五至二十家客户总是占台积电营收80%。我在九七年自兼总经理後，就把熟悉这十五至二十家大客户的CEO当作是我的重要责任。他们几乎都在美国与台湾，大部分密集在加州。我每年访问每位CEO至少一、两次，他们也常来台湾访问他们"实质上的"晶圆厂。尽管如，我自一九九七年自兼台积电总经理至二零一八年退休的二十年中，虽常与这些大客户的CEO会面，但与"老朋友"叙旧的场面却不多见。因不只大客户常常变，大客户的CEO也常变。我自定的与他们"熟悉"的标准是：假设有事，双方随时打电话给对方，而对方一定会接。

至于"多层面"沟通，大部分在有问题时发生，但有时也在制程研发阶段发生。总之，客户工程师与我们工程师的直接沟通，省了许多第三者代言的误会，也增强不少互信。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">My experience at TI told me: IDM companies that own their own fabs often have their design and wafer manufacturing departments playing "company politics" and jealous of each other because the departments belong to different chains of command—they rarely cooperate. TSMC, as a pure wafer manufacturing company, could cooperate seamlessly with IDM customers' design departments. Of course, fabless design companies were even more so our genuinely cooperative customers and partners.</p>
<p class="en-para">In the mid-'90s, we further declared to all customers that we wanted to be their "virtual fab." Since we were a "virtual fab," we should be able to communicate with customers, especially their design departments, without barriers. Around the mid-to-late '90s, I began advocating "multi-level" communication with key customers and internally.</p>
<p class="en-para">Although we had over a hundred customers by the mid-'90s and several hundred after 2000, in each period the top 15-20 customers always accounted for 80% of TSMC revenue. After I took over as my own general manager in 1997, I made it an important responsibility to become familiar with the CEOs of these 15-20 major customers. They were almost all in the U.S. and Taiwan, mostly concentrated in California. I visited each CEO at least once or twice a year, and they also often visited Taiwan to see their "virtual fab." Nevertheless, during the twenty years from 1997, when I was TSMC general manager, until my retirement in 2018, although I often met with these major customers' CEOs, opportunities to "catch up with old friends" were rare. Because not only did major customers change often, but major customers' CEOs also changed frequently. My own standard for being "familiar" with them was: if something came up, both parties could call the other at any time, and the other party would definitely answer.</p>
<p class="en-para">Regarding "multi-level" communication, it mostly occurred when there were manufacturing problems, but sometimes also during process R&amp;D stages. In any case, direct communication between customer engineers and our engineers saved many misunderstandings caused by third-party intermediaries and built considerable mutual trust.</p>
</div>
