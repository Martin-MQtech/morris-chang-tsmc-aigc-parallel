## WaferTech的教训

一九九零到一九九五年当中，台积电营收的平均年成长率超过67%。一九九五年营收接近十一亿美元，客户唯一的抱怨是我们产能不足。我和总经理布鲁克自信心最高之时，感觉即看我们台湾厂48%的营业净利率，只要给我们的厂的获利率应该不会太差。恰巧九五年时，新竹园区已没有土地可给我们，台南科学园区又还没有成立。我与布鲁克就在这阴错阳差之际，决定与Altera、亚德诺半导体（Analog Devices, Inc., ADI）、ISSI（矽成）这三个客户合资在美国华盛顿州卡马斯（Camas）建设一座八吋晶圆厂，并命名为WaferTech。

我们雇了史密斯（Kenneth G. Smith）为总经理。他以他的资历，应该可以胜任这个新厂总经理职。同时，我们在卡马斯买了四倍立即需要的土地，以备以后扩充，随后于九六年七月开始动土兴建八吋晶圆厂。

雇了总经理，动土后的五年间WaferTech连续不断地给我"惊奇"。这惊奇分两类：

第一类是"成本"的惊奇。一言以蔽之："什么都比原先估计的贵。"在台积电九五年已有很专业的成本估计单位，但是这个单位的专长是估算台湾的成本，对美国成本估算外行。在九六至二零零零年五年中，工厂及洁净室建筑等远超过预算，接着是制造设备、薪酬、甚至办公室用具等等，都超出原来预算。

第二类更令我沮丧的是"人事"的惊奇。WaferTech是我和布鲁克的共同决定，我们征询新竹的营运团队，所以在我们宣布要在美国设厂时，新竹的营运团队就有反弹。我更大的惊奇来自美国方面。雇史密斯不久後，我就发现他不太尊重台积电的制造技术，也不太尊重台积电的既有成就。至于台积电的经理人，除了布鲁克和我外，他一个都不服。

<p class="en-para">From 1990 to 1995, TSMC's average annual revenue growth exceeded 67%. In 1995, revenue approached $1.1 billion, and customers' only complaint was our insufficient capacity. At the peak of confidence for me and General Manager Brook, looking at our Taiwan fabs' 48% operating margin, we assumed a U.S. fab's profitability wouldn't be too bad. As it happened, by 1995 there was no more land available in Hsinchu Science Park, and Tainan Science Park had not yet been established. Brook and I, by this twist of fate, decided to form a joint venture with three customers—Altera, Analog Devices, ISSI—to build an 8-inch fab in Camas, Washington, U.S.A., named WaferTech.</p>
<p class="en-para">We hired Kenneth G. Smith as general manager, qualified by the role. At the same time, we purchased four times the immediately needed land in Camas for future expansion, then broke ground in July 1996 to build the 8-inch fab.</p>
<p class="en-para">After hiring the general manager and breaking ground, WaferTech continuously gave me "surprises" over the next five years. These surprises fell into two categories:</p>
<p class="en-para">The first category was "cost" surprises. In short: "Everything was much more expensive than originally estimated." Although TSMC already had a highly specialized cost estimation unit in 1995, their expertise was estimating Taiwan costs—they were laymen at U.S. cost estimation. During the five years 1996-2000, first factory and cleanroom construction far exceeded budget, then manufacturing equipment, salaries, even office supplies—everything exceeded the original budget.</p>
<p class="en-para">The second category, which frustrated me even more, was "people" surprises. WaferTech was a joint decision by Brook and me; we hadn't consulted the Hsinchu operations team. So when we announced building a fab in the U.S., the Hsinchu operations team had significant pushback. My bigger surprise came from the American side. Not long after hiring Smith, I discovered he didn't much respect TSMC's manufacturing technology, nor TSMC's existing accomplishments. As for TSMC managers, heBrook and I, he was unconvinced by any of them.</p>

## 收拾残局

WaferTech自九八年开始量产後，亏损加剧，又与新和总部不和。到了二零零零年，已成为台积电的烂摊子，需要行动了。

我的第一步行动是买回合资伙伴的股份，而且让他们稍赚一点。在买回时，合资夥伴已因为WaferTech的亏损正在担心他们的投资。我让他们在获利条件下出脱这投资，他们都很高兴。本来以合资方式成立WaferTech的目的，也就是要巩固我们与客户的关系。现在WaferTech虽没有成功，客户关系还是巩固了。

第二步是解雇史密斯。新竹和WaferTech不能合作，人事不和是主要原因。第三步是派台积电营运资深副总左大川出任WaferTech总经理，并直接对台积电总经理曾繁城负责。此后数年，新竹派了不少工程师和经理人员到WaferTech协助。

左大川在WaferTech待了几乎四年，WaferTech营运渐趋稳定。"稳定"的意义是：在同样的产品、同样的售价上，WaferTech的毛利率大约比台湾厂的毛利率低20至25个百分点。通常台湾厂的毛利率是45%，WaferTech的毛利率是20%至25%，但是WaferTech的营业净利还是正的。既然还有正的营业净利，我们决定把它维持下去，但是我们不扩充了。直至今日，WaferTech仍是台积电的一部分。

## SSMC新加坡合资

九七中期，飞利浦忽然对我要求合资在新加坡建SSMC。这是飞利浦内部已规画有些时日的方案，飞利浦已得到新加坡政府经济发展局（EDB）同意，亦为合资夥伴。根据飞利浦的建议，最后的结局会是台积电出资39%，飞利浦61%。总经理由飞利浦指派，营运副总由台积电指派。八吋技术由台积电供应。

我听了这建议立刻的反应是：飞利浦又想讨我们便宜。我婉拒了。但是飞利浦名在此之後两次台积电董人代表重复提此建议，对我施加高度压力。

从"政治"层面看，飞利浦在九七年仍拥有台积电28.2%股权，占十席董事的四席；如果因为他们真的因为这件事而与我作对，倒也是一件麻烦事。从正面看，我很欣赏新加坡的生产力，接受飞利浦的建议可以当作台积电在费最小力气下，增加一个小工厂。至于台积电对SSMC的技术移转？八技术发展已近尾声。

在考虑各种得失後，我决定接受飞利浦的SSMC合资建议。SSMC在九九年动土，二零零零年开始量产。後来成绩颇佳。

<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">After WaferTech started Volume production in 1998, losses intensified and discord with Hsinchu headquarters continued. By 2000, WaferTech had become TSMC's mess that needed action.</p>
<p class="en-para">My first move was to buy out the joint venture partners' shares, letting them earn a small profit. At the time of buyout, the joint venture partners were already worried about their investment due to WaferTech's losses. I let them exit the investment under profitable conditions, and they were all happy. The original purpose of forming WaferTech as a joint venture was precisely to strengthen relationships with customers. Now although WaferTech hadn't succeeded, customer relationships were strengthened.</p>
<p class="en-para">The second move was firing Smith. The inability of Hsinchu and WaferTech to cooperate—personnel discord was the main reason. The third move was dispatching TSMC Senior VP of Operations Zuo Dachuan as WaferTech general manager, reporting directly to TSMC General Manager Zeng Fan-cheng. In the following years, Hsinchu sent many engineers and managers to assist WaferTech.</p>
<p class="en-para">Zuo Dachuan stayed at WaferTech for almost four years, and WaferTech's operations gradually stabilized. "Stable" meant: with the same products and the same selling prices, WaferTech's gross margin was about 20-25 percentage points lower than Taiwan fabs'. Typically Taiwan fabs had gross margins of 45%, while WaferTech's was 20-25%, but WaferTech's net operating profit was still positive. Since there was still positive net operating profit, we decided to keep it running, but we wouldn't expand it. To this day, WaferTech remains part of TSMC.</p>
<p class="en-para">### SSMC Singapore Joint Venture</p>
<p class="en-para">In mid-1997, Philips suddenly asked me to form a joint venture to build SSMC in Singapore. This was a plan Philips had already been developing for some time. Philips had obtained the agreement of Singapore's Economic Development Board (EDB) to also be a joint venture partner. Per Philips' proposal, the final arrangement would be TSMC contributing 39%, Philips 61%. The general manager would be assigned by Philips; the COO by TSMC. 8-inch technology would be supplied by TSMC.</p>
<p class="en-para">My immediate reaction to this suggestion: Philips was trying to take advantage of us again. I declined. But Philips pursued it tenaciously; twice after that, Chairman of the TSMC board repeated this proposal, putting high pressure on me.</p>
<p class="en-para">From a "politics" perspective, Philips still owned 28.2% of TSMC in 1997 and held four of the ten board seats; if they really opposed me over this matter, it would be troublesome. On the positive side, I had long admired Singapore's productivity. Accepting Philips' proposal could be viewed as adding a small factory with minimal effort. As for TSMC's technology transfer to SSMC? 8-inch technology development was nearing its end.</p>
<p class="en-para">After weighing the pros and cons, I decided to accept Philips' SSMC joint venture proposal. SSMC broke ground in 1999 and began Volume production in 2000. It performed quite well thereafter.</p>
