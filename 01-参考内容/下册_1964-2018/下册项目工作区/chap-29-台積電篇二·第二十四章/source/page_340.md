## 购并德碁及世大

一九九八年曾繁城刚自世界先进回台积电任总经理不久，公司事务又因为严重的"供不应求"而繁忙起来。九九年初的某一晚，繁城忽然说他要早点走，去"看看"德碁的工厂。如上章所述，德碁那时已因为德仪决定退出记忆体，而丧失了技术来源；在实际营运上，也已在严重亏损状态中。

在这晚繁城突然告诉我他要"看看"德碁前，我和他早已谈过购并德碁的可能，但德碁是一记忆体公司，把记忆体产能转成晶圆代工产能，要添加不少设备，费时又费钱，也不能立刻解决台积的产能问题，所以我们搁置了购并德碁之议。现在，客户的压力不断增加，繁城认为必须重新检讨购并德碁。

在这卖方不但想卖、甚至必须卖的情形下，只要买方肯出合理的价钱，谈判可以很快顺利结束。九十九年六月台积电与德碁签约：台积电先买德碁30%股权，并开始经营德碁为晶圆代工公司；如没有"惊奇"，台积在一年后购并全部德碁股权。签约後，我们立刻派陈健邦为德碁总经理。

我们与德碁的签约启动了另一家公司也要卖给我们的要求。那另一家公司是世大（世大积体电路股份有限公司）。它成立于一九九六年。创办人张汐京从前在建厂工作，并无经营半导体事业经验，但看到台积电的成功，也想成立一家晶圆代工公司。他找到了中华开发银行及其他几家台湾公司成为世大的大股东。

九九年底，正是我们最缺产能的时期，中华开发总经理胡定吾（亦为世大副董事长）抓对了时机，又制造了一个若有若无的竞对者（联对我们的开出高价。

当时我无意与胡定吾纠缠。我们的大方向都是对的；我们正全速驰向世界半导体业中自五零年代德仪、七零年代英特尔、八零年代三星以来最大的成功；我们不能因为要在所有细节上都不吃亏而降低我们的动能。

台积电在二零零零年初宣布购并世大案。当时飞利浦仍是拥有28.2%股权的台积电最大股东。我在与世大谈判时并未征询飞利浦意见，但在宣布当天打电话荷兰飞利浦总部做口头报告。飞利浦对我们要付的价钱表示高度震惊。我挂电话後想飞利浦的情绪要及早安抚，不然他们可能在董事会杯葛。遂在次日立刻派遣台积电总经理曾繁城、会计长何丽梅前去荷兰解释为甚件购并案是我们在赢取客户信任策略中必须做的事。此行颇成功。

世大购并案又掀起的波澜。我本来以为已结案的德碁购并案，现在因为我们宣布购并世大的价格高于原来议定的德碁价格，德碁股东聚会集体抗议，并要求德碁董事长施振荣重议购并价格。为了抚平他们，我们只得又调整德碁的购并价。

二零零零年七月，德碁和世大购并案正式生效。德碁和世大的招牌从此消失。德碁的晶圆厂变成台积电的七厂；世大的晶圆厂变成台积电的八厂。

<p class="en-para">In 1998, not long after Zeng Fan-cheng returned from Vanguard International to serve as TSMC general manager, the company was again swamped with business due to severe "demand exceeding supply." Now, with mounting customer pressure, Fan-cheng believed we must revisit the Tepro acquisition.</p>
<p class="en-para">When the seller not only wants to sell but must sell, negotiations can conclude quickly and smoothly as long as the buyer offers a reasonable price. In June 1999, TSMC signed an agreement with Tepro: TSMC first purchased 30% of Tepro's equity and began operating Tepro as a foundry company. If there were no "surprises," TSMC would acquire all Tepro equity a year later. After signing, we immediately assigned Chen Jianbang as Tepro's general manager.</p>
<p class="en-para">Our signing with Tepro triggered another company wanting to sell to us as well. That other company was WSMC (WaferTech Semiconductor Corporation, actually 世大积体电路). It was established in 1996. Its founder Zhang Xijing had previously been in charge of fab construction at TI and had no experience running a semiconductor business, but seeing TSMC's success, also wanted to start a foundry company. He enlisted China Development Bank and several other Taiwanese companies as WSMC's major shareholders.</p>
<p class="en-para">At year-end 1999, when we were most short of capacity, China Development General Manager Hu Dingwu (also WSMC's vice chairman) seized the right moment and, by manufacturing a potential competitor (UMC), set a high price for us.</p>
<p class="en-para">At the time, I had no intention of tangling with Hu Dingwu. Our general direction was right; we were at full throttle toward the biggest success in the global semiconductor world since TI in the '50s, Intel in the '70s, and Samsung in the '80s. We couldn't sacrifice our momentum just to avoid losing on every detail.</p>
<p class="en-para">In early 2000, TSMC announced the WSMC acquisition. At that time, Philips was still TSMC's largest shareholder with 28.2% equity. I hadn't consulted Philips during WSMC negotiations, but on the day of the announcement, I called Philips Netherlands headquarters to give a verbal report. Philips expressed high shock at the price we were going to pay. After hanging up, I thought Philips' emotions needed to be calmed soon, otherwise they might filibuster in the board meeting. The next day, I immediately dispatched TSMC General Manager Zeng Fan-cheng and CFO He Limei to the Netherlands to explain why this acquisition was a must-do in our strategy of winning customer trust. <p class="en-para">The WSMC acquisition stirred up another ripple. Tepro shareholders gathered to collectively protest and demanded that Tepro Chairman Shi Zhengrong renegotiate the acquisition price. To appease them, we had to adjust Tepro's acquisition price upward.</p>
<p class="en-para">In July 2000, the Tepro and WSMC acquisitions formally took effect. The Tepro and WSMC brands disappeared from then on. Tepro's fabs became TSMC's Fab 7; WSMC's fabs became TSMC's Fab 8.</p>

## 麦肯锡顾问公司的发现

一九九六年，总经理布鲁克与我有一个关于公司组织的争执。台积电自成立后，一直都是"功能式"组织，即：营运（制造）部、研发部、市场行销部……等。布鲁克认为应采取德仪形式"业务单位"组织。我则相当坚决认为"业务单位"组织不适用于台积电。布鲁克不服，要找国际级顾问公司的意见。

麦肯锡花了两、三个月时间，先在内部了解我们的业务後，再与我们的十几个主要客户谈。结论是完全同意我的看法："业务单位"组织不适于台积电，还是继续"功能式"组织较好。

麦肯锡的结论解决了我和布鲁克在组织上的争执，但正如一般国际级顾问公司的做法：回答你给他的问题後，他会发现一个新问题，让他再为你效劳。这次，麦肯锡说："在我们与你们的客户讨论你们组织问题时，我们发现你们客户与你们的关系不是很好。"

这样的说辞，当然引起我的高度注意。十几年来，争取客户信任一直是我们主要目标之一，怎会关系不好？麦肯锡说他无法做详细的回答，但推荐另一家专做客户满意度调查的顾问公司。

自一九九七年起，我们就雇这家顾问公司，每年为我们做客户满意度调查。每年调查报告总是厚厚的一本，包括了所有我们为客户服务的项目：产能足够否？反应度够快否？价格合理否？交货如期否？……等等。作为第三者，这家顾问公司较我们自己更会听到客户的真话。每年我都亲自聆听顾问公司的报告，并主持报告後的检讨会议。每年总有许许多多大小的改进项目，我与高阶主管指定需要改进的单位。

这套制度——每年专业性找出客户不满意处、认明需要采取改进行动的单位、追踪改进——的确增强客户对我们的信任。自一九九七年起，我们年年都做，到我退休，已做了二十一年矣。

<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">In 1996, General Manager Brook and I had a dispute about company organization. Since its founding, TSMC had always used a "functional" organization: Operations (Manufacturing) Department, R&amp;D Department, Marketing Department, etc. Brook believed we should adopt TI's "business unit" organization. I was quite adamant that "business unit" organization was not suitable for TSMC. Brook disagreed and wanted the opinion of an international consulting firm.</p>
<p class="en-para">McKinsey spent two to three months first understanding our business internally, then talking to a dozen or so of our major customers. The conclusion completely agreed with my view: "business unit" organization was not suitable for TSMC; continuing with the "functional" organization was better.</p>
<p class="en-para">McKinsey's conclusion resolved the organizational dispute between Brook and me. But as is common among international consulting firms: after answering the question you gave them, they discover a new problem to be of further service. This time, McKinsey said: "While discussing your organizational issues with your customers, we found that your customer relationships were not that great."</p>
<p class="en-para">Such a statement naturally drew my high attention. For over a decade, winning customer trust had been one of our primary objectives—how could the relationships be not good? McKinsey said they couldn't provide a detailed answer but recommended another consulting firm specializing in customer satisfaction surveys.</p>
<p class="en-para">From 1997 onward, we hired this firm to conduct annual customer satisfaction surveys for us. Each annual report was a thick volume covering all aspects of our customer service: Is capacity sufficient? Is responsiveness fast enough? Are prices reasonable? Is delivery on time? . . . and more. As a third party, this consulting firm could hear customers' true feelings better than we could ourselves. Every year I personally listened to the consulting firm's report and chaired the post-report review meeting. Every year there were numerous improvement projects of various sizes, and I and senior management assigned the units needing improvement.</p>
<p class="en-para">This entire system—professionally identifying customer dissatisfaction annually, designating units to take improvement action, tracking improvements—indeed strengthened customer trust in us. We did this every year from 1997 through my retirement, for 21 years in total.</p>
