## IC设计公司（无晶圆厂）先驱

在通用最后几个月的凄风苦雨中，我接触了两位「IC设计公司」创业者，并且为通用做成了第一笔「晶圆代工」生意。这段经验值得在此一提。

一九八四年十一月，戈登·坎普（Gordon Campbell）来访。我们不熟，但互相知道对方是谁。他找我的目的是要通用投资他现在想办的公司——「要五千万美元投资。」我问：「你有营运计画吗？」他说：「没有。」但又指指他的脑袋说：「都在这里。」我告诉他通用应该有兴趣投资，但所有投资案必须经过董事会，我要有一份正式营运计画才能提到董事会去。坎普说：「没问题，我回去後就写，两星期内寄给你。」

两星期过去了，三星期过去了，没有收到计画。我就打电话给坎普。坎普回答：「啊，对不起，英理士，我不需要你投资了。」

「为什么？」

「因为我不需要五千万，只要五百万就成；五百万我随便凑凑就凑得起来。」

「为什么只要五百万？」

「因为我不建晶圆厂了，把晶圆制造包给别人。」

对此，这个投资案就这样无疾而终。後来坎普创办了Chips and Technologies，果然是最无晶圆厂的IC设计公司，相当成功，後来也是台积电的客户。

坎普访问後几星期，一位素昧平生的创业者——经由通用半导体总经理的介绍——也来看我，他名叫乔治·布雷果思（George Perlegos），已创办了Atmel，也是无晶圆厂的IC设计公司，现在要通用替他做晶圆代工。那时我们的晶圆厂正缺订单，所以我立刻指示半导体总经理与乔治谈条件。此後两个月，我常常听到总经理的埋怨：「乔治在我们工厂里指指点点，侮辱我们的工程师」、「乔治要改变我们的制程」、「这是我们的厂，好像乔治认为是他的」。虽然很多埋怨，但案子仍在进行，而且最後，我听说要开始「试片」了。一直到我辞职前六天，总经理很紧张地打电话给我：「乔治大喊大骂，情绪失去控制，说我们建议的合同太苛刻。」次日，我亲自电话乔治，与他谈判。在那天的日记上，我记下：「我稍作让步」。但是五天后我就离开通用。後来听说这桩「晶圆代工」案子继续下去，而且通用和Atmel双方都还算满意。

<p class="en-para"><b>Pioneers of IC Design (Fabless) Companies</b></p>
<p class="en-para">During the windy, rain-drenched final months at GI, I met two "IC design company" entrepreneurs and closed GI's first "foundry" deal. This experience merits recounting.</p>
<p class="en-para">In November 1984, Gordon Campbell visited. We didn't know each other well but knew of each other. His purpose in seeing me was to ask GI to invest in the company he was starting: "I need $50 million in investment." I asked: "Do you have a business plan?" He said: "No." But pointing to his head: "It's all here here." I told him GI should be interested, but all investments required board approval, and I would need a formal business plan before taking it to the board. Campbell said: "No problem, I'll write it and send it within two weeks."</p>
<p class="en-para">Two weeks passed, three weeks passed — no plan received. I called Campbell. He replied: "Oh, sorry, Morris, I don't need your investment anymore." "Why?" "Because I don't need $50 million — $5 million will do. I can scrape together $5 million easily." "Why only $5 million?" "Because I'm not building a fab. I'm outsourcing wafer manufacturing to others."</p>
<p class="en-para">With that, the investment deal came to nothing. Campbell went on to found Chips and Technologies, indeed one of the earliest fabless IC design companies, quite successful, and later a TSMC customer.</p>
<p class="en-para">A few weeks after Campbell's visit, a stranger — introduced by the GI semiconductor GM — also came to see me. His name was George Perlegos. He had founded Atmel, also a fabless IC design company, and now he wanted GI to do wafer foundry work for him. Our fab was short on orders at the time, so I immediately directed the semiconductor GM to negotiate terms with George. Over the next two months, I frequently heard the GM's complaints: "George points and pokes around our fab, insulting our engineers." "George wants to change our process." "It's our fab, but George acts like it's his." Despite many complaints, the deal progressed, and eventually, I heard they were about to begin "test runs." Until six days before my resignation, the GM nervously called me: "George is yelling and cursing, emotionally out of control, saying our proposed contract was too harsh." The next day, I personally called George to negotiate. In my diary that day, I wrote: "Made minor concessions." But five days later I left GI. I heard later that the foundry deal went forward, and both GI and Atmel were reasonably satisfied.</p>

### Cheat Sheet · 商业语汇

1. **Fabless IC design company (无晶圆厂IC设计公司)**
**中文解释**：指只从事IC设计而不自建晶圆厂的公司；「无晶圆厂」（fabless）模式是半导体产业专业化分工的起点——它需要一个可信赖的「纯代工」夥伴，而这正是台积电後来扮演的角色。
**商业造句">The fabless IC design model — designing chips without owning a fab — was the seed of semiconductor industry specialization that would later make TSMC's pure-play foundry model essential.*

2. **Foundry as contract manufacturing (晶圆代工作为契约制造)**
**中文解释**：指代工厂为其他公司的设计制造晶圆；「晶圆代工」的商业模式在台积电之前并不成熟——IDM（整合组件制造商）通常只为自己制造，而「纯代工」的概念在八〇年代中期还是一个崭新的、未被验证的商业模式。
**商业造句">The foundry concept — manufacturing wafers for other companies' designs — was an unproven business model in the mid-1980s that would later become Chang's foundational insight for TSMC.*
