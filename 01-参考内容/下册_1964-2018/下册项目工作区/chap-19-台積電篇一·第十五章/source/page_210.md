## 改革三：每年考绩最低三%员工留职察看

我到工研院时，工研院已每年打员工考绩，但考绩的作用不大，仅是少许加薪的差别。不曾有员工因考绩差而被解雇，甚至连正式警告的机制都没有。

考绩制度目的是借「赏罚分明」，而激励成效好的人员，并且警告成效不好的人员。在美国企业内，赏罚幅度可以相当大。举大公司GE为例，威尔许（Jack Welch）在一九八一年任GE总裁后，规定GE每年解雇考绩最差的一〇%。威尔许一直以此政策为傲，是他退休时最成功政策之一。再举德仪为例，每年新年假后的第一个星期，「经营委员会」花整整一个星期的时间，排列公司考绩最佳的前一百名，以及检讨如何处理考绩最差者。

一九八五年工研院的考绩制度，只拷贝了美国企业考绩制度的皮毛，并没有达到借「赏罚分明」而激励或警告同仁的目的。

当然我也知道工研院是一个准政府机构，「赏」的幅度不大（只是多加一点薪，没有分红，更没有股票承购权之类的「奖励」），所以「罚」的幅度也不宜太大。但对考绩最差的少数员工，应该给他们一个正式警告——留职察看半年，不加薪，但也不扣薪，看半年后再说。

有些员工其实有能力可以做好工作，但因为不够努力，所以对这类员工，半年的「留职察看」正可鼓励他们努力一点。有些因为被放在错的位置上，没有能力做这位置的工作，所以对这类员工，半年「留职察看」也可以让上层主管或人力资源单位调动他们到比较适宜的职位。

我并不想解雇任何员工，我只想整顿一下最劣考绩的员工——激励他们努力一点，或转调他们到比较适合他们能力的职位，因而提高工研院的一般表现。

一九八六年，我与徐贤修董事长、副院长、所长、协理们讨论了我的想法后，决定列考绩最低的三%为「留职察看」。徐董事长非常赞成此举，副院长及所长们有几个赞成、有几个有保留。我起初想列五%为「留职察看」，因为有几位所长认为五%太高，所以改为三%。

我没有想到，虽然我们只列三%员工为「留职察看」，但至少三%的员工感到「唇亡齿寒」？还是本来安逸地寄居在准政府机构的工作保障下，现在发现这个外国来的新院长居然威胁起他们的安全感来？总之，黑函满天飞。工研院董事、经济部，甚至立法委员、监察委员都收到黑函。

我的「改革三」，只做了一年，第二年就做不下去了。

<p class="en-para"><b>Reform 3: Bottom 3% on Probation Annually</b></p>
<p class="en-para">When I arrived at ITRI, annual performance reviews were already in practice, but they had little effect — merely modest differences in salary increases. No employee had ever been fired for poor performance. There wasn't even a formal warning mechanism.</p>
<p class="en-para">The purpose of a performance review system is to "reward the good and punish the bad" — to motivate high performers and warn underperformers. In American companies, the reward-and-punishment range can be quite large. Take GE as an example: after Jack Welch became CEO in 1981, he mandated that GE fire the bottom 10% of performers annually. Welch was always proud of this policy, citing it as one of his most successful initiatives at retirement. Or take TI: every year in the first week after New Year's, the Operations Committee spent a full week ranking the company's top 100 performers and discussing how to handle the worst performers.</p>
<p class="en-para">ITRI's 1985 performance review system had copied only the veneer of American corporate practices without achieving the motivational or warning purpose of "rewarding the good and punishing the bad."</p>
<p class="en-para">Of course I understood that as a quasi-government agency, ITRI's "reward" range was small (just slightly higher pay, no bonuses, no stock options), so the "punishment" range should also be modest. But for the small number of worst performers, they should receive a formal warning — six-month probation with no salary increase but no cut, to be reviewed again after six months.</p>
<p class="en-para">Some employees had the ability to do good work but lacked effort; for them, six months of probation could motivate them to try harder. Some were in the wrong role, lacking the capability for their position; for them, probation could allow management or HR to reassign them to a more suitable position.</p>
<p class="en-para">I had no desire to fire anyone. I simply wanted to address the worst performers — either motivate them to improve or reassign them to roles that better matched their abilities, thereby lifting ITRI's overall performance.</p>
<p class="en-para">In 1986, after discussing my ideas with Chairman Hsu, the Vice President, institute directors, and associate directors, we decided to place the bottom 3% on probation. Chairman Hsu strongly endorsed this; among the Vice President and directors, several supported it, several had reservations. I had initially wanted to set it at 5%, but since several directors felt 5% was too high, we settled on 3%.</p>
<p class="en-para">What I did not anticipate: although we only placed 3% on probation, at least that 3% felt the threat — or was it that those who had long enjoyed the security of a quasi-government job now found that this foreign-born new president was threatening their safety? In any case, anonymous letters flew everywhere. ITRI directors, the Ministry of Economic Affairs, and even legislators and Control Yuan members all received them.</p>
<p class="en-para">"Reform 3" lasted only one year; the second year it became impossible to continue.</p>

### Cheat Sheet · 商业语汇

1. **Bottom 10% firing (末位淘汰)**
**出自**：杰克·威尔许在GE推行的「活力曲线」——每年解雇考绩最差的10%；张忠谋试图以较温和的3%「留职察看」引入竞争机制。
**商业造句**：*Chang's adoption of a 3% probation policy — a milder version of Jack Welch's famous bottom-10% firing rule — was an attempt to introduce performance discipline into a risk-averse institutional culture.*

2. **Probation (留职察看)**
**中文解释**：对表现不佳员工给予一定期间的观察，期间不加薪但不扣薪，期满后再评估；这是介于「口头警告」和「解雇」之间的中间处分。
**商业造句**：*The probation mechanism was designed as a middle-ground penalty — formal enough to signal seriousness, but humane enough to give underperformers a chance to improve.*

3. **Anonymous letters (黑函)**
**中文解释**：匿名检举信，常被用于政治或组织内的斗争；张忠谋的改革三引发大量黑函攻击，是台湾公机构改革少见的社会现象。
**商业造句**：*The flood of anonymous letters against Chang's reform was a textbook case of how institutional resistance weaponizes bureaucratic channels to undermine change.*
