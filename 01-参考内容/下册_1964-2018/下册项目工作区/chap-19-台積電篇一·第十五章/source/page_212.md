## 胡定华的辞职与我的去职

雪上加霜的，是副院长胡定华的辞职。

自从我单枪匹马、自命为「改革者」到工研院那时刻，我就把胡定华当作我最亲近的同僚；那时我知道工研院不多，知道台湾更少，我认胡定华为向导。我的所有「改革」，都先与他讨论；尤其衍生台积电一事，胡和电子所所长史钦泰是我的得力助手。

但在一九八七年四月，胡定华忽然对我说他要交通大学当校长。我当下说：「工研院院长好」，我就说：「我不会做很久，如你留下，那我要走时，我一定推荐你为院长。」他很坦白地说：「『由副扶正』在台湾不太有，而且如果你离开，你也不见得有能力推荐我。」

在我成长的企业文化里，如果下属有好的出路要离开，我不会强留他，只会祝福他；因为如果真的把他强留下来，我会认为我欠了他一个债，而且是一个还不尽的债。他总是会想：如果去了那个职位，是否会比留着好。

所以在胡定华怀疑他做工研院院长的机会时，我也不再留他，反而鼓励他去交大当校长。但第二天他就告诉我，交大的事已经被人做了。后来阮大年来当交大校长，来看我，告诉我交大校长本来是胡定华，但俞国华院长认为我在工研院需要胡定华为辅佐，所以变成是他了。阮在告诉我时有点不高兴，好像他只是「第二选择」。所以俞院长的这个决定，得罪了三个人：阮，因为只是「第二选择」；胡，因为使他失去当交大校长的机会；我，因为我虽器重胡定华，但不认为那么需要他，而我的老阁却认为我必须要有他辅佐。

俞院长的苦心，还是没用；他能使胡定华不去交大，但不能使他留在工研院。半年后，胡就告诉我他要去H&Q台湾创投基金徐大麟旗下。这基金就是一九八五年我没有接受、花半时与徐大麟共同经营的基金。我不认为对胡定华，这是一个好机会；我认为这机会还比胡定华仍有可能得到工研院院长职位差。但他去意已坚。他在一九八八年二月正式辞职。

胡定华的辞职，锁定了我欠缺「人和」的名声。紧接着他的辞职，我的两个上司（工研院董事长徐贤修及经济部部长李达海）都告诉我：依院长认为我不善处理人事。不久后任陈履安任经济部部长，我立刻发现要见陈部长，远较以前要见李部长难。李达海部长与我有一个相当「随遇而安」的关系。因为我的「改革」需要他的支持，我常去见他，总只要一、两天前打电话与他秘书约就行。陈部长却需要好几天，甚至一、两星期前约，有时约了后还被他临时更改。有一次，我准时到经济部，在他办公外面的客厅等了半小时后，他匆匆出来，见我在等他，对我说：「啊，我要去楼开会，你跟我一起下去，在楼梯上跟我说话吧。」

<p class="en-para"><b>Hu Ding-hua's Resignation and My Departure</b></p>
<p class="en-para">To add frost to the snow, Vice President Hu Ding-hua resigned.</p>
<p class="en-para">From the moment I arrived at ITRI, a self-appointed "reformer" riding solo, I had treated Hu Ding-hua as my closest colleague. I knew little about ITRI and even less about Taiwan; I regarded Hu as my guide. I discussed all my reforms with him first. On the TSMC spin-off in particular, Hu and Electronics Director Shi Qin-tai were my key lieutenants.</p>
<p class="en-para">But in April 1987, Hu suddenly told me he was leaving to become President of Chiao Tung University. I said: "The ITRI presidency is better." I told him: "I won't be here long. If you stay, when I leave I'll recommend you as president." He replied candidly: "Promotion from deputy to president is rare in Taiwan, and even if you leave, you might not have the influence to recommend me."</p>
<p class="en-para">In the corporate culture I grew up in, if a subordinate has a good opportunity to leave, I wouldn't hold him back — I'd only wish him well. For if I truly held him back, I'd feel I owed him a debt that could never be repaid. He would always wonder whether leaving would have been better.</p>
<p class="en-para">When Hu questioned his prospects for the ITRI presidency, I didn't hold him back — instead I encouraged him to take the Chiao Tung position. But the next day he told me the Chiao Tung job had gone to someone else. Later, Ruan Da-nian, who became Chiao Tung President, visited me and told me the position was originally meant for Hu, but Premier Yu felt I needed Hu's assistance at ITRI, so it went to Ruan instead. Ruan seemed a bit unhappy telling me this, as if he were merely the "second choice." So Premier Yu's decision offended three people: Ruan, for being the "second choice"; Hu, for losing the Chiao Tung presidency; and me, because although I valued Hu, I didn't think I needed him that much, yet my superior believed I must have his support.</p>
<p class="en-para">Premier Yu's good intentions were futile. He could prevent Hu from going to Chiao Tung, but he couldn't make him stay at ITRI. Six months later, Hu told me he was leaving to join Hsu Ta-lin's H&Q Taiwan VC fund — the same fund I had declined to co-run back in 1985. I did not think this was a good opportunity for Hu; I thought it worse than his chances of eventually becoming ITRI President. But his mind was made up. He formally resigned in February 1988.</p>
<p class="en-para">Hu's resignation sealed my reputation for lacking "harmony with people." Shortly after his departure, both my bosses — ITRI Chairman Hsu Hsiu-hsien and Minister of Economic Affairs Li Da-hai — told me that in the Minister's view I was poor at handling personnel matters. Soon after, Chen Lian became Minister of Economic Affairs. I immediately found that seeing Minister Chen was far harder than seeing Minister Li had been. Minister Li and I had a fairly relaxed relationship. Because my reforms needed his support, I often visited him — typically just needing to call his secretary a day or two ahead. Minister Chen required scheduling days, even one to two weeks in advance; sometimes appointments were changed at the last minute. Once I arrived at the MOEA on time. After waiting half an hour in the reception room outside his office, he hurried out, saw me waiting, and said: "Oh, I'm going to a meeting downstairs — come with me and talk to me on the stairs."</p>

### Cheat Sheet · 商业语汇

1. **"Second choice" (第二选择)**
**中文解释**：指在选拔过程中并非首选的候选人；阮大年得知自己只是交大校长的「第二选择」后的不满，体现了组织任命中的人情考量对个人尊严的影响。
**商业造句**：*Being the "second choice" is a subtle but toxic form of organizational disrespect — it signals that someone else was preferred, and you were the compromise.*

2. **Promotion from deputy to president (由副扶正)**
**中文解释**：从副职升为正职；胡定华认为在台湾的文化中，「由副扶正」不太常见，这句话反映了他对自身晋升前景的悲观判断。
**商业造句**：*Hu's skepticism about "promotion from deputy to president" reflected a cultural reality in Taiwan's institutional landscape — lateral hires from outside were often preferred over internal succession.*

3. **Lacking "harmony with people" (欠缺人和)**
**中文解释**：张忠谋被公认为缺乏「人和」——即在组织内维持和谐关系的能力；这是他在工研院改革失败的关键原因。
**商业造句**：*The verdict that Chang lacked "harmony with people" was the obituary of his reform agenda — in a culture that values consensus, being labeled divisive is a career-ending judgment.*
