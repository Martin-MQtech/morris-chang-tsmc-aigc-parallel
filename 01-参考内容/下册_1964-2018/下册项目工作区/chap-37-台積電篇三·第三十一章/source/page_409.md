**亨利·季辛吉（Henry Kissinger）**

季辛吉以亨利·季辛吉（Henry Kissinger）任理事长的最后一年（二○○五至二○○六年）。费城召开的理事会议中，季辛吉事先已经知道我在美加上大学及研究所，也在德仪做事多年，之后才到台湾创办台积电，所以特別找我单独谈。

我到了一个宴席场合，季辛吉问我一个当时很热门的伊朗核武问题，那人很完整地解释为何当时媒体建议的方案不可行。接着季辛吉将话题转向台湾。首先他谈一九七一年他第一次访问北京周恩来。他说毛泽东掌权时，毛、周两人对「世界只有一个中国，就是中华人民共和国，而台湾属于中国」是无可妥协的坚决；然而对当时的美国，想要把中国从苏联的圈子拉出来又是多么重要。

季辛吉真是一个不错的外交家，在娓娓道出三十几年前的故事时，他也表达无可奈何的遗憾和淡淡的哀伤。临别时，他还给了我一张名片，希望我到他的纽约办公室去看他。我一直没去看他——我想，除了闲聊世界，没什么理由要找他。只是到了二○二三我在电视上见到他已一百岁，高度驼背，而且身材萎缩得走路都要人扶着的状況下，还飞去北京见习近平。回想我在二○○六年见他时，他还相當健壮英挺，不禁有些感慨。

**从演讲学到的**

二○○七年十二月十二日晚在华府国会演讲后，次日我就照原定行程启程返台。十五日清晨抵达台北，当天晚上恰巧高盛集团合伙人宋学仁设宴，约十余位宾客。既然我刚在华府，学仁就要我报告一下在那边的见闻，我就把「山雨欲来风满楼」的感觉讲述了一番。想不到的是，当晚一位我不很熟的朋友在两三年后见到我，特別谢谢我——说他那晚听了我出清股票，因此躲避了二○○八年全球金融危机股价狂泻的灾难。

<p class="en-para">**Henry Kissinger**</p>
<p class="en-para">In 2005–2006, the final year of Kissinger's tenure as chairman of the board, a board meeting was held in Philadelphia. Having learned in advance that I had studied in the United States and Canada, worked many years at TI, and then came to Taiwan to found TSMC, he specifically asked to speak with me privately.</p>
<p class="en-para">At a banquet, Kissinger asked me about the then-hot issue of Iran's nuclear program, and I explained comprehensively why the方案 proposed by the media at the time was unworkable. He then turned the conversation to Taiwan. He began by recounting his first visit to Beijing in 1971 to meet Zhou Enlai. He said that when Mao Zedong was in power, both Mao and Zhou were uncompromisingly firm on the position that 'there is only one China in the world, the People's Republic of China, and Taiwan belongs to China'; yet for the United States at the time, it was vitally important to draw China out of the Soviet orbit.</p>
<p class="en-para">Kissinger was indeed a fine diplomat. As he recounted the story from over thirty years earlier, he conveyed a sense of helpless regret and a faint sadness. At parting, he gave me his business card and hoped I would visit him at his New York office. I never did—I could think of no reason to seek him out beyond casual conversation about the world. But in 2023, when I saw on television that he was one hundred years old, severely hunched, and so diminished in stature that he needed assistance to walk, and yet he still flew to Beijing to meet Xi Jinping, I couldn't help but reflect on how vigorous and commanding he had appeared when I saw him in 2006.</p>
<p class="en-para">**What I Learned from a Speech**
After my speech at the U.S. Capitol on the evening of December 12, 2007, I departed the next day as planned and returned to Taiwan. I arrived in Taipei in the early morning of the fifteenth, and that evening an opportunity arose: Henry Song, a partner at Goldman Sachs, hosted a dinner with about a dozen guests. Since I had just returned from Washington, Song asked me to share what I had observed. I described the feeling that 'storms gather before the rain.' Not long afterward, in a couple of years, an acquaintance whom I did not know well specifically thanked me—he told me that having heard my remarks that night, he liquidated his stock holdings and thus avoided the devastation of the 2008 global financial crisis.</p>
