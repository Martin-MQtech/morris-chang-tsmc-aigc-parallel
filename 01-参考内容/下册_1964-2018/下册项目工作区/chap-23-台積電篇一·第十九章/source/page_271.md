## 魏谋辞职

正在业务看好，"筚路蓝缕"时代就要结束，"咆哮"时代即将开始，魏谋突然辞职。其实他的辞职有迹可循，虽非我所促使，但亦非我不欢迎。

魏谋是一个相当杰出的工程师。在台积电的头四年（一九八七至九零年），他对制造工程颇有贡献。他领导台积电的制造团队接受英特尔要求的制造制度，使我们的制造迈上世界级。他也创新——二厂的设计、袖珍洁净箱、采用名不见经传艾司摩尔的曝光机等等。

但是他可对市场、行销一无所知，甚至没有兴趣。这当然不是一位称职总经理应有的态度。我在不得已情况下任命他为总经理，而他在市场、行销上的缺点，在九零年已明显地出现了。

更成问题的是他到处为自己打算的性格。他接任台积电总经理不久后的一天，他的座车（他不在里面）与一辆工研院的车相擦，那时我还是工研院院长，八、九成时间花在工研院上，我的办公室也在工研院里。魏谋的办公室在台积电一厂，是工研院租给台积电的。我忽然接到魏谋电话，我还以为台积电有什么突破，高兴地接起话筒，但是他用责问的口气说："我以台积电总经理的地位，对工研院院长…"

我还是他的上司。为了这样的小事（没人受伤，保险公司理赔），值得用这样的态度对待你的上司吗？

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">Just when the business was looking up, the "trailblazing" era was about to end, and the "roaring" era was about to begin, Wei Mou suddenly resigned. In truth, his resignation had been foreshadowed. While I had not encouraged it, neither was I unwelcoming of it.</p>
<p class="en-para">Wei Mou was a quite outstanding engineer. In TSMC's first four years (1987-1990), he made considerable contributions to manufacturing engineering. He led TSMC's manufacturing team to adopt the production systems that Intel required, bringing our manufacturing up to world-class. He also innovated—Fab 2 design, SMIF mini-environments, adoption of the then-unknown ASML steppers, and more.</p>
<p class="en-para">But he knew nothing about marketing and sales, and was not even interested. This was certainly not an attitude a competent general manager should have. I had appointed him general manager out of necessity, and his shortcomings in marketing became glaringly apparent by 1990.</p>
<p class="en-para">Even more problematic was his self-serving personality. Not long after taking over as TSMC general manager, one day his car (he was not inside) lightly scraped an ITRI vehicle. At that time I was still ITRI president, spending 80-90% of my time on ITRI matters; my office was also in ITRI. Wei Mou's office was in TSMC Fab 1, leased to TSMC by ITRI. I suddenly received a call from Wei Mou. I thought TSMC had some breakthrough and happily picked up the receiver, but he said in a demanding tone: "As general manager of TSMC, I have a right to say to the ITRI president..."</p>
<p class="en-para">I was still his boss. For such a minor matter (no one was injured, insurance covered it), was it worth treating your superior with such an attitude?</p>
</div>

## 魏谋对飞利浦输诚

更严重的事发生了。八八九年底，飞利浦提出股权承购要求，魏谋立刻认定飞利浦必定很快成为台积电的主人。他在第一个机会——飞利浦提出申诸后的第一个台积电董事会，私约飞利浦的IC总经理（那时克列斯曼已被撤职，换了一位对我相当友善的哈格曼斯达（Heinz Hagmeister））。魏谋告诉哈氏：飞利浦购并台积电后，他会留下来，所以飞利浦不必担心台积电的稳定性。

连哈格曼斯达都惊奇魏谋这样的提早"输诚"，所以第二天就告诉我魏谋对他说的话——显然哈氏认为与我好关系，比魏谋的输诚更重要。

我没有告诉魏谋我已知道他对飞利浦输诚，但九零年上半年，他几乎每星期问我："承购案进行得怎样？"我不说。我有我的战术和战略，只能与赵扬清讨论，不能对任何人说，更不能对叛逆者说。后来他不耐烦了，凶悍地对我说："我是总经理，为什么不告诉我？"我回答："好好地做你的总经理，此事与你无关。"

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">Something more serious happened. At year-end 1989, when Philips made its equity subscription demand, Wei Mou immediately concluded that Philips would soon become TSMC's master. At the very first opportunity—the first TSMC board meeting after Philips made its request—he privately met with Philips' IC general manager (by then Krigesman had been removed and replaced by a quite friendly Heinz Hagmeister). Wei Mou told Hagmeister: after Philips acquired TSMC, he would stay, so Philips need not worry about TSMC's stability.</p>
<p class="en-para">Even Hagmeister was astonished by Wei Mou's premature "surrender," so the next day he told me what Wei Mou had said to him—clearly Hagmeister believed maintaining a good relationship with me was more important than Wei Mou's defection.</p>
<p class="en-para">I didn't tell Wei Mou that I already knew about his defection to Philips. But in the first half of 1990, he asked me almost weekly: "How is the subscription case progressing?" I didn't tell him. I had my own tactics and strategy, which I could only discuss with Zhao Yangqing—not with anyone else, and certainly not with a defector. Eventually, growing impatient, he said fiercely to me: "I'm the general manager; why won't you tell me?" I replied: "Do your job as general manager properly. This matter doesn't concern you."</p>
</div>

## 魏谋离去

九零年秋，承购案继续在我策略下拖延，魏谋等不住了，刚巧新加坡的"特许半导体公司"要聘他为总经理，他辞职了。我并不惊奇。在他对飞利浦输诚那一刻，他显然以为我已是即将退出舞台的过去人物，而他要保护自己的既有地位。事实上，我不但不是过去人物，我的黄金时代正要开始，而他却与他一生难得的黄金机会擦身而过。

台积电并没亏待魏谋。他离开时，已认了好几次的员工认股。如果他没卖掉这些股票，这些股票应也是一笔小财。我也认为这是他那几年在台积电制造工程上贡献应得的报酬。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">In the fall of 1990, with the subscription case continuing to be delayed under my strategy, Wei Mou could wait no longer. As it happened, Singapore's "Chartered Semiconductor" wanted to hire him as general manager, and he resigned. I was not surprised. At the moment he defected to Philips, he apparently believed I was already a fading figure about to exit the stage, and he wanted to protect his own position. In reality, I was not a fading figure at all—my golden era was just beginning—and yet he let the golden opportunity of a lifetime pass him by.</p>
<p class="en-para">TSMC had not treated Wei Mou poorly. When he left, he had already subscribed to employee stock options several times. If he hadn't sold those shares, they would have been a small fortune. I also felt this was deserved compensation for his contributions to TSMC's manufacturing engineering over the years.</p>
</div>
