## 二厂的创新

在倡议台积电及募资时，我一直视一厂为过渡厂，而自己设计、建造的二厂为我们头五年的主力。募资金额也以建立及营运二厂所需资本为主。一厂只是初期"练兵"地点而已。所以几乎从一厂开张第一天，我们就开始设计、规划二厂；并且在同年九月，就向董事会提出二厂投资案。

二厂的初期规模是每月（六吋）三万片。在我们对董事会提案二厂时，我们每月生意只有几百片！克列斯曼（代表飞利浦的台积电董事）以为我们发疯，领导全董事会反对，当然不能通过。

董事会三个月一次。被拒的一个半月后，我们重整旗鼓，把提案的包装稍微更改，而且给足克列斯曼面子——我、戴克斯、魏谋、繁城四人特地联袂飞硅谷，访克列斯曼及飞利浦半导体财务长于他们在Signetics的办公室，并花了几小时时间解释美欧生意即将到来，我们需要现在就通过二厂案。这次访问果然奏效。三个月后的董事会，克列斯曼不再反对，二厂投资案也就顺利通过了。

二厂于次年（一九八八年）四月在新竹科学园区动土，一九九零年开始运作，开启了我下篇要讲的台积电"咆哮九零年代"。

使得台积电能够咆哮的原因之一，是二厂的创新。当然，"专业晶圆代工"商业模式是台积电最大的创新，但持续不断技术上、生产上、市场上的创新，自公司创立就开始。二厂就有两个相当重要的决定。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">When proposing TSMC and raising funds, I always viewed Fab 1 as a transitional facility, while Fab 2, which we would design and build ourselves, would be our main workhorse for the first five years. The fundraising amount was also based on the capital needed to build and operate Fab 2. Fab 1 was merely an initial "training" site. So almost from Fab 1's first day of operations, we began designing and planning Fab 2, and in September of that same year, we presented the Fab 2 investment proposal to the board.</p>
<p class="en-para">Fab 2's initial projected capacity was 30,000 wafers (6-inch) per month. When we proposed Fab 2 to the board, our monthly business was only a few hundred wafers! Krigsman (the TSMC director representing Philips) thought we had gone crazy and led the entire board in opposition—naturally, it could not pass.</p>
<p class="en-para">The board met once every three months. A month and a half after the rejection, we regrouped, slightly adjusted the presentation packaging, and gave Krigesman ample face—Dexter, Wei Mou, Fan-cheng, and I together flew to Silicon Valley and visited Krigesman and the Philips semiconductor CFO at their Signetics offices, spending several hours explaining that U.S. and European business was imminent and we needed to pass the Fab 2 proposal now. This visit proved effective. Three months later at the board meeting, Krigesman no longer opposed, and the Fab 2 investment proposal passed smoothly.</p>
<p class="en-para">Fab 2 broke ground in Hsinchu Science Park in April the following year (1988), and began operations in 1990, ushering in what I will discuss in the next volume as TSMC's "Roaring '90s."</p>
<p class="en-para">One of the reasons TSMC was able to roar was Fab 2's innovations. Of course, the "dedicated foundry" business model was TSMC's biggest innovation, but continuous innovation in technology, production, and marketing had begun since the company's founding. Fab 2 alone involved two very important decisions.</p>
</div>

## 第一个决定：SMIF袖珍洁净箱

第一个决定是启用SMIF（Standard Mechanical Interface，袖珍洁净箱）。IC制程必须在"无尘"环境内，如果整个工厂都是"洁净室"，那么排气系统相当昂贵，而且运作时非常费电。SMIF是一家名叫Asyst的硅谷公司所发明，它的妙处是一个"袖珍"洁净箱，可以与生产机器连接。与标准洁净室比较，它既节省排气系统的资本支出，又节省电力，而且据Asyst当时说，"袖珍"洁净环境其实比标准洁净室更为洁净，所以应该提高良率。

魏谋和曾繁城都支持SMIF。我打电话给一位英特尔友人，问他知不知道SMIF？他说，英特尔在实验室试用过，SMIF"很有希望。但是，英特尔是大公司，承担不起大规模使用SMIF的风险。你们是刚开始的小公司，也许承担得起这风险"。

这句无意的轻语（至少我想是无意的），倒提高了我的冒险意愿。毕竟，小公司要赶上大公司，必须在对的地方做大事不愿或不敢做的事。我希望SMIF是一个对的地方。

我们决定用SMIF。台积电的二厂是世界上第一家在量产环境内用SMIF的工厂。此后，所有台积电晶圆厂都用SMIF。后来听说同业也陆续采用SMIF。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">The first decision was to adopt SMIF (Standard Mechanical Interface, mini-environments). IC processing must take place in a "dust-free" environment. If the entire factory were a "clean room," the ventilation system would be extremely expensive, and very costly to operate. SMIF was invented by a Silicon Valley company called Asyst. Its clever feature was a "miniature" clean environment that could be connected to production equipment. Compared to a standard clean room, it saved both capital expenditure on ventilation systems and electricity, and moreover, according to Asyst at the time, the "miniature" clean environment was actually cleaner than a standard clean room, so it should improve yield.</p>
<p class="en-para">Both Wei Mou and Zeng Fan-cheng supported SMIF. I called an Intel friend and asked if he knew about SMIF. He said Intel had trialed it in the lab; SMIF "shows promise. But Intel is a big company and cannot afford the risk of large-scale SMIF adoption. You're a small, new company—maybe you can afford the risk."</p>
<p class="en-para">This offhand remark (at least I believe it was offhand) actually increased my willingness to take risks. After all, for a small company to catch up to big companies, it must, in the right places, do things that big companies are unwilling or afraid to do. I hoped SMIF was a right place.</p>
<p class="en-para">We decided to use SMIF. TSMC's Fab 2 was the first factory in the world to use SMIF in a production environment. Thereafter, all TSMC fabs used SMIF. Later I heard that competitors also gradually adopted SMIF.</p>
</div>

## 第二个决定：采用艾司摩尔（ASML）的曝光机

第二个决定是采用艾司摩尔（ASML）的曝光机。曝光机在生产线上诸多机器中最昂贵。在建二厂时日本Nikon以及Canon公司几乎占有全部世界曝光机市场。

艾司摩尔却是一家新公司。巧合的是，它和台积电一样，也是飞利浦投资的公司（飞利浦投资IC相关事业的智慧，实在远胜它自己经营IC事业的智慧）。我还记得在募资时期有一次访问荷兰飞利浦时，一位中年人从空旷走廊的另一端滚球般走过来。与我在一起的飞利浦副总与他打招呼，也对我们说："让我介绍，这位是我们投资的艾司摩尔公司董事长，这位是我们投资的TSMC董事长。"

台积电成立后，Nietzsche、Nikon、ASML高阶经理人员多次来推销各自的曝光机。经过魏谋和曾繁城详细研究，认为艾司摩尔机器有技术优点。唯一问题是：没人用过艾司摩尔机器，台积电会是第一家，的确有相当风险。

我们还是决定用艾司摩尔。在这课题上，我的想法和在SMIF上一样：小公司如要追上大公司必须要冒些险。

此后三十几年，艾司摩尔一直是台积电最主要且最重要的曝光机供应商。同时，艾司摩尔的技术也一直领先Canon与Nikon。艾司摩尔逐渐扩大市占率。至今日，它的市占率已独占鳌头，公司市值也已超过两千亿美元。台积电与艾司摩尔几乎同时诞生，一起成长，一直以伙伴关系相处。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">The second decision was to adopt ASML steppers (exposure equipment). Steppers are the most expensive of many machines on a production line. When building Fab 2, Japanese Nikon and Canon virtually dominated the entire world stepper market.</p>
<p class="en-para">ASML, however, was a startup. Coincidentally, like TSMC, it was also a Philips-invested company (Philips' wisdom in investing in IC-related businesses far exceeded its wisdom in operating its own IC business). I still recall that during the fundraising period, on a visit to Philips in the Netherlands, a middle-aged man came rolling toward us from the other end of a long hallway. The Philips VP walking with me greeted him and said to us: "Let me introduce—this is the chairman of ASML, the company we invested in; this is the chairman of TSMC, the company we invested in."</p>
<p class="en-para">After TSMC's founding, senior executives from Nikon, Canon, and ASML all came multiple times to pitch their respective steppers. After detailed study by Wei Mou and Zeng Fan-cheng, they concluded that ASML's machines had technical merits. The sole problem was: no one had used ASMM machines before; TSMC would be the first—there was considerable risk.</p>
<p class="en-para">We decided to go ahead with ASML. My thinking here was the same as with SMIF: if a small company wants to catch up to big companies, it must take some risks.</p>
<p class="en-para">For the next thirty-plus years, ASML has remained TSMC's primary and most important stepper supplier. At the same time, ASML's technology has consistently stayed ahead of Canon and Nikon. ASML gradually expanded its market share. To this day, its market share is dominant, and its market capitalization has exceeded $200 billion. TSMC and ASML were born almost at the same time, grew together, and have always maintained a partnership relationship.</p>
</div>
