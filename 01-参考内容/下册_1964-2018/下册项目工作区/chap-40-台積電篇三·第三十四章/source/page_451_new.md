<p class="en-para">After Sandi's retirement, I announced on November 12 that Tim Liu and Morris Wei would serve as TSMC's co-CEOs. Since 2005, when Morris Cai was CEO and they each served as senior vice president in charge of the Advanced Technology Business and Mainstream Technology Business respectively, both Tim and Morris had been among TSMC's most important managers.</p>
<p class="en-para">This organizational structure remained in place until I retired in 2018.</p>
<p class="en-para">My retirement decision, however, had been made in 2017. From 2010, when I resumed an active role at the company, through to 2017 when I made my retirement decision, TSMC's internal morale was high and its external growth was strong. Revenue grew from 103 billion NT dollars in 2010 to 977.4 billion in 2017; operating profit grew from 195.6 billion in 2010 to near a historical high in 2017.</p>
<p class="en-para">On the technology side, during these nine years TSMC sequentially launched forty, twenty-eight, twenty, sixteen, twelve, and ten nanometer nodes. By 2017 we were also preparing to mass-produce seven nanometer the following year—a head start that greatly outpaced our competitors. Meanwhile, in chip packaging technology, we launched InFO and CoWoS—the former critical for smartphones, the latter for artificial intelligence.</p>
<p class="en-para">These nine years also brought me to the age of eighty-six. I reflected that life is impermanent, and I should take advantage of my continued good health to retire fully from TSMC and do the things I had always wanted to do but had not yet: (1) write the second volume of my autobiography; (2) introduce to Sophie the places I frequently visited and loved most during my years at TI—Paris, the French Riviera, Monaco, Switzerland, London, and northern Italy; (3) indulgently satisfy my hobbies from university days: reading outside my professional field, listening to classical music, and competitive bridge.</p>
