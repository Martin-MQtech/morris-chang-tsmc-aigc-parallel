<p class="en-para">I had begun packing in my Hsinchu office and at our Hsinchu home as early as late May. The office was handed over to the new chairman on June 5. Our Hsinchu home had been purchased by me, but Sophie sold it before my retirement.</p>
<p class="en-para">My sixty-three-year career, from age twenty-four to eighty-seven, thus came to an end. I had never intended to work so long. At TI, I had always hoped to retire at sixty-five. When I came to Taiwan at fifty-four, I still hoped to retire at sixty-five. But after founding TSMC, the more successful it became, the higher my enthusiasm and expectations rose, until eventually I simply could not bring myself to stop.</p>
<p class="en-para">In my sixty-three-year career—three years at Sylvania (1955–1958), twenty-five at TI (1958–1983), and thirty-two at TSMC (1985–2018, including the two years of planning and fundraising)—almost all of it was in the semiconductor industry. Except for the later years at TI, it was almost entirely active and forward-moving.</p>
<p class="en-para">My most successful undertaking was obviously my last one—TSMC. TSMC is now a 'must-have' company in the world, and the world's 'need'—especially in artificial intelligence—is growing rapidly. TSMC must closely follow this growth in demand, and it cannot relax on cost.</p>
<p class="en-para">The responsibility for TSMC now rests on my successors' shoulders. I hold them in the deepest expectation and offer them my most sincere blessings.</p>
