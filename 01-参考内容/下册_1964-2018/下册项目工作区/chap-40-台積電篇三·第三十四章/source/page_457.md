**Planning for the Handover and Retirement**

My succession planning began in 2005 with the release of the CEO role to Tsai Li-hsing while I remained chairman. But after difficulties led me to reclaim the CEO role in 2009, the succession path shifted. In 2012, I promoted three Co-COOs—Shang-yi Chiang, Liu De-yin, and Wei Che-chia—tasking them with rotating responsibility for R&D, manufacturing, and business development. When Chiang retired in 2013, Liu and Wei were named Co-CEOs.

In 2017, now eighty-six, I made my retirement decision. TSMC's operational and technological momentum was strong. I crafted a "dual-key" succession plan: Liu De-yin as chairman and Wei Che-chia as president, with equal rank and compensation. The board approved in September 2017, and in October I announced the plan publicly, culminating in a memorable thirtieth-anniversary celebration where, after my retirement remarks, the audience rose to their feet in thunderous applause—a tribute from the customers who had been my partners for decades.

On June 5, 2018, I retired. My sixty-three-year career in semiconductors—from Sylvania to Texas Instruments to TSMC—had come to an end. TSMC's burden now rested on my successors' shoulders.
