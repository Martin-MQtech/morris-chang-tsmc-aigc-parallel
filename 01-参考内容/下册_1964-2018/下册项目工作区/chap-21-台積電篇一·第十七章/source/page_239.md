# 台积电篇一·第十七章｜募资

## 寻找第二主角

俞国华院长指定台积电"五人小组"时，政府已决定出资48%。但是，俞院长有一个条件：我必须先找一家有IC技术的跨国公司，要他们做第二主要投资者；然后，政府才能去找台湾民间，台湾民间也才有信心凑足余额。

俞院长的预言百分之百正确。后来，即使飞利浦同意投资27.6%后，政府还是花了九牛二虎之力，才凑足剩余的24.1%。

一九八五年是世界半导体业历史到那时为止最不景气的一年——成长率是负16%。大部分半导体公司都求生存。在这时刻，我找他们投资一家以前从未尝试过的"专业晶圆代工"公司，而且这家公司位在当时美、日、欧半导体公司都认为是"半导体不毛之地"的台湾！

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">When Premier Yu Kuo-hua appointed the "Five-Person Committee" for TSMC, the government had already decided to contribute 48% of the capital. However, Premier Yu set one condition: I had to first find a multinational company with IC technology to serve as the second lead investor; only then would the government approach Taiwanese private investors, and only then would they have confidence to raise the remainder.</p>
<p class="en-para">Premier Yu's prediction proved 100% correct. Later, even after Philips agreed to invest 27.6%, the government still had to expend tremendous effort to raise the remaining 24.1%.</p>
<p class="en-para">1985 was the worst year in the history of the global semiconductor industry up to that point—growth rate was negative 16%. Most semiconductor companies were merely surviving. At this moment, I approached them to invest in a "dedicated foundry" company—a business model never attempted before—and one located in Taiwan, which American, Japanese, and European semiconductor firms all considered a "semiconductor barren land"!</p>
</div>

## 四处碰壁

在八五年十二月及翌年一月，我还是试了。虽然那时我已相信飞利浦有投资诚意，但一切条件都还未开始谈，我不知道谈得成否。况且，我认为多找几家公司来与飞利浦竞争，绝对对我们有利。我写信或打电话给英特尔、德仪、摩托罗拉、松下、新力、迪吉多、超微、汤姆生等公司的总裁。除了松下是孙运璿先生特别要我接触，其余都是我在德仪及通用时期熟悉的。大部分都有回信，但信是千篇一律的"无兴趣"。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">In December 1985 and January 1986, I still tried. Although by then I believed Philips had genuine investment interest, no terms had been discussed yet, and I didn't know if negotiations would succeed. Moreover, I believed that having multiple companies compete with Philips would definitely be to our advantage. I wrote letters or made phone calls to the CEOs of Intel, Texas Instruments, Motorola, Matsushita, Sony, DEC, AMD, and Thomson. Except for Matsushita, which Mr. Sun Yun-suan specifically asked me to approach, the rest were companies I had known well from my TI and GE days. Most responded, but the letters were uniformly "not interested."</p>
</div>
