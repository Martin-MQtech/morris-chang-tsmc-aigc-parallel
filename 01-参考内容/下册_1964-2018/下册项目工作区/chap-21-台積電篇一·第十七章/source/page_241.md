## 飞利浦的动机

这个二流一排的半导体公司，为什么在这半导体业萧条年头，别的半导体公司都无兴趣投资台積電之时，单独有兴趣？答案有二。其一：半导体并非飞利浦主业，虽然八五年半导体萧条，但飞利浦别的业务并不萧条，所以总公司仍有足够现金可供投资。答案之二更为重要：那时中国大陆尚未开放，飞利浦总部已决定把台湾作为他们的低工资生产基地。他们本来在台湾就有IC装配测试厂，现在准备在台湾大肆建立被动元件、光电等生产基地；他们正在想如何才能获得台湾政府的青睐，恰巧碰到台湾政府招外商投资台積電，台積電就成为他们台湾扩充计划的第一炮。

把镜头移后几年。果然，在八零年代晚期及九零年代初期，飞利浦在台湾大肆扩充被动元件及光电等生产基地，雇用员工最高达四万余人。但是，九零年代中期大陆开始开放，外国公司蜂拥而入，大陆成为新的世界低工资生产基地。飞利浦也弃旧抱新，很快地从台湾搬到大陆去，在台湾只留下行销人员。今日飞利浦在台湾的员工不到一千人；对台積電的投资，也在获厚利后于二零零八年终结——但这些都是以后的故事。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">Why was this tier-2 tier-1 semiconductor company—alone among all semiconductor companies—interested in investing in TSMC during an industry downturn, when others had no interest? There were two answers. First: semiconductors were not Philips' core business. Although the semiconductor sector was in recession in 1985, Philips' other businesses were not, so headquarters still had sufficient cash for investment. The second answer was more important: mainland China had not yet opened up, and Philips headquarters had already decided to make Taiwan their low-wage production base. They already had an IC assembly and test factory in Taiwan, and were now planning to aggressively build production sites for passive components, optoelectronics, and more. They were wondering how to gain the favor of the Taiwanese government—and then, by coincidence, the Taiwanese government sought foreign investment for TSMC. TSMC became the first shot of their Taiwan expansion plan.</p>
<p class="en-para">Fast forward a few years. Indeed, in the late 1980s and early 1990s, Philips aggressively expanded its passive component and optoelectronics production bases in Taiwan, employing up to 40,000 workers at its peak. But by the mid-1990s, mainland China began opening up; foreign companies poured in, and mainland China became the world's new low-wage production base. Philips also abandoned the old for the new, quickly relocating from Taiwan to mainland China, leaving only marketing staff in Taiwan. Today, Philips employs fewer than a thousand people in Taiwan; its investment in TSMC, after reaping substantial profits, ended in 2008—but these are all stories for later.</p>
</div>

## 初访荷兰

回到一九八五年。李政委告知我飞利浦有兴趣投资后，我就与飞利浦接触，接着遵他们嘱写投资建议书，并在十一月三日至六日访问荷兰飞利浦。我曾在一九六九年以德仪集成电路总经理身份访问荷兰飞利浦。那时德仪稳居世界IC业领袖，飞利浦技术落后，想买德仪的"技术授权"。我受飞利浦之邀，去考察他们是否有接受德仪技术的能力。那次访问，我以"征服者"姿态出现。

但这次却大不相同。这次我是"恳求者"，恳求飞利浦投资。角色转换，沧海桑田，使我晚上在旅舍中唏嘘不已。尤其，这次我并没有见到对方的主角——飞利浦IC总经理克列斯曼（Krigsman），他在美国。

这次访问飞利浦，给我最深印象是飞利浦正在巨额投资建两个IC厂。他们在奈梅亨建IC生产厂，同时在恩荷芬建IC"研发厂"；引导我参观的人告诉我："研发厂的规模与生产厂相似。"亲眼看到这样巨额的投资在进行式中（必在两亿美元以上），我比较放心我们的要求（四千万美元）会被接受。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">Back to 1985. After Commissioner Li informed me of Philips' investment interest, I made contact with Philips, then followed their instructions to write an investment proposal, and visited Philips in the Netherlands from November 3 to 6. I had visited Philips in 1969 as TI's integrated circuit general manager. At that time, TI stood firmly as the world leader in the IC industry; Philips was behind technologically and wanted to purchase TI's "technology license." I accepted Philips' invitation to assess whether they had the capability to absorb TI's technology. On that visit, I appeared as a "conqueror."</p>
<p class="en-para">But this time was completely different. This time I was a "supplicant," begging Philips to invest. The role reversal—the vast change of circumstances—made me sigh in my hotel room at night. Moreover, this time I did not meet the key person on their side—Philips' IC general manager Krigsman. He was in the United States.</p>
<p class="en-para">What impressed me most during this Philips visit was that they were making enormous investments to build two IC factories. They were building an IC production facility in Nijmegen, and simultaneously an IC "R&amp;D fab" in Eindhoven; the person guiding me told me: "The R&amp;D fab's scale is similar to the production fab." Witnessing such massive investment firsthand (it must have exceeded $200 million), I felt more confident that our request ($40 million) would be accepted.</p>
</div>
