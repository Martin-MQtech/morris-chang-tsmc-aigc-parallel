## 德仪与英特尔的拒绝

除了德仪和英特尔。德仪是我的老东家，缘虽断但情犹在。德仪的半导体总经理席克（第十章曾提到他）邀我去达拉斯见面。我去了，对他及几位他的幕僚做了一个简报。当时他就说希望不大，但答应考虑并与总裁夏伯特讨论。几个月后，我与飞利浦已即将成局，席克又邀请我再去达拉斯。这次他没有幕僚陪同，单独告诉我德仪不准备投资。

英特尔的故事相似。我写信给英特尔总裁，也是我的老朋友摩尔。摩尔邀我与他的技术及生产总监巴瑞特（Craig Barrett）相会。我做了半小时左右的简报，并且回答了他的问题。后来几星期音讯全无，我就打电话找巴瑞特，找了好几个地方才找到他。他说：英特尔无兴趣投资。

现在（一九八六年五月）只剩飞利浦了——虽然飞利浦还不知道他们是唯一表达兴趣的投资者。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">Except for TI and Intel. TI was my former employer—the bond was broken but the affection remained. TI's semiconductor general manager Sheek (mentioned in Chapter 10) invited me to Dallas. I went and gave a briefing to him and several of his staff. At the time he said the chances weren't great, but agreed to consider it and discuss with president Sheppard. Months later, when my negotiations with Philips were nearly concluded, Sheek invited me to Dallas again. This time he had no staff accompanying him and told me privately that TI would not invest.</p>
<p class="en-para">Intel's story was similar. I wrote to Intel's president, my old friend Gordon Moore. Moore arranged for me to meet his technology and production director Craig Barrett. I gave about a half-hour briefing and answered his questions. Then weeks went by with no word, so I called Barrett, tracking him down through several locations. He said: Intel was not interested in investing.</p>
<p class="en-para">By now (May 1986), only Philips remained—though Philips didn't yet know they were the sole investor to express interest.</p>

## 飞利浦

飞利浦成立于一八九一年，以电灯泡起家。在二十世纪，它陆续进入家电、被动元件、半导体、光电、医疗器材等业。在一九八零年代，它是世界最大跨国公司之一，也是一家有IC技术的公司。

但是在半导体业内，无论从营收规模、技术强度或获利率角度看，飞利浦都非世界第一流。我把它排在二流行列中的第一排。这样排法虽非褒，但亦非贬。因为在我心目中，当时一流的第一排为英特尔独占；德仪、摩托罗拉只是一流的第二排。二流的第一除飞利浦外，还有人。二流的第二排以及三流，人更多。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">Philips was founded in 1891, starting with light bulbs. Throughout the twentieth century, it successively entered home appliances, passive components, semiconductors, optoelectronics, and medical equipment. In the 1980s, it was one of the world's largest multinational corporations, and a company with IC technology.</p>
<p class="en-para">However, within the semiconductor industry—whether measured by revenue scale, technology strength, or profitability—Philips was not world-class first tier. I ranked it in the first row of the second tier. This ranking was neither praise nor criticism. Because in my mind, the first row of first tier was occupied solely by Intel; TI and Motorola were merely the second row of first tier. The first row of second tier, besides Philips, had others. The second row of second tier and third tier had even more.</p>
