> **背景知识延伸｜摩尔定律与节点演进**
>
> 摩尔定律由英特尔创办人之一戈登·摩尔（Gordon Moore）于1965年提出。台积电在十四年内完成九个节点的跃迁（2.0µm→0.18µm），意味着每十六个月推进一代——比摩尔定律的节奏还快。这背后是台积电在九十年代累计投入数百亿新台币的研发支出，以及其独特商业模式带来的规模经济效益。
>
> <div class="rebook-translation">
> <span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
> <p class="en-para">Moore's Law, named after Intel co-founder Gordon Moore, was proposed in 1965. In fourteen years, TSMC completed nine technology-node transitions (from 2.0 µm to 0.18 µm)—advancing one generation roughly every sixteen months, a pace that outstripped Moore's original forecast. Behind this acceleration lay TSMC's cumulative R&D expenditure of tens of billions of New Taiwan dollars throughout the 1990s, along with the economies of scale afforded by its unique foundry business model.</p>

> **修辞赏析｜「第二排的第一个」**
>
> 蒋尚义以「如果能坐到第二排第一个就很不错了」委婉回应张忠谋。这一比喻极其生动：把全球半导体产业比作阶梯教室，台积电坐在后排，距离「前排」还有相当距离。而张忠谋对这个比喻的解读——「你是说你需要三倍投资才能坐到前排？」——瞬间将对方委婉的退缩转化为进取的动力。这一段对话，将两人性格的差异浓缩在一个教室隐喻之中。
>
> <div class="rebook-translation">
> <span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
> <p class="en-para">Chiang Shang-yi responded to Chang's ambition with characteristic tact, saying that simply "sitting in the first row of the second row would be good enough." The metaphor is vivid: if the global semiconductor industry is a lecture hall, TSMC occupied the back rows, still a considerable distance from the "front." But Chang's immediate reading of the remark—"You mean you need three times the investment to get to the front?"—instantly transformed Chiang's gentle retreat into forward momentum. In this brief exchange, the difference between the two men's temperaments is compressed into a single classroom metaphor.</p>
> </div>
