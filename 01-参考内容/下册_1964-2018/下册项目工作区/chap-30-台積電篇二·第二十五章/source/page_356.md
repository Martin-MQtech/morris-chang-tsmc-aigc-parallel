## 对台积电内部讲授"经营"

九六、九七年，公司规横渐大，中阶管理人员中已出现不少我不认识的面孔，我想：应该趁这时机对中、高级经理做一系列的"经营台积电"讲授；现在讲，可能十分之二、三听课的人还会注意听，甚至传授给他们的手下；现在不讲，将来公司更大，没有机会了。

于是我开课了，每星期一小时。所有高、中阶的主管都被邀请参加，共七、八十人。我很喜欢教书，但又不喜欢准备。每次上课，我都有一个题目，然後以自然会话方式讲这题目。黑板或白板是我唯一工具。有时我的教材缺乏整理和次序，但我认为一个好学生应该在自己脑子中做这整理，这样他才更有收获。

这样的讲授，大约也举行了二、三十次吧。有收获吗？有。但恐怕不在经营技术上。毕竟台积电的组织是"功能型"的——研发部、制造部……等等。各部的经营都相当专业，而我的讲授却是"一般性"的——价值、愿景、策略……等等。最大的收获是：我们在这快速成长的关键年头，把台积电的价值观，也是我们的"经营理念"落实了。

在台积电三十一年，我和公司同仁都犯了不少错误，但绝少触犯这十条"经营理念"；在极少数触犯的案例上，我们都施予严厉的惩处。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">In 1996-97, the company was growing rapidly, and among middle-level managers there were already many faces I didn't recognize. I thought: I should take this opportunity to give a series of "Managing TSMC" lectures to middle and senior managers. If I lectured now, perhaps 20-30% of the audience would still pay attention and even pass it on to their subordinates. If I didn't lecture now, when the company got even bigger, there would be no opportunity.</p>
<p class="en-para">So I started the class, one hour every week. All senior and middle-level managers were invited, totaling 70-80 people. I very much enjoyed teaching, but disliked preparation. Each class I had a topic, which I would discuss in a natural conversational manner. The blackboard or whiteboard was my only tool. Sometimes my material lacked organization and order, but I believed a good student should do this organization in his own mind—that way he would gain more.</p>
<p class="en-para">This kind of lecturing was held perhaps twenty to thirty times. Was there gain? Yes. But probably not in management technique. After all, TSMC's organization was "functional"—R&amp;D Department, Manufacturing Department, etc. Each department's operations were quite professional, while my lectures were "general"—values, vision, strategy, etc. The biggest gain was: during this critical year of rapid growth, we instilled TSMC's values—our "business philosophy."</p>
<p class="en-para">During my 31 years at TSMC, my colleagues and I made many mistakes, but we rarely violated these ten "business philosophies." In the rare cases where they were violated, we imposed severe punishment.</p>
</div>

## 当交大"荣誉管理讲座"教授

我在台积电内部讲授"经营"的消息，很快地传到同在新竹的交通大学。张俊彦校长及管理科学系朱博涌教授都很热心要我开一门有学分的课，朱教授还自愿做我的"助教"。"经营管理专题"课就在九八年秋季学期出现。大约有一百位学生报名，其中一大半是EMBA学生，一小半是管理科学系大学生或别的学系学生，还有几位旁听生。每星期一堂课，每堂课两小时，当中休息几分钟。我一共教了十二堂，还有两堂考试——考试都由朱教授负责，打分也由他负责。我只负责讲十二堂。

这十二堂的题目是：（一）企业愿景；（二）企业文化；（三）组织结构与计；（四）决策与授权；（五）功能性组织；（六）沟通；（七）激励；（八）绩效评估；（九）学习曲线与市场占有率；（十）策略与年度规画；（十一）开会；（十二）世界级企业。

这十二堂课包含了我所有经营经验及心得，从德仪到通用到工研院到台积电。当然，许多经验和心得是负面的，但我认为负面的教训往往比正面的经验更铭心刻骨。知道不做什麼与知道做什麼一样重要。

在我开始这门课时，我对我的约一百位学生说："假如十年後，有十位学生觉得我在此讲的对他有一点用处，对我这已是很好的回馈。"

如今二十余年过去，我还未遇见任何人对我说："啊，我是你当年的交大学生。"更没有人对我说："啊，你当年讲的对我很有用。"事实上，想想我自己，不要讲小学了，从初中到博士班，我也有过一百位以上老师，其中好几位影响我後来的人生。我曾经告诉过他们吗？没有。大部分在课程结束後，就变成陌生人了。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">News of my internal "management" lectures at TSMC quickly spread to nearby National Chiao Tung University (NCTU) in Hsinchu. President Zhang Junyan and Professor Zhu Boyong of the Department of Management Science were very enthusiastic about having me teach a for-credit course. Professor Zhu even volunteered to be my "teaching assistant." The "Special Topics in Business Management" course appeared in the fall semester of 1998. About a hundred students enrolled, of whom more than half were EMBA students, less than half were undergraduate students from the Department of Management Science or other departments, and a few were auditors. One class per week, two hours per class, with a short break in between. I taught twelve sessions total, plus two exams—both exams were administered and graded by Professor Zhu. I was only responsible for the twelve lectures.</p>
<p class="en-para">The twelve session topics were: (1) Corporate Vision; (2) Corporate Culture; (3) Organizational Structure and Design; (4) Decision Making and Delegation; (5) Functional Organization; (6) Communication; (7) Motivation; (8) Performance Appraisal; (9) Learning Curve and Market Share; (10) Strategy and Annual Planning; (11) Meetings; (12) World-Class Enterprise.</p>
<p class="en-para">These twelve sessions contained all my management experience and insights, from TI to GE to ITRI to TSMC. Of course, much of the experience and many insights were negative, but I believe negative lessons are often more indelibly memorable than positive experiences. Knowing what not to do is as important as knowing what to do.</p>
<p class="en-para">When I started this course, I told my approximately 100 students: "If, ten years from now, ten students feel that what I taught here was of some use to them, that would already be a great reward for me."</p>
<p class="en-para">Now more than twenty years have passed, and I have still not met anyone who said to me: "Ah, I was your student at NCTU." Still less has anyone said: "Ah, what you taught back then was very useful to me." In fact, thinking about myself—not to mention elementary school—from middle school through Ph.D. programs, I too had over a hundred teachers, several of whom influenced my later life. Did I ever tell them? No. Most became strangers after the course ended.</p>
</div>

## 後记

後记：补充一段在此章之初，我写到当年这段"现存强权（德仪）对新兴挑战者（英代爾）"的故事时，所感受到的强烈讽刺意味：四、五十年後的英代爾，这个几十年半导体霸主，正在面对新兴的台积电。虽然鹿死谁手尚未可测，但台积电占有优势却是明显的事实，只要台积电不自踩自己脚，应该胜算在握。难道这是我的"复仇记"吗？我没有这个意思。反正上次英代爾领先时，我已离开德仪；这次尽管我情系台积电，但无论谁得胜，我也已经退休多时了。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">Epilogue: A supplementary note. Earlier in this chapter, when I wrote about the irony of the "established power (TI) vs. emerging challenger (Intel)" story from those days: forty or fifty years later, Intel, the semiconductor hegemon for decades, is now facing an emerging TSMC. Although it is still uncertain who will prevail, TSMC holds a clear advantage—as long as TSMC doesn't shoot itself in the foot, victory should be within grasp. Is this my "revenge story"? I have no such intention. After all, when Intel took the lead last time, I had already left TI. This time, although my heart is with TSMC, whoever wins, I retired long ago.</p>
</div>
