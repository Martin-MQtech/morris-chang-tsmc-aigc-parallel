# 台积电篇二·第二十五章｜作者及教授

## 作序与写自传

一九九五年初，友人虞有澄寄了一本他写的、但尚未出版的《我看英代爾》给我，并嘱我为他作序。我问他要他说：两千字左右吧。

我花了一整天的时间读《我看英代爾》，读後百感交集。一九九五年正是英代爾"如日中天"的年代。有澄很早就加入英代爾，所以他讲的英代爾故事的前一段正是我在德州仪器竭力与英代爾竞争的时候（见第九章）。当时胜负未分，但我离开德仪後，英代爾领先了。当年这段"德仪（TI）对英代爾（Intel）"的故事，无论是公司对公司对国家，在历史上不断出现，也带来不少可歌可泣的事迹。

我读了虞著《我看英代爾》後，又花了一整天时间为有澄写序。几十年来的中文"长篇"，写起来还算顺手。

想不到这篇序竟成了我成为作者的"进身阶"。虞书出版後几个月，天下文化创办人高希均教授就来看我，劝我写自传。其实我认识高教授已有好几年，但他大概一直以为我一身洋气，不可能写中文，直到看了我爲虞写的序後，才决定"试"一下。我贸然答应了。

接下来是几乎两年的前熬。回忆是享受，动笔却是煎熬。但那时我只六十几岁，体力旺盛，有时感情来了，边写边泣，可以写到深夜二、三时，第二天照样上班。终于在九七年年底，《张忠谋自传：上册——一九三一一一九六四》完成。九八年三月出版。

书发表会里，有人问高希均创办人，对我的"中文打几分"？高教授耸从他的肩膀，不经意地说："七十分左右。"

虽然高教授只打七十分，"自传上册"倒很受欢迎。它是二十几年来台湾最畅销书之一，直至今日，我还不时地拿两、三万元的版税。美国文豪海明威对想做作家青年的忠告："简单的字句，真实的感情"，似乎很有道理。

<p class="en-para">## Writing a Foreword and Writing an Autobiography</p>
<p class="en-para">In early 1995, my friend You Yicheng sent me a pre-publication copy of his book "My View of Intel" and asked me to write a foreword. I asked him how long it should be. He said: "About two thousand words."</p>
<p class="en-para">I spent an entire day reading "My View of Intel," and after reading it, a hundred feelings welled up. 1995 was the heyday of Intel. You Yicheng had joined Intel very early, so the earlier part of the Intel story he told was precisely the time when I was fiercely competing with Intel at Texas Instruments (see Chapter 9). At that time, the winner had not yet been decided, but after I left TI, Intel took the lead. This backthen story of "TI vs. Intel," whether company-to-country or country-to-country, has appeared repeatedly in history and given rise to many epic and moving stories.</p>
<p class="en-para">After reading You's "My View of Intel," I spent another entire day writing the foreword for him. It was a rare "long" Chinese piece for me in decades, but it flowed smoothly enough.</p>
<p class="en-para">I never expected this foreword would become my "stepping stone" to becoming an author. A few months after You's book was published, Mr. Commonwealth Publishing founder Professor Kao Hsi-chun came to see me and urged me to write an autobiography. Actually, I had known Professor Kao for several years, but he probably always thought I was too "Westernized" to write in Chinese. Only after reading the foreword I wrote for You did he decide to "test" me. I rashly agreed.</p>
<p class="en-para">What followed was nearly two years of suffering. Reminiscing was enjoyable, but the actual writing was torment. But at that time I was only in my early sixties, with abundant energy. Sometimes when emotions came over me, I would write through tears until 2 or 3 in the morning, then go to work as usual the next day. Finally, at the end of 1997, "The Autobiography of Morris Chang: Volume 1—1931-1964" was completed. It was published in March 1998.</p>
<p class="en-para">At the book launch, someone asked founder Professor Kao what "score" he would give my Chinese. Professor Kao shrugged his shoulders nonchalantly and said: "About 70 points."</p>
<p class="en-para">Although Professor Kao only gave it 70 points, "Autobiography Volume 1" turned out to be very popular. It was one of Taiwan's best-selling books for over 20 years. To this day, I still occasionally receive twenty to thirty thousand NT dollars in royalties. The American literary giant Ernest Hemingway's advice to aspiring writers—"simple words, true feelings"—seems to make a lot of sense.</p>
