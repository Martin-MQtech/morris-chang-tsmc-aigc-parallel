# 台积电篇一·第十六章｜与命运的约会

## 倡议台积电

「我与命运有一个约会！」（I have a rendezvous with destiny!）这句话，现在不流行了，但在上世纪中期，却常从美国人——尤其年轻美国人口中听到。

在我一生中，也有两次对自己说这话。第一次是一九五八年，我自熟悉的「文化城」波士顿，开四天车到陌生的「牛仔乡」达拉斯，向德州仪器公司报到；第二次是一九八五年，我决定离开已经安身立命的美国，来到人地文化生疏的台湾。

两次与命运赴约都做对了。第一次去德仪赴约，德仪改变了我前半生；第二次去台湾赴约，台湾改变了我后半生。只是，我起初以为台湾的约会地点是工研院，后来命运告诉我我错了——约会地点是台积电。

工研院院长交接典礼的第二天（一九八五年八月二十一日），方前院长就拿了一张纸，对我交代「要办事项」；第一件显然是方前院长最担心的。那就是：三家华裔美商创办的IC设计公司——国善、茂矽、及华智，各要求政府出资为每家建晶圆厂。那时建一座晶圆厂至少需要几千万美元，三座就要一亿美元以上，怎么办？

对这么无理的要求，我的立刻反应是：拒绝他们不就完了？但这答案似乎不能使方前院长释怀。第二天我就找了胡定华副院长及电子所所长史钦泰，问他们为什么不能干脆拒绝这三家IC设计公司的无理要求？是否还有什么「隐情」？

胡、史说没有「隐情」，但有「忧虑」。政府不是已有十年之久，出巨资给工研院研究IC？政府不是要建立台湾IC工业？现在，现成的IC公司要政府资助他们建厂，政府为什么不帮忙？难道政府「促进工业技术发展」的雄心壮志，只限于让工研院研究又研究？

胡与史当然觉得政府不能答应这三家公司建晶圆厂的要求，但也认为不能只说「不」，要给他们一个「交代」。

这一切发生在我出任院长的头几天，而且发生在工研院内经费最大、员工最多、也被认为最成功的研究案上。但是工研院的矛盾已暴露无遗：研究工作可以自以为很成功，但对工业的利益却说不出来。

方贤齐不是唯一担心怎么交代这三家IC设计公司之人。两星期后（九月四日），李国鼎政委要见我。李一开头就提此事。我已有几乎两星期时间想此问题，所以我当时就提「共同晶圆厂」（Common Wafer Fab）观念。李似甚喜我有答案，告诉我他要多召集几个人，一起讨论我的建议。

<p class="en-para"><b>The Founding of TSMC</b></p>
<p class="en-para">"I have a rendezvous with destiny!" This phrase is no longer fashionable, but in the mid-20th century, one often heard it from Americans — especially young ones.</p>
<p class="en-para">In my own life, I have said these words to myself twice. The first time was in 1958, when I drove four days from the familiar "city of culture" Boston to the unfamiliar "cowboy country" Dallas to report to Texas Instruments. The second time was in 1985, when I decided to leave America — where I had already put down roots — for Taiwan, a land whose people and culture were foreign to me.</p>
<p class="en-para">Both rendezvous with destiny turned out right. The first, at TI, changed the first half of my life. The second, in Taiwan, would change the second half. Only, I initially assumed the rendezvous point in Taiwan would be ITRI. Later, destiny told me I was wrong — the rendezvous point was TSMC.</p>
<p class="en-para">The day after the ITRI presidential transition ceremony (August 21, 1985), former President Fang handed me a list of "action items." The first was clearly what worried Fang most: three Chinese-American-founded IC design companies — Mosel, Vitelic, and HMC — each demanded that the government fund a wafer fab for them. At that time, building a single fab cost at least tens of millions of dollars; three fabs would exceed $100 million. What to do?</p>
<p class="en-para">My immediate reaction to such unreasonable demands: Just say no. But this answer didn't seem to put Fang's mind at ease. The next day I called in Vice President Hu Ding-hua and Electronics Director Shi Qin-tai and asked them: Why not simply refuse these three IC design companies' unreasonable demands? Was there some "hidden story"?</p>
<p class="en-para">Hu and Shi said there was no "hidden story," but there were "concerns." Hadn't the government spent heavily for ten years funding ITRI's IC research? Wasn't the government trying to build Taiwan's IC industry? Now, actual IC companies were asking the government to help them build fabs — why wouldn't the government help? Was the government's ambition to "promote industrial technology development" limited to letting ITRI do research and more research?</p>
<p class="en-para">Hu and Shi certainly felt the government couldn't grant these three companies' requests for fabs, but they also felt that simply saying "no" wasn't enough — they needed to give them an "answer."</p>
<p class="en-para">All this happened in my first few days as president, and it concerned ITRI's largest, most-staffed, and supposedly most successful research program. But ITRI's contradiction was now fully exposed: the research could consider itself very successful, yet could not articulate its benefit to industry.</p>
<p class="en-para">Fang Xianqi was not the only one worried about how to handle these three IC design companies. Two weeks later (September 4), Minister K.T. Li asked to see me. Li brought up the matter right away. I had spent nearly two weeks thinking about this problem, so I immediately proposed the "Common Wafer Fab" concept. Li seemed delighted that I had an answer and told me he would convene a larger group to discuss my proposal.</p>

### Cheat Sheet · 商业语汇

1. **"I have a rendezvous with destiny" (与命运有一个约会)**
**出自**：美国前总统罗斯福及上世纪中期流行语；张忠谋两次以此自况——一九五八年赴德仪、一九八五年赴台湾。
**商业造句**：*Chang's "rendezvous with destiny" framing captured the existential weight of his decision to leave America for Taiwan — a leap into the unknown that would reshape the global semiconductor industry.*

2. **Common Wafer Fab (共同晶圆厂)**
**中文解释**：张忠谋最初的构想——由政府出资建一座晶圆厂，供多家IC设计公司共享使用；这个概念后来演化为「专业晶圆代工」模式。
**商业造句**：*The "Common Wafer Fab" concept was the seed from which the dedicated foundry model would grow — a radical reimagining of how semiconductor manufacturing could be organized.*

3. **IC design company (IC设计公司)**
**中文解释**：只从事IC设计、不拥有晶圆厂的公司（即「无厂IC设计公司」）；国善、茂矽、华智是台湾最早的一批IC设计公司。
**商业造句**：*The three IC design companies' request for government-funded fabs was the catalyst that forced Chang to design an entirely new business model for Taiwan's semiconductor industry.*
