## 初步决定办台积电

这样以自己所长决定自己「商业模式」，却留下一个大的问题：「专业晶圆代工」能有多少生意？对这问题，当时我没有确切答案，但有若干线索；这些线索也使我在构想未来新公司的经营策略上，加了几条要点：

我们的客户将会是刚萌芽的IC设计公司；或是自有晶圆厂但不愿多投资的IDM公司；也可能是大规模、有IC设计能力的电脑公司。总之，作为他们的晶圆代工者，我们与客户站在同一阵线上。所以，「不与客户竞争」，以及「客户是我们的伙伴」也成为新公司的座右铭。

我们的长处是成熟技术的IC生产，我们提供给客户的价值是：让客户相信我们比竞争者（那时的竞争者是IDM）可靠。毕竟竞争者只是以代工为副业，而代工是我们的专业。所以，「对客户不轻易承诺，一旦做出承诺，必定不计代价、全力以赴」成了我们的座右铭。

新公司的市场绝大部分会在美国；次之，欧洲及日本；台湾市场相对地相当小。新公司初期依赖台湾市场，但必须急急开发美国市场。我熟悉美国市场，但我的本职是工研院院长，而且当时是忙碌于改革的工研院院长。所以，新公司的总经理应该是一个有能力为新公司开发美国市场之人——看起来应该有美国半导体公司CEO经验。

李政委在九月七日（星期六）告诉我要我在九月十日下午对俞院长报告「共同晶圆厂」案。次日为星期日，我不想惊动胡定华副院长及史钦泰电子所所长，但星期一早，我就到办公室召集胡、史一起准备对俞院长的报告。忙了一天多，许多投影片还是我的秘书手写的。这份投影片报告现在已在台积电创新馆长期展览。

我以严肃但坦荡的心情面对俞院长及出席的政府首长——政委、经济部部长李达海、经建会主委赵耀东、财政部次长何显重、国科会主委陈履安、行政院秘书长王章清及院、部、会等幕僚共二十余人。我严肃，因为如何继续政府已努力十年的IC计划是一个重要课题；我坦荡，因为我认为我个人利益不牵涉在这课题里，况且我到台湾还不到三星期，此时此刻要对行政院院长报告一个可能新公司的建议，是突如其来的要求。

我知道与会者除李政委及陈主委外，都不太懂IC，即使李、陈基本也是外行，所以尽量「深入浅出」，报告要点摘要如下：

- IC对信息社会及经济的重要性；美、欧、日、韩纷纷巨额投入，世界IC业剧烈竞争状态。
- 台湾自一九七五年开始研究IC，此举很有先见，但IC技术发展甚快，十年来工研院的IC技术非但没有与先进国家缩短距离，反而更落后。
- 台湾IC公司（包括联电、华智、茂矽、国善）均在「临界规模」以下。
- 对台湾而言，IC是一个高代价的长期赌博。是否要进行这个赌博，是一个值得仔细考虑的问题。

如果进行这个赌博，有两条路可走：（一）成立新公司（暂命名为台湾电路制造有限公司），采取「专业晶圆代工」商业模式；（二）增资联电。（一）之优点为「专业代工」，成功机率较大；（二）之优点为可利用现成联电经营团队。

无论（一）或（二），初步营运计划为：（A）租赁工研院「示范线」；（B）开始建新厂，产能每月三万片六吋晶圆；五年内需要投资（包括自有资本及银行贷款）为美元两亿四千三百五十万元。

我讲四十五分钟，接着俞院长要求出席者发表意见约一小时，最后俞院长做结论：「工研院所提成立新公司发展超大型积体电路……其可行性如何？如何筹组？……请李政委约集有关单位研究后再议。」在我提的两个选择中，他已选择了（一）。

<p class="en-para"><b>The Preliminary Decision to Found TSMC</b></p>
<p class="en-para">Designing a business model around our strengths, however, left a big question: How much business would there actually be for a "dedicated foundry"? I had no precise answer, but I had several clues; these clues also led me to add several points to the new company's operating strategy:</p>
<p class="en-para">Our customers would be nascent IC design companies; or IDMs with fabs but unwilling to invest more; or large computer companies with IC design capability. In any case, as their foundry partner, we would stand on the same side as our customers. Hence, "we do not compete with our customers" and "our customers are our partners" would become the new company's mottos.</p>
<p class="en-para">Our strength would be mature-technology IC production. The value we offered customers: making them trust us more than competitors (then, IDMs). After all, competitors treated foundry as a sideline; foundry would be our specialty. So "never make promises lightly — once a promise is made, spare no cost and go all out" became another motto.</p>
<p class="en-para">The new company's market would be overwhelmingly in the U.S., followed by Europe and Japan; the Taiwan market would be relatively tiny. Initially, the new company would depend on Taiwan, but it had to urgently develop the American market. I knew the American market well, but my primary role was ITRI President, and a busy reform-minded one at that. So the new company's General Manager should be someone capable of opening the U.S. market — seemingly, someone with experience as a U.S. semiconductor CEO.</p>
<p class="en-para">On September 7 (Saturday), Minister Li told me to report on the Common Wafer Fab to Premier Yu on the afternoon of the 10th. The next day being Sunday, I didn't want to bother Vice President Hu or Director Shi, but early Monday morning I called Hu and Shi to my office to prepare the briefing. We worked for more than a day; many slides were handwritten by my secretary. That slide presentation is now on permanent display at the TSMC Innovation Museum.</p>
<p class="en-para">I faced Premier Yu and the attending government leaders — the Minister, MOEA Minister Li Da-hai, CEPD Chairman Zhao Yaodong, MOF Vice Minister He Xianzhong, NSC Minister Chen Lian, Secretary-General Wang Zhangqing, and over twenty staff from the Yuan and ministries — with a serious but open heart. I was serious because how to continue the government's decade-long IC effort was a vital question. I was open because I had no personal stake in the matter. Moreover, I had been in Taiwan less than three weeks; being asked to present a proposal for a possible new company to the Premier was a sudden demand.</p>
<p class="en-para">I knew that aside from Minister Li and Minister Chen, most attendees didn't understand ICs. Even Li and Chen were essentially laypeople. So I made it as accessible as possible. The key points:</p>
<p class="en-para">• The importance of ICs to the information society and economy; the U.S., Europe, Japan, and Korea investing heavily; intense global competition.</p>
<p class="en-para">• Taiwan had started IC research in 1975, showing foresight, but IC technology had advanced so rapidly that ITRI's technology had not merely failed to close the gap — it had fallen further behind.</p>
<p class="en-para">• Taiwan's IC companies (including UMC, Vitelic, Mosel, and HMC) were all below "critical mass."</p>
<p class="en-para">• For Taiwan, IC was a high-stakes, long-term gamble. Whether to take this gamble deserved careful consideration.</p>
<p class="en-para">If taking the gamble, there were two paths: (1) establish a new company (tentatively named Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company), adopting the "dedicated foundry" business model; (2) increase capital for UMC. Path (1)'s advantage: dedicated foundry, higher probability of success. Path (2)'s advantage: leverage UMC's existing management team.</p>
<p class="en-para">Either path: (A) lease ITRI's "demonstration line"; (B) begin building a new fab with monthly capacity of 30,000 6-inch wafers; requiring total investment (equity plus bank loans) of $243.5 million USD over five years.</p>
<p class="en-para">I spoke for 45 minutes. Premier Yu then solicited opinions from attendees for about an hour. Finally, Premier Yu concluded: "Regarding ITRI's proposal to establish a new company for VLSI development... What is its feasibility? How to organize it?... I ask Minister Li to convene relevant units for further study." Of the two options I had presented, he had already selected option (1).</p>

### Cheat Sheet · 商业语汇

1. **"We do not compete with our customers" (不与客户竞争)**
**中文解释**：台积电最核心的经营原则——只代不设计与客户竞争；这一原则是台积电能够取得IC设计公司信任的基础。
**商业造句**：*The "we do not compete with our customers" principle was the foundational covenant that made the foundry model viable — without it, no fabless company would trust TSMC with its designs.*

2. **Critical mass (临界规模)**
**中文解释**：指企业达到能自我维持、具备竞争力的最小规模；张忠谋认为当时台湾所有IC公司都未达临界规模。
**商业造句**：*Chang's assessment that all Taiwanese IC companies were below "critical mass" was the brutal arithmetic that justified creating an entirely new entity rather than backing any existing player.*

3. **High-stakes, long-term gamble (高代价的长期赌博)**
**中文解释**：张忠谋对IC产业投资本质的形容——投入极大、回收极长、不确定性极高；这也是政府决策者必须面对的现实。
**商业造句**：*Framing the IC industry as a "high-stakes, long-term gamble" was Chang's way of forcing policymakers to confront the magnitude of the decision — this was not a modest investment with predictable returns.*
