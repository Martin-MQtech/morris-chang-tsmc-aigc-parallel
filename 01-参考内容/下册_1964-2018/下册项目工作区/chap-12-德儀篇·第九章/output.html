张忠谋

德仪篇·第九章｜半导体集团总经理的奋斗及挣扎

一九六八年，在我任德仪集成电路总经理时，创业投资人阿瑟·洛克（Arthur Rock）邀请诺伊斯（Robert Noyce）和摩尔（Gordon Moore）创办英特尔。这是当年科技界的大新闻。

我自五〇年代后期就认识诺伊斯和摩尔，那时我每年都参加十月在华盛顿召开的「国际电子元件会议」（International Electron Devices Meeting, IEDM），诺伊斯与摩尔都在快捷半导体任职，诺伊斯是总经理，摩尔是他的研发副总，他们两人每年轮流参加此会，我与他们每次在会议中见面都有愉快的交谈，有时还与别的会友一起出去喝酒、吃饭、在深秋夜晚唱歌回旅馆。那时我们都年轻，二十几岁，自以为已投入了一个前途无限的行业，自己也变成前途无限了；年轻人在他们的憧憬和希望里，享受无穷的乐趣。

ReadShift 双语翻译

In 1968, when I was General Manager of TI's Integrated Circuits Division, venture capitalist Arthur Rock invited Robert Noyce and Gordon Moore to found Intel. This was major news in the technology world that year.

I had known Noyce and Moore since the late 1950s. Back then, I attended the International Electron Devices Meeting (IEDM) held every October in Washington, D.C. Noyce and Moore were both at Fairchild Semiconductor — Noyce as General Manager, Moore as his VP of R&D. They alternated attending the meeting each year, and whenever we met, we had delightful conversations. Sometimes we would go out for drinks and dinner with other conference friends and sing our way back to the hotel on late autumn nights. We were all young then, in our late twenties, convinced we had entered a limitless industry and that we ourselves had limitless futures. Young people revel in their dreams and hopes, enjoying boundless joy.

Cheat Sheet · 商业语汇
Integrated Circuits Division (集成电路部门)—集成电路（IC）部门是德仪的核心业务单位，负责设计、制造和销售集成电路芯片；在半导体的商业版图中，「部门」是战略执行的基本单元——它的总经理既是技术决策者，也是利润责任人。
Venture capitalist (创业投资人)—创业投资人是向新创公司提供资金以换取股权的投资者；阿瑟·洛克是矽谷早期最重要的创投家之一——他先后投资了英特尔、苹果、科学数据系统（SDS）等，被称为「矽谷创投之父」。

摩尔长我两岁半，诺伊斯长我三岁半，我们几乎同时进入半导体业。他们的学历远胜过当时的我——诺伊斯是麻省理工的物理博士，在博士论文上就已研究半导体物理；摩尔是加州理工学院博士。他们两位都在得了博士学位后才进入半导体业；而我进入半导体业时只有一个与半导体无关的机械工程硕士学位，我的半导体知识，在初期完全靠自修，直到进入半导体业六年后才有机会到斯坦福读半导体博士。

在快捷，诺伊斯和摩尔就企图以技术取胜，也常成功：例如诺伊斯本人就是集成电路共同发明人，而快捷的「平面化」技术也是集成电路实际化的主要原因。快捷吸引了不少技术人才，包括诺伊斯和摩尔的「叛逆八人」（指他们背叛萧克利离开萧克利实验室）就有七人有技术专长，后来快捷又雇了刚从匈牙利逃难到美国读博士的安迪·葛洛夫（Andy Grove）。

诺伊斯和摩尔在快捷任职十年，为快捷打造出一个像模像样的半导体部门，但他们自己并没有致富，而且他们认为半导体部门赚的钱，都被总公司拿去，不让他们全力发展半导体部门。就在这时间点，创投家洛克邀请诺伊斯和摩尔创办自己的公司。洛克的诱饵是新公司的股票。洛克先问诺伊斯和摩尔：「你们每人有多少流动财产（不要计入你们房子、汽车的价值）？告诉我。」诺伊斯和摩尔的流动财产都是二十几万美元，洛克要了他们每人二十四万五千美元，卖给他们每人12.25%英特尔股票。

Moore was two and a half years older than me, Noyce three and a half years older. We entered the semiconductor industry at almost the same time. Their academic credentials far surpassed mine at the time — Noyce held a Ph.D. in physics from MIT, and his doctoral thesis had already explored semiconductor physics; Moore held a Ph.D. from Caltech. Both had entered the semiconductor industry only after earning their doctorates. When I entered the industry, I held only a master's degree in mechanical engineering, unrelated to semiconductors. My semiconductor knowledge was entirely self-taught in the early years, and I did not have the opportunity to pursue a semiconductor Ph.D. at Stanford until six years into my career.

At Fairchild, Noyce and Moore sought to win through technology, and they often succeeded: Noyce himself was co-inventor of the integrated circuit, and Fairchild's "planar process" was a principal reason the IC became practical. Fairchild attracted many technical talents — among the "Traitorous Eight" (those who defected from Shockley to leave Shockley Labs), seven had technical specialties. Later, Fairchild also hired Andy Grove, a recent Hungarian refugee then pursuing his Ph.D. at an American university.

Noyce and Moore spent ten years at Fairchild, building a respectable semiconductor division, but they did not grow wealthy themselves. They believed the semiconductor division's profits were siphoned off by corporate headquarters, preventing them from fully developing the semiconductor business. At that precise moment, venture capitalist Rock invited Noyce and Moore to start their own company. Rock's bait was equity in the new company. He first asked Noyce and Moore: "How much liquid assets do each of you have (not counting the value of your houses or cars)? Tell me." Both had liquid assets in the low hundreds of thousands. Rock took $245,000 from each of them and sold each a 12.25% stake in Intel.

Cheat Sheet · 商业语汇
"Traitorous Eight" (叛逆八人)—指一九五七年离开萧克利半导体实验室、共同创办快捷半导体的八位工程师；他们是矽谷创业文化的奠基者——没有「叛逆八人」，就没有快捷，也就没有后来的英特尔和整个半导体产业生态。
Equity as bait (股票诱饵)—指创投家用新创公司的股权作为吸引创业者的核心激励手段；在尚未上市的公司中，股票所代表的是「未知价值」——这种不确定性恰恰是创业经济最强大的驱动力。

这是五十几年前的事。现在诺伊斯早已过世，但摩尔健在。二〇一八年《福布斯》杂志的财富调查：摩尔的财富八十五亿美元，起源就是那12.25%英特尔股票。

诺伊斯、摩尔创办英特尔后，雇进的第一号员工就是在快捷追随摩尔的安迪·葛洛夫。以后的三十几年，诺伊斯、摩尔和葛洛夫相继为英特尔的总裁和董事长。这三人组合是一个黄金组合，三人均有杰出的技术背景，诺伊斯又是一个富有魅力的领导者，也是精明的商人；摩尔有敏锐观察力（摩尔定律就是他观察力的明证），也是脚踏实地的领导者。葛洛夫与摩尔共事最久，与摩尔有互补功能。摩尔比较温和，而葛洛夫是严格的纪律执行者。

我接掌德仪半导体集团时（一九七二年），英特尔已是强劲的德仪竞争对手。以后六年（一九七二至一九七八年），我任半导体集团总经理的主要竞争对手，就是英特尔。

ReadShift 双语翻译

This was over fifty years ago. Noyce has long since passed away, but Moore was still alive at the time of writing. Forbes' 2018 wealth survey estimated Moore's fortune at $8.5 billion — originating from that 12.25% Intel stake.

After Noyce and Moore founded Intel, the first employee they hired was Andy Grove, who had followed Moore at Fairchild. For the next thirty-plus years, Noyce, Moore, and Grove successively served as Intel's President and Chairman. This trio was a golden combination — all three had outstanding technical backgrounds. Noyce was also a charismatic leader and shrewd businessman. Moore had keen observational powers (Moore's Law being the clearest evidence) and was a down-to-earth leader. Grove had worked with Moore the longest and served as his complement: Moore was relatively gentle, while Grove was a strict enforcer of discipline.

When I took over TI's Semiconductor Group in 1972, Intel was already a formidable competitor. For the next six years (1972–1978), Intel would be my primary rival as Semiconductor Group GM.

德仪的人才问题

面对以技术取胜的强劲竞争对手，我最大的挑战就是要找技术人才。现在先回忆一下当时（一九七二年）德仪的人才情况。当我初加入德仪时（一九五八年），德仪员工几乎都是德州人，我和基比是极少数的「外省人」（我来自麻省，基比来自俄克拉荷马州），我们也因此建立了一层「外省情谊」；在接受外省、甚至外国人这点上，德仪在一九五八至一九七二这十几年内，有很大的进步，德仪已是世界级的公司，吸引全世界的人才。

一九七二年，德仪半导体绝大部分员工都在达拉斯、休士顿两城居住。这两个城市都相当适宜技术人才居住，也不缺基层技术人才的来源——德州大学奥斯汀分校是相当优秀的学校，每年供应足够学士和硕士毕业生。德仪缺的是高阶研发人才。这个缺口有它的历史渊源：如第六章所述，一直到一九六八年，MOS还不被德仪重视，德仪重起MOS炉灶，又被那一年全部MOS研发人员出走打乱，此后比较容易被计算器IC吸引，而忽略了MOS，直到一九七一年，MOS集成电路还只做到记忆体上。

Facing a formidable competitor that won through technology, my biggest challenge was recruiting technical talent. Let me recall the talent situation at the time (1972). When I first joined TI in 1958, nearly all TI employees were Texans. Kilby and I were among the few "out-of-staters" (I was from Massachusetts, Kilby from Oklahoma), and we forged an "out-of-state bond" because of it. In terms of accepting out-of-staters and even foreigners, TI had made enormous progress over the next fourteen years. TI had become a world-class company attracting talent from around the globe.

In 1972, the vast majority of TI Semiconductor employees lived in Dallas and Houston. Both cities were quite suitable for technical talent and had no shortage of entry-level technical talent — the University of Texas at Austin was an excellent school supplying ample bachelor's and master's graduates each year. What TI lacked was high-level R&D talent. This gap had its historical roots: as described in Chapter 6, MOS was not valued at TI until 1968. TI had to rebuild its MOS effort from scratch, only to have the entire MOS R&D team quit that year. After that, the relatively easier calculator IC work drew attention while MOS was neglected. By 1971, MOS integrated circuits were still only being applied to memory.

Cheat Sheet · 商业语汇
Golden combination (黄金组合)—指三位创始人（诺伊斯、摩尔、葛洛夫）在技术远见、商业敏锐和组织纪律上的完美互补；在创业研究中，「三人黄金组合」是英特尔早期成功的关键——它证明了一个好的创始团队需要的不只是技术，还需要领导力和执行纪律的平衡。
High-level R&D talent gap (高阶研发人才缺口)—指企业在高端技术人才供给上的结构性短缺；在半导体产业中，「高阶研发人才」的争夺往往决定技术路线的成败——谁拥有最好的MOS工程师，谁就拥有下一代产品的入场券。

英特尔倒是一心一意地发展MOS。他们开发MOS技术，更重要的，他们在一九七一年发明了微处理器，实现了「以技术取胜」的策略。

当然，在我领导下的德仪半导体集团要急起直追了，但在我接任初期，我就发现在吸引人才上，有两个挥之不去的困难：

第一，英特尔是新创、尚未上市的公司，它可以给予优秀人才不少股票或股票承购权（两位创办人每人获得12.25%股权就是一例），而这些股票或承购权往往被视为奇珍异宝，因为它们含的是未知价值（上市后的股价）。相反地，德仪是上市已久的成熟公司，股价波动不大，而且只有最高层或非常杰出的人员才获得承购权。

第二，英特尔地处硅谷，很容易从竞争者那里吸引人才。那时流行的一句话：「假使你在硅谷换公司，不必迁居，只要每天早上朝另一方向开车就可以了」；还有，硅谷流动率高，如果你觉得换错公司了，尽可以再换一个公司。相反地，德仪独处德州，要一个人才带家带眷搬到德州，这人得做一个相当的决定。尤其在一九七〇年代，夫妇两人同时工作已是常态，要说服一个人到德仪来，还要说服他（或她）的配偶；更何况，虽然达拉斯和休士顿是很宜居家的城市，但在气候、文化、子女教育上，许多有抱负的人还是更喜欢硅谷。

ReadShift 双语翻译

Intel, by contrast, pursued MOS single-handedly. They developed MOS technology, and more importantly, they invented the microprocessor in 1971, executing their "win through technology" strategy.

Of course, under my leadership, TI's Semiconductor Group had to play catch-up. But early in my tenure, I discovered two persistent difficulties in attracting talent:

First, Intel was a private, pre-IPO startup. It could offer talented people substantial stock or stock options (the founders each receiving a 12.25% stake being a prime example), and these shares or options were often treasured as precious gems because they embodied unknown value (the post-IPO stock price). Conversely, TI was a long-public, mature company with a relatively stable stock price, and options were reserved only for the highest-level executives or truly exceptional individuals.

Second, Intel was located in Silicon Valley and could easily poach talent from competitors. A popular saying at the time: "If you change companies in Silicon Valley, you don't have to move — just drive in the opposite direction every morning." Moreover, Silicon Valley had high turnover: if you felt you had joined the wrong company, you could always switch again. Conversely, TI was isolated in Texas. Asking a talented person to uproot their family and move to Texas required a major decision. In the 1970s especially, dual-career couples were already the norm. You had to persuade not only the individual but also their spouse. Furthermore, although Dallas and Houston were very livable cities, many ambitious people still preferred Silicon Valley for climate, culture, and children's education.

Cheat Sheet · 商业语汇
Stock options as recruiting leverage (股票承购权作为人才杠杆)—指新创公司以股权作为吸引高阶人才的核心武器；在上市前的私人公司中，期权的价值来源于「未来溢价」——这种激励机制是德仪等成熟上市公司无法复制的。
Geographic isolation of talent hubs (人才聚集地的地理隔离)—指企业的地理位置对人才吸引力的重大影响；在矽谷，「不需要搬家就能换工作」是一个戏剧化的产业聚落效应——它让雇主之间的挖角成本接近于零。

总之，在吸引高阶研发人才上，我任半导体总经理初期（一九七二至七四年）就有这两点挥之不去的困难，但我还能增加研发经费，还能继续找人；到了后期（一九七五至七七年），德仪消费者产品集团开始经营困难，公司赚钱的责任几乎都落在半导体集团身上，我增加研发经费的要求，屡被夏伯特、彪希驳回，我连雇新人的本钱都没有了！

但毕竟初期雇进来的新人，奠定了德仪此后二十余年在记忆体的地位。

「记忆体」的奋斗

「记忆体」在资讯世界用途极广、市场极大，而七〇年代初期MOS技术的突飞猛进，正是最有利于记忆体的发展。我自一九七一年接掌MOS单位，七二年又接掌全部半导体业务后，一直把「深入记忆体市场」作为首要任务。

七〇年代初期，英特尔的1K记忆体是市场热门货，我在「自传上册」也曾提到，德仪电脑部门为了要英特尔的1K记忆体，托我找英特尔，我打电话给英特尔董事长诺伊斯，结果与他做了一个交易：他供应了记忆体给德仪电脑部门，而我供应了矽原料给英特尔。

不但英特尔，一九六九年背叛德仪的工程师（见本书第六章）组成的Mostek公司，也在生产1K记忆体，与英特尔一起享受这大好的市场。德仪把大部分精力放在计算器IC上，做不出1K记忆体。

一九七一、七二年之际，我雇了不少新人，新人中包括几位有经验的「高手」，我把他们都放在记忆体上，与他们讨论后我做了一个重要的策略决定：放弃1K，全力开发下一代记忆体：4K！

ReadShift 双语翻译

In short, in attracting high-level R&D talent, I faced these two persistent difficulties in my early years as Semiconductor GM (1972–1974). But I was still able to increase R&D spending and continue hiring. By the later period (1975–1977), TI's Consumer Products Group had begun struggling operationally, and the responsibility for keeping the company profitable fell almost entirely on the Semiconductor Group. My requests to increase R&D spending were repeatedly rejected by Shepherd and Shep — I didn't even have the budget to hire new people!

But the new hires from the early period did lay the foundation for TI's two-decade dominance in memory.

The Memory Battle

Memory had the broadest applications and largest market in the information world. The rapid advancement of MOS technology in the early 1970s was precisely the most favorable condition for memory development. Ever since I took over the MOS unit in 1971 and then the entire semiconductor business in 1972, I had made "penetrating the memory market" a top priority.

In the early 1970s, Intel's 1K memory was a hot commodity. I mentioned in Volume 1 that TI's Computer Department, desperate for Intel's 1K memory, asked me to approach Intel. I called Intel Chairman Noyce and we struck a trade: he would supply memory to TI's Computer Department, and I would supply silicon raw materials to Intel.

Not only Intel — the engineers who had defected from TI in 1969 (see Chapter 6) had formed Mostek, which was also producing 1K memory, enjoying the booming market alongside Intel. TI had devoted most of its energy to calculator ICs and couldn't produce 1K memory.

Around 1971–72, I hired a number of new people, including several experienced "aces." I assigned them all to memory development, and after discussions with them, I made a critical strategic decision: skip 1K entirely and focus all efforts on the next generation — 4K!

Cheat Sheet · 商业语汇
Memory (记忆体)—记忆体（Memory）是半导体市场中用量最大的品类之一，用于存储数据和程序；在商业战略中，记忆体战役是七〇至八〇年代最激烈的竞争战场——谁率先推出新一代记忆体，谁就拥有定价权和市场占有率。
Strategic leapfrogging (策略性跳过一代)—指企业在技术路线选择上跳过竞争者已占据的当季产品，直接投入下一代的研发；这种高风险的「跳过策略」需要精确的时机和充足的工程能力——一旦成功，可以获得巨大竞争优势；一旦失败，则可能两头落空。

当时，的确有一番到底下一代应该是2K或4K的讨论。但我选择了比较大担的4K，我认为：我们的新一代记忆体应该把竞争者甩得远一点。这个我在德仪的决定，倒是为世界记忆体业界开了一个先例：以后每一代都比前一代多四倍记忆比特（bits）。

4K计划的主导工程师是华裔美籍Clinton Kuo。他的团队不负大家所望，两年后（一九七四年）就把4K开发出来。我们立刻开始量产。一九七四至七六这三年，是德仪在记忆体上非常光辉的年代——德仪在4K的市占率超过50%。但是我们没有把竞争者甩得很远，Mostek比德仪稍晚推出了他们的4K。他们的4K是另一个「技术标准」，而且性能较德仪稍优。

一九七四年4K研发团队成功地完成了他们的开发，而且交棒给生产部门后，我立刻派他们开发16K。同时，我也组新团队，开发别的记忆体（如SRAM、EPROM等），它们市场较小，但也有一定的策略价值。

I firmly opposed TI entering the calculator market. But Shep supported it, and so did Shepherd. In 1972, almost simultaneously with my taking the Semiconductor Group GM post, TI announced its entry into consumer products — with calculators as the vanguard.

On the allocation of calculator ICs, the conflict between Shep and me sharpened further. TI's Semiconductor Group had limited IC capacity. The internal Consumer Products Group demanded priority supply, while external customers were also clamoring for product. Shep, in his capacity as President, sent me a direct email ordering me to prioritize internal customers.

I read that email and actually shredded it — not out of emotion, but after careful deliberation: "I cannot leave written evidence ordering me to prioritize internal customers, because doing so would harm the interests of external customers, and external customers are the foundation of TI's long-term semiconductor development."

Shep probably learned about the shredded email. From then on, his attitude toward me shifted from supportive to reserved.

微处理器的多头马车

七〇年代中期，半导体业出现了另一个划时代的产品——微处理器（Microprocessor）。英特尔在一九七一年发明了微处理器，但直到七〇年代中期，它的应用潜力才真正显现。

德仪也必须发展微处理器。但与英特尔不同，德仪的微处理器面临一个独特的难题：多头马车。

英特尔的微处理器完全对外销售。它的唯一使命就是满足外部客户。德仪的微处理器却要同时满足多个内部客户：国防系统要用、电脑部门要用、消费者产品部门要用。每个内部客户都有不同的需求，不同的性能规格，不同的交货时程。我们像一个马同时被几辆马车拖着跑——每一辆都往不同的方向出力。

更糟的是，内部客户优先的思维让德仪的微处理器团队在满足外部客户时总是慢半拍。外部客户需要快速交货、标准规格，但我们的团队首先得应付内部那些不断修改规格的需求。

英特尔微处理器和软体——微软的作业系统，也成为此后个人电脑的「技术标准」；英特尔（和微软）的命运也随波而起。相形之下，德仪渐渐失色。

德仪微处理器不是有内部客户吗？他们到哪里去了？的确，国防系统用了少量；电脑部门决定不用半导体部门的微处理器；倒是消费者产品部门决定用半导体部门的微处理器来开发「家庭电脑」。

「家庭电脑」在一九七九年问世，起初定价太高，销路不佳，搞新设计；一九八一年再问世，这次定价奇低，销路很好，但却是卖一个亏一个；一九八三年竞争加剧，价格更低，亏本更严重，撑到一九八四年，家庭电脑的亏损已经到了要拖垮全公司地步。终于，夏伯特和彪希决定完全退出家庭电脑市场。

没有外部客户、只有内部家庭电脑这一个大客户的德仪微处理器，也与德仪家庭电脑同归于尽。

The Microprocessor's Multi-Head Horse

By the mid-1970s, the semiconductor industry witnessed another epochal product — the microprocessor. Intel invented the microprocessor in 1971, but it wasn't until the mid-1970s that its application potential truly emerged.

TI, too, had to develop microprocessors. But unlike Intel, TI's microprocessor faced a unique dilemma: the multi-head horse.

Intel's microprocessors were sold entirely externally. Its sole mission was to serve outside customers. TI's microprocessors, however, had to simultaneously serve multiple internal customers: defense systems needed them, the computer department needed them, the consumer products department needed them. Each internal customer had different requirements, different performance specifications, different delivery schedules. We were like a single horse being pulled by several carriages — each pulling in a different direction.

Worse, the internal-customer-first mindset caused TI's microprocessor team to always lag when serving external customers. External customers needed rapid delivery and standard specifications, but our team first had to deal with internal demands that kept changing specifications.

Intel's microprocessors and Microsoft's operating system became the "technical standard" for personal computers. Intel's (and Microsoft's) fortunes soared. By contrast, TI gradually faded.

Didn't TI's microprocessors have internal customers? Where did they go? Indeed, defense systems used a small quantity; the computer department decided not to use the Semiconductor Division's microprocessors. It was the Consumer Products Department that decided to use them to develop "home computers."

The "home computer" launched in 1979. The initial price was too high; sales were poor. A new design followed. It re-launched in 1981, this time at an amazingly low price. Sales were strong — but every unit sold lost money. By 1983, competition intensified, prices dropped further, losses mounted. In 1984, home computer losses reached the point of threatening to drag down the entire company. Finally, Shepherd and Shep decided to exit the home computer market entirely.

TI's microprocessors, which had no external customers and only the internal home computer as a major customer, perished alongside TI's home computers.

The Lubbock Fab

Compounding TI's geographic disadvantage in recruiting talent and its disadvantage of lacking stock options, Shep made a mid-1970s decision: he ordered the Semiconductor Group to build an advanced-technology wafer fab in Lubbock.

Lubbock is a small city in western Texas, about 500 kilometers from Dallas (population roughly 150,000 in 1975). It had direct flights to Dallas, about an hour's flight, so transportation was reasonably convenient — but it was certainly not within Dallas's "daily living circle." For technical talent accustomed to living in major cities (Dallas, Houston, Silicon Valley), Lubbock was absolutely a backwater they had no desire to live in.

So why did Shep want to build a fab there?

It turned out Shep had been born to a cotton-farming family in the countryside not far from Lubbock. To the young Shep, Lubbock had been a metropolis. He later attended "Texas Tech" in Lubbock, earning both his bachelor's and master's degrees there. Naturally, he had deep emotional ties to Lubbock.

Adolescent admiration for a place often becomes a lifelong cherished memory. Just as I had admired Hong Kong as a "paradise" in elementary school and Shanghai as the "Paris of the East" in high school — though I later visited places far more beautiful and serene than Hong Kong, far more prosperous than Shanghai — whenever I think of the Hong Kong and Shanghai of my youth, I still feel a fond nostalgia.

However, I never asked TI to build a plant in Shanghai or Hong Kong.

Shep was different. When he became Executive Vice President (1972, the same year I took the Semiconductor GM role), he began building in Lubbock and ordered the newly formed Consumer Products Group to relocate there, making Lubbock their headquarters. Several years later, Shep ordered the Semiconductor Group to build a high-tech MOS fab in Lubbock.

The Self-Development Equipment Myth

Shepherd and Shep made another decision that deeply troubled me: they ordered the Semiconductor Division to develop and manufacture its own production equipment as much as possible.

Self-development of production equipment was not without reason. TI practiced the "synergy" principle. Shepherd ordered defense systems, computers, consumer products and other divisions to use the Semiconductor Division's ICs, while simultaneously ordering the Semiconductor Division to develop and manufacture everything from silicon raw materials to IC production equipment.

In the 1950s and 1960s, TI indeed successfully "made its own" IC testing instruments and automatic bonding machines. The engineers who developed these instruments or machines became company heroes.

The problem lay precisely in these 1950s–60s heroes of the company. They had aged from their youth into middle age, but still wanted to continue making contributions. Their specialty — production equipment mechanical engineering — had been overtaken by outside equipment suppliers. Moreover, outside suppliers served the entire semiconductor industry — a large market — while TI's production equipment division had only one customer: TI's Semiconductor Division.

Shep's Absurdity and Shepherd's Bewilderment
