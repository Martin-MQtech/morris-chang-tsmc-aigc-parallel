台积电篇二第二十二章

张忠谋

台积电篇二·第二十二章｜经营权之战落幕，在台湾、美国上市

拖延战术

在第十九章《筚路蓝缕》内，我已叙述在一九八九年底台积电业绩初露曙光时，飞利浦就提出增加股权至51%要求。我也叙述我认此为要求为台积电的"经营权之战"——也是我事业人生的生死战。如果飞利浦成功增股至51%，我很难想像他们会让我继续充分自主地经营台积电；我将面临十年内继德仪、通用、工研院后的第四次失败；我的唯一的未来，恐怕是回美国创业、或教书。即使原始股东的股权会因为飞利浦经营下的台积电而增值不少，但他们的前途也会在失去专业经理人的带领下而比较没有保障。

其实对我而言，台积电"经营权之战"赢了，我的事业前途才有希望。为了抵抗飞利浦增加股权至51%要求，我的初期战术是拖延——拖延战术。在投资协定中，飞利浦的承购价格以三个因素"等量考虑"决定。这三因素中，第二："转让时之公司获利能力及获利预测"及第三："在美国证券市场上市之美国半导体公司之市价/净值比"都有相当主观性，因此可以各有各的看法。果然，在一九八九至一九九零年飞利浦与开发基金一共七次的会议中，飞利浦提出的承购价格总只是十几元，甚至二十元，而开发基金提出的都是七、八元。这样大的差距无法妥协，双方就停止开会了一年。

## Delay Tactics

In Chapter 19, "Trailblazing," I described how, at year-end 1989 when TSMC's performance first showed promise, Philips demanded an increase in its stake to 51%. I also described how I considered this demand as TSMC's "battle for control"—and a battle for my own professional life and death. If Philips succeeded in increasing its stake to 51%, I could hardly imagine they would let me continue running TSMC with full autonomy. I would face my fourth career failure in a decade—after TI, GE, and ITRI. My only future would probably be returning to the U.S. to start a business or teach. Even though the original shareholders' equity would appreciate considerably under Philips-managed TSMC, their prospects would also be less secure with the loss of professional management.

In fact, for me, only by winning TSMC's "battle for control" would my professional future have hope. To resist Philips' demand to increase equity to 51%, my initial tactic was delay. Under the investment agreement, Philips' purchase price was determined by three factors given "equal consideration." Among these three factors, the second, "the company's earning power and profit forecast at transfer" and the third, "the price-to-book ratio of U.S. semiconductor companies listed on the U.S. stock market," both had considerable room for subjective judgment, so each side could hold its own view. Sure enough, in seven meetings between Philips and the Development Fund during 1989-1990, Philips always proposed a purchase price of only NT$10-20, while the Development Fund proposed NT$7-8. Such a large gap could not be bridged, and both sides stopped meeting for a year.

上市准备

在协商僵持的两年中，台积电的业绩已有飞跃式的成长——营业净利自八九年的四亿五千万元跃至九一年的十二亿四千万元。同时，我也开始做上市准备。九一年四月，我透过开发基金的推荐，打了一通电话给当时大华证券的总经理张孝威，告诉他想委托大华证券担任我们上市承销商，并请他到我办公室商议。

张孝威来了，我把台积电的股东投资协议给他看。这上面有谁派几名董事，谁派董事长，谁派总经理，财务副总等等……孝威说："让我回去研究一下，再跟你回复。"

一个月后，孝威又来了。这次他带了一张皱皱的便条，上面有十几项"必须改"，例如：谁都不能派董事长、总经理，谁都不能派董或监察人……更重要的，不能有一个超过半数股权的股东。

孝威告诉我的，其实是：必须废止当年为了台积电募资时吸引飞利浦投资的"股东协议书"，不然，不要想上市。

During the two years of negotiation deadlock, TSMC's performance had grown by leaps and bounds—net operating profit jumped from NT$450 million in 1989 to NT$1.24 billion in 1991. Meanwhile, I began preparing for an IPO. In April 1991, through a Development Fund referral, I made a phone call to Zhang Xiaowei, then general manager of Grand Cathay Securities, telling him we wanted to engage Grand Cathay as our IPO underwriter and inviting him to my office to discuss.

Zhang Xiaowei came. I showed him TSMC's shareholders' investment agreement. It specified who appointed how many directors, who appointed the chairman, who appointed the general manager, who appointed the financial vice president, and so on. Xiaowei said: "Let me go back and study this, and I'll get back to you."

A month later, Xiaowei came back. This time he brought a crumpled note listing over a dozen "must change" items, e.g.: no one can appoint the chairman or general manager; no one can appoint directors or supervisors. . . . More importantly: there cannot be a shareholder holding more than 50% of equity.

What Xiaowei was really telling me was: the "Shareholders Agreement" that had been crafted to attract Philips' investment during TSMC's fundraising must be annulled—otherwise, don't even think about an IPO.

致函飞利浦

一九九一年九月，开发基金及飞利浦的"承购权"谈判已僵持了一年余，我也自大华证券了解：如台积电要上市，最大股东的股权必须限制在一半以下。我想：台积电向上市迈进一大步的时机已到。我就致函飞利浦的台湾总经理罗益强。罗氏同时亦为台积电董事（飞利浦法人代表）。在此函内，我告诉他若台积电股票上市，原始股东可能有好几倍的投资回收，但飞利浦的股权必须限制在40%以内。

这"40%"的数字是我选的，我认为40%既符合上市规定，又是飞利浦可以接受的。罗益强请示飞利荷兰总部，荷兰总部原则上接受降低承购股权至40%，但双方还是需要同意一个"合理"的承购价格。

鸭子几乎已经煮熟，我想，最后一步还是让开发基金来走吧。开发基金当时归财政部管，主任委员职亦为时任财政部的部长王建煊兼任。我就在九一年十月走访王部长，请教如何化解飞利浦对承购价的僵局。王部长果然提出"锦囊妙计"：成立两个独立的"TSMC股价评审小组"，每组七位委员，均应是台湾社会贤达或金融专家，开发基金及飞利浦各提名约一半。

In September 1991, the "subscription rights" negotiations between the Development Fund and飞利浦 had been deadlocked for over a year. I also learned from Grand Cathay Securities: if TSMC wanted to go public, the largest shareholder's equity must be limited to below 50%. I thought: the time had come for TSMC to take a major step toward listing. I wrote to Luo Yiqiang, the general manager of Philips Taiwan, who was also a TSMC director (Philips' representative). In the letter, I told him that if TSMC stock went public, original shareholders might realize several times their investment return, but Philips' equity would need to be limited to within 40%.

The "40%" figure was my choice. I believed 40% both complied with IPO regulations and was acceptable to Philips. Luo Yiqiang consulted Philips Netherlands headquarters, which in principle accepted reducing the subscription equity to 40%, but both sides still needed to agree on a "reasonable" purchase price.

The duck was almost cooked. I thought: the last step should be left to the Development Fund. At that time, the Development Fund was under the Ministry of Finance, and the chairmanship was held concurrently by the Minister of Finance, Wang Jianxuan. In October 1991, I visited Minister Wang, seeking his advice on breaking the deadlock with Philips over the purchase price. Minister Wang indeed produced a "secret plan": establish two independent "TSMC Stock Price Evaluation Committees" of seven members each, consisting of distinguished members of Taiwanese society or financial experts, with the Development Fund and Philips each nominating about half.

股价评审小组

王部长的建议很快地被飞利浦接受。两个小组也很快地成立。事实上，飞利浦只提名了一位委员：曹岳维。曹君是当时中欧贸易促进会理事长。开发基金和我很快地把两个小组组成，每组召集人及委员如下：

第一组——曹岳维（召集人）、施振荣、黄言章、马秀如、李麟、江万龄、徐小波第二组——江万龄（召集人）、黄茂雄、徐小波、张春雄、杜荣、王建煊（后由他人代）

台积电提供两小组需要的财务及财务预测资料，我也指定林坤禧副总为台积电联络人。自九一年十一月至九二年四月，这两小组经过多次讨论，各自达到结论。第二小组先在九二年四月二十一日提报告，建议承购股价20.44元；第一小组在四月二十八日提报告，建议承购股价17.84至19.18元。两小组的建议股价相当接近，那段时期台积电的"未上市股票参考价"也在十七至十八元左右。

在成立两个"TSMC股价评审小组"前，开发基金及飞利浦均同意：小组建议不具约束力，只提供双方参考。所以要完成承购仍要开发基金和飞利浦正式协商；这在九二年八月发生，最后协商价为21.79元，飞利浦增加股权至40%，但更重要的是同意废止一九八六年的"股东协议书"。

至此台积电上市的路已铺好了！但最后还有一步路要走。台积电在九二年十月三十日开始"公开发行"。根据证交法规定，"公开发行"两年后方可上市。

Minister Wang's suggestion was quickly accepted by Philips. The two committees were also quickly formed. In fact, Philips nominated only one member: Cao Yuewei, who was then chairman of the Association for the Promotion of Trade with Central and Eastern Europe. The Development Fund and I quickly formed the two committees, each with the following convenor and members:

Committee 1—Cao Yuewei (Convenor), Shi Zhengrong, Huang Yanzhang, Ma Xiuru, Lin Li, Jiang Wanling, Xu Xiaobo Committee 2—Jiang Wanling (Convenor), Huang Maoxiong, Xu Xiaobo, Zhang Chunxiong, Du Rong, Wang Jianxuan (later replaced)

TSMC provided both committees with needed financial and financial forecast data. I also designated Vice President Lin Kunxi as TSMC's contact person. From November 1991 to April 1992, after extensive discussion, each committee reached its conclusion. The second committee submitted its report on April 21, 1992, recommending a purchase price of NT$20.44. The first committee submitted its report on April 28, 1992, recommending a purchase price of NT$17.84-19.18. The two committees' recommended prices were quite close, and during this period TSMC's "unlisted stock reference price" was also around NT$17-18.

Before the two "TSMC Stock Price Evaluation Committees" were established, the Development Fund and Philips had both agreed: the committee recommendations were non-binding, for reference only. So to complete the subscription, the Development Fund and Philips still needed to negotiate formally. This occurred in August 1992. The final negotiated price was NT$21.79, Philips increased its equity to 40%, but more importantly, both parties agreed to annul the 1986 "Shareholders Agreement."

Now the road to TSMC's listing was paved! But there was still one more step. TSMC began its "public offering" on October 30, 1992. Under securities regulations, listing could occur two years after the "public offering."

台湾上市

小插曲不妨在此一提。根据一九八六年的"股东协议书"，飞利浦要求承购时，任何原始股东均可把拥有的股票卖给飞利浦；如无人要卖，开发基金就是"最后必须卖方"。九二年十月，当一切都谈妥后，王部长以公文征询所有原始股东，是否愿以21.79元股价转让给飞利浦？结果竟是无一股东愿意。在股价已涨了一倍多后，他们此刻不愿出售股票的意志，与六年前他们不愿意投资台积电的意志一样坚强。

我不知现在台湾公司上市是否还是很难？但一九九四年台积电绝对是"民"恳求"官"。台积电在二月二十八日提出上市申请，证交所人员于三月十六、十七两天来公司查核，并且面试我。不少质问：当年为什幺要政府（开发基金）出资成立台积电？为什幺要飞利浦投资，让"肥水"外流？为什幺租工研院的实验工厂？他们对当时台积电在极少人看好的情况下募资（一九八五至八六年）的困难，几乎一无所知——或者，装成一无所知？被面试的我，感觉像是个罪犯。真的，在台湾做事竟是这幺难！

面试后被嘱咐写一篇《台积公司成立沿革》。在我的工作日程上，我在三月二十三日记着：整日写台积成立沿革。

当然，证交所的质问，至少还有俞国华、李国鼎可以为我作证。但是，九四年是李登辉任总统的第七年，他的权力基础已很稳固。相对地，俞、李这两位"老臣"已释权多年，我也不想再去麻烦他们援助。

幸而这次倒也不需要他们的援救。《台积公司成立沿革》缴卷后，证交所似已满意。六月二十四日，证交所通过我们的上市案。九月五日，台积电以90元股价上市，但却是"有价无市"，此后六交易日，每天都"无量涨停"。直至九月十二日，股价涨到142.5元才开始有交易。对原始股东而言，如果他们在九四年九月十二日卖出，投资回收是14倍。

An interesting aside: Under the 1986 "Shareholders Agreement," when Philips demanded subscription, any original shareholder could sell their shares to Philips; if no one wanted to sell, the Development Fund was the "last must-sell party." In October 1992, after everything was agreed, Minister Wang sent an official letter to all original shareholders asking whether they were willing to transfer shares to Philips at the NT$21.79 price. The result: not a single shareholder was willing. After the stock price had more than doubled, their resolve not to sell at this moment was as strong as their resolve not to invest in TSMC six years earlier.

I don't know if it's still very difficult for Taiwanese companies to go public, but in 1994, TSMC was definitely the "private sector" begging the "government." TSMC submitted its IPO application on February 28. SEC investigators came to the company on March 16-17 for audits and interviewed me. Many questions: Why did we need the government (Development Fund) to fund TSMC's establishment? Why let Philips invest, allowing "fertile water" to flow to outsiders? Why rent ITRI's experimental factory? They knew almost nothing about the difficulties of fundraising for TSMC when very few believed in it (1985-86)—or perhaps they were pretending not to know? Being interviewed, I felt like a criminal. Really, doing something in Taiwan was that hard!

After the interview, I was instructed to write a piece on "The Founding History of TSMC." In my work diary, on March 23 I noted: "Spent the whole day write TSMC founding history."

Of course, for the SEC's questioning, at least Yu Kuo-hua and Li Guoding could testify on my behalf. But 1994 was the seventh year of Lee Teng-hui's presidency, and his power base was already quite solid. Relatively speaking, Yu and Li, these two "senior ministers," had been out of power for years, and I didn't want to trouble them for their assistance.

Fortunately, this time their rescue wasn't needed. After submitting "The Founding History of TSMC," the SEC seemed satisfied. On June 24, the SEC approved our listing. On September 5, TSMC listed at an IPO price of NT$90, but it was "priced with no market"—for the next six trading days, it hit the daily price limit up with "no volume" every day. It wasn't until September 12, when the price rose to NT$142.50, that trading began. For original shareholders, if they sold on September 12, 1994, their investment return was 14x. Of course, better—and much better—returns lay ahead.

美国ADR上市

台积电在台湾上市的证券商是大华证券，但在正式上市时，原来对我做建议的张孝威总经理已离职，由宋学仁接替。台积电上市后宋也离开大华而加入美商高盛银行（Goldman Sachs）。宋学仁虽然在大华只待了短时期，但在这短时期内刚好碰到台积电在台湾上市。他很看好的前景，况且他初入高盛，还不是"Partner"，正想快快立功升级为"合伙人"。如能说服台积电在美国上市，将是不小的功劳。

有此背景，宋学仁在我们已通过台湾上市、而尚未承销之际，就开始游说我以ADR（American Depositary Receipt）方式在美国上市。对此我起初很冷淡；我刚经历在台湾上市，已对上市前的准备工作感觉疲倦；况且，在美国上市又有什幺好处？

后来的几个月，终于打动了我。他的理由是：飞利浦与开发基金都想出脱台积电股票，而台湾股票市场交易量低；如飞利浦或开发基金大量释股，将遭遇"愈卖愈贱"的恶运。相对的，美国市场远较活泼。所以，在美国上市可视为让飞利浦与开发基金卖股票的一个好渠道。

在飞利浦同意降低承购权至40%，并废止"投资协议"时，我已认定飞利浦已放弃原先要控制及经营台积电的意向，而转为要赚钱。但是，假如他们因为卖股不易，仍长期继续保持相当高的股权，那他们的股权仍会像"达摩克利斯之剑"一般，悬挂在我头上。何不给他们一条快速售股的捷径？

Grand Cathay Securities was the underwriter for TSMC's Taiwan listing, but by the time of the formal listing, general manager Zhang Xiaowei, who had originally advised me, had left and was replaced by Song Xueren. After TSMC's listing, Song also left Grand Cathay to join the American investment bank Goldman Sachs. Although Song Xueren was only at Grand Cathay for a brief period, it happened to coincide with TSMC's Taiwan listing. He was very bullish on TSMC's prospects. Moreover, having just joined Goldman Sachs, he was not yet a "Partner" and was eager to achieve merit and get promoted to "partner" quickly. Persuading TSMC to list in the U.S. would be no small accomplishment.

Against this background, while we had already passed Taiwan listing but not yet been underwritten, Song Xueren began lobbying me to list in the U.S. via ADR (American Depositary Receipt). I was initially quite cool to this. I had just gone through the Taiwan IPO and felt exhausted by the preparatory work; moreover, what was the benefit of listing in the U.S.?

In the following months, he finally moved me. His reasoning was: both the Development Fund and Philips wanted to sell TSMC stock, but the Taiwan stock market had low trading volume. If飞利浦 or the Development Fund sold large quantities, they would face the nightmare of "the more you sell, the cheaper it gets." The U.S. market was far more active. Therefore, listing in the U.S. could be seen as a good channel for飞利浦 and the Development Fund to sell their shares.

By the time Philips agreed to reduce its subscription rights to 40% and annul the "Investment Agreement," I had already concluded that Philips had abandoned its original intention to control and operate TSMC and switched to wanting to make money. But if they continued to hold a rather high equity stake long-term because selling shares was difficult, their equity would still hang over my head like the "Sword of Damocles." Why not give them a fast track to sell shares?

高盛长久合作

我对"在美国上市"的看法转为积极后，就与宋学仁说我要与高盛高层建立直接关系；我心里想的不只是这次在美国上市，而是在长久的未来，如果台积能如我那时已期许地成功，就的确需要一家关系密切的世界级投资银行，而高盛恰可充当这角色。宋学仁很配合，把高盛关键人物，包括最高层，都介绍给我。此后几十年，高盛几乎是台积电的唯一投资银行，而且把这工作做得很好。

台积电ADR于一九九七年十月八日在纽约证券交易所以代号TSM上市。台积电每一单位ADR代表台湾的五股。ADR交易远较台股交易活泼。以目前情形来说，ADR大约占全部股权20%，平均每天交易量为一千万ADR，相当于五千万台股；台股占全部股权80%，平均每天交易量只是三千万股；所以ADR交易之活泼度，是台股的六倍多！

我的主要目的——让飞利浦尽快释股，以消灭挂在经营者头上的"达摩克利斯之剑"——也逐渐实现。ADR上市前，飞利浦尚拥有31%股权；八年后（二零零五年）已减至16.4%。二零零八年八月全部出清。

After my view of "listing in the U.S." turned positive, I told Song Xueren that I wanted to establish direct relationships with Goldman Sachs senior management. What I had in mind was not just this U.S. listing, but for the long-term future: if TSMC could succeed as I already envisioned at that time, we would indeed need a close relationship with a world-class investment bank—and Goldman Sachs could precisely play that role. Song Xueren was very cooperative and introduced me to Goldman Sachs key figures, including the highest levels. For decades thereafter, Goldman Sachs has been virtually TSMC's sole investment banking partner, and they did the job very well.

TSMC's ADR was listed on the New York Stock Exchange on October 8, 1997 under the ticker TSM. Each TSMC ADR represents five shares traded in Taiwan. ADR trading is far more active than Taiwan share trading. As of now, ADRs account for about 20% of total equity, with average daily trading volume of 10 million ADRs (equivalent to 50 million Taiwan shares). Taiwan shares account for 80% of total equity, with average daily trading volume of only 30 million shares. So the activity level of ADR trading is more than six times that of Taiwan shares!

My primary goal—allowing飞利浦 to quickly release shares, to eliminate the "Sword of Damocles" hanging over management—was gradually achieved. Before the ADR listing,飞利浦 still owned 31% equity; eight years later (2005), it had reduced to 16.4%. In August 2008, it fully exited.

投资回收与反思

飞利浦在台积电投资上赚了多少钱？我们估计了一下：它的投资是三十亿元，总回收（包括股利及出售股票获利）是四千零三十八亿元——二十一年内一百三十五倍回收！非常好的回收！但是他们原来要经营台积电，要把台积电拥为己有的野心却落空了。他们本以为台积电只是一个台湾性、台湾级的公司，而飞利浦却是世界级的公司。他们在台积电成立四、五年后才发现：这家公司会比他们自己更"世界级"。

开发基金呢？他们一直遵守了"不干涉经营"的原则，但他们卖台积电股票的力道，比飞利浦还大。他们在飞利浦执行承购权后，仍拥有30.8%台积电，但在之后的六年内，就在台湾市场卖得只剩17.4%。ADR上市后，他们更是痛快地卖，卖到二零零五年九月，只剩6.4%。

开发基金赚了多少钱？它的投资是二十二亿元。出售股票收入及截至二零二二年一月底股利收入为两千七百六十七亿元。现在尚握有的6.4%，如以目前股价（约六百三十元）计，约值一兆零四百一十八亿元。也就是说，如果现在出清台积电股票，他们的总投资回收约六百倍。

当然，真正的回收还不能用金钱算。今日台积电的技术和产品，已渗入世界几十亿人民的工作和生活，以及几十万公司、组织的作业中。如果台积电忽然消失，世界几十亿人民的生活肯定会受影响。当年李国鼎、俞国华要成立台积电，为的是要发展台湾半导体工业，至于投资回收，我想他们的目标有限——只要小赚就不对了。

我必须承认，没有政府的投资和协助，我办不起来台积电。在一片"市场经济"呼声中，有些事情还是要由政府来做的。

How much did Philips make on its TSMC investment? We estimated: its investment was NT$3 billion, total recovery (including dividends and stock sale profits) was NT$403.8 billion—a 135x return in 21 years! An excellent return! But their ambition to operate TSMC and claim it as their own was defeated. They originally thought TSMC would be a Taiwan-level company, while Philips was a world-class company. Only four or five years after TSMC's founding did they realize: this company would be even more "world-class" than they were themselves.

The Development Fund? They consistently adhered to the principle of "non-interference in operations," but their selling of TSMC stock was even more aggressive than Philips. After Philips executed its subscription rights, the Development Fund still held 30.8% of TSMC, but within the next six years they sold down to 17.4% on the Taiwan market. After the ADR listing, they sold even more vigorously, down to 6.4% by September 2005.

How much did the Development Fund earn? Its investment was NT$2.2 billion. Stock sale proceeds plus dividend income through end of January 2022 totaled NT$276.7 billion. The 6.4% still held, at the current stock price (about NT$630), would be valued at approximately NT$1.0418 trillion. In other words, if they liquidated their TSMC stock now, their total investment return would be about 600x.

Of course, the true return cannot even be measured in money. Today TSMC's technology and products have permeated the work and lives of billions of people around the world, as well as the operations of hundreds of thousands of companies and organizations. If TSMC suddenly disappeared, the lives of billions of people would certainly be affected. Back then Li Guoding and Yu Kuo-hua wanted to establish TSMC to develop Taiwan's semiconductor industry. As for investment return, I think their goals were limited—just a small profit would have been fine.

I must admit: without the government's investment and assistance, I could not have started TSMC. Amidst the chorus calls for "market economy," there are still things that need to be done by the government.
