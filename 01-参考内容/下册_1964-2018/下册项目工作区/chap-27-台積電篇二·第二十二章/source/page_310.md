## 上市准备

在协商僵持的两年中，台积电的业绩已有飞跃式的成长——营业净利自八九年的四亿五千万元跃至九一年的十二亿四千万元。同时，我也开始做上市准备。九一年四月，我透过开发基金的推荐，打了一通电话给当时大华证券的总经理张孝威，告诉他想委托大华证券担任我们上市承销商，并请他到我办公室商议。

张孝威来了，我把台积电的股东投资协议给他看。这上面有谁派几名董事，谁派董事长，谁派总经理，财务副总等等……孝威说："让我回去研究一下，再跟你回复。"

一个月后，孝威又来了。这次他带了一张皱皱的便条，上面有十几项"必须改"，例如：谁都不能派董事长、总经理，谁都不能派董或监察人……更重要的，不能有一个超过半数股权的股东。

孝威告诉我的，其实是：必须废止当年为了台积电募资时吸引飞利浦投资的"股东协议书"，不然，不要想上市。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">During the two years of negotiation deadlock, TSMC's performance had grown by leaps and bounds—net operating profit jumped from NT$450 million in 1989 to NT$1.24 billion in 1991. Meanwhile, I began preparing for an IPO. In April 1991, through a Development Fund referral, I made a phone call to Zhang Xiaowei, then general manager of Grand Cathay Securities, telling him we wanted to engage Grand Cathay as our IPO underwriter and inviting him to my office to discuss.</p>
<p class="en-para">Zhang Xiaowei came. I showed him TSMC's shareholders' investment agreement. It specified who appointed how many directors, who appointed the chairman, who appointed the general manager, who appointed the financial vice president, and so on. Xiaowei said: "Let me go back and study this, and I'll get back to you."</p>
<p class="en-para">A month later, Xiaowei came back. This time he brought a crumpled note listing over a dozen "must change" items, e.g.: no one can appoint the chairman or general manager; no one can appoint directors or supervisors. . . . More importantly: there cannot be a shareholder holding more than 50% of equity.</p>
<p class="en-para">What Xiaowei was really telling me was: the "Shareholders Agreement" that had been crafted to attract Philips' investment during TSMC's fundraising must be annulled—otherwise, don't even think about an IPO.</p>
</div>

## 致函飞利浦

一九九一年九月，开发基金及飞利浦的"承购权"谈判已僵持了一年余，我也自大华证券了解：如台积电要上市，最大股东的股权必须限制在一半以下。我想：台积电向上市迈进一大步的时机已到。我就致函飞利浦的台湾总经理罗益强。罗氏同时亦为台积电董事（飞利浦法人代表）。在此函内，我告诉他若台积电股票上市，原始股东可能有好几倍的投资回收，但飞利浦的股权必须限制在40%以内。

这"40%"的数字是我选的，我认为40%既符合上市规定，又是飞利浦可以接受的。罗益强请示飞利荷兰总部，荷兰总部原则上接受降低承购股权至40%，但双方还是需要同意一个"合理"的承购价格。

鸭子几乎已经煮熟，我想，最后一步还是让开发基金来走吧。开发基金当时归财政部管，主任委员职亦为时任财政部的部长王建煊兼任。我就在九一年十月走访王部长，请教如何化解飞利浦对承购价的僵局。王部长果然提出"锦囊妙计"：成立两个独立的"TSMC股价评审小组"，每组七位委员，均应是台湾社会贤达或金融专家，开发基金及飞利浦各提名约一半。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">In September 1991, the "subscription rights" negotiations between the Development Fund and飞利浦 had been deadlocked for over a year. I also learned from Grand Cathay Securities: if TSMC wanted to go public, the largest shareholder's equity must be limited to below 50%. I thought: the time had come for TSMC to take a major step toward listing. I wrote to Luo Yiqiang, the general manager of Philips Taiwan, who was also a TSMC director (Philips' representative). In the letter, I told him that if TSMC stock went public, original shareholders might realize several times their investment return, but Philips' equity would need to be limited to within 40%.</p>
<p class="en-para">The "40%" figure was my choice. I believed 40% both complied with IPO regulations and was acceptable to Philips. Luo Yiqiang consulted Philips Netherlands headquarters, which in principle accepted reducing the subscription equity to 40%, but both sides still needed to agree on a "reasonable" purchase price.</p>
<p class="en-para">The duck was almost cooked. I thought: the last step should be left to the Development Fund. At that time, the Development Fund was under the Ministry of Finance, and the chairmanship was held concurrently by the Minister of Finance, Wang Jianxuan. In October 1991, I visited Minister Wang, seeking his advice on breaking the deadlock with Philips over the purchase price. Minister Wang indeed produced a "secret plan": establish two independent "TSMC Stock Price Evaluation Committees" of seven members each, consisting of distinguished members of Taiwanese society or financial experts, with the Development Fund and Philips each nominating about half.</p>
</div>
