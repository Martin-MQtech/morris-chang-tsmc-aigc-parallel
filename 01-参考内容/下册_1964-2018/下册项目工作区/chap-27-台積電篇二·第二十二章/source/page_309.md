# 台积电篇二·第二十二章｜经营权之战落幕，在台湾、美国上市

## 拖延战术

在第十九章《筚路蓝缕》内，我已叙述在一九八九年底台积电业绩初露曙光时，飞利浦就提出增加股权至51%要求。我也叙述我认此为要求为台积电的"经营权之战"——也是我事业人生的生死战。如果飞利浦成功增股至51%，我很难想像他们会让我继续充分自主地经营台积电；我将面临十年内继德仪、通用、工研院后的第四次失败；我的唯一的未来，恐怕是回美国创业、或教书。即使原始股东的股权会因为飞利浦经营下的台积电而增值不少，但他们的前途也会在失去专业经理人的带领下而比较没有保障。

其实对我而言，台积电"经营权之战"赢了，我的事业前途才有希望。为了抵抗飞利浦增加股权至51%要求，我的初期战术是拖延——拖延战术。在投资协定中，飞利浦的承购价格以三个因素"等量考虑"决定。这三因素中，第二："转让时之公司获利能力及获利预测"及第三："在美国证券市场上市之美国半导体公司之市价/净值比"都有相当主观性，因此可以各有各的看法。果然，在一九八九至一九九零年飞利浦与开发基金一共七次的会议中，飞利浦提出的承购价格总只是十几元，甚至二十元，而开发基金提出的都是七、八元。这样大的差距无法妥协，双方就停止开会了一年。

<p class="en-para">## Delay Tactics</p>
<p class="en-para">In Chapter 19, "Trailblazing," I described how, at year-end 1989 when TSMC's performance first showed promise, Philips demanded an increase in its stake to 51%. I also described how I considered this demand as TSMC's "battle for control"—and a battle for my own professional life and death. If Philips succeeded in increasing its stake to 51%, I could hardly imagine they would let me continue running TSMC with full autonomy. I would face my fourth career failure in a decade—after TI, GE, and ITRI. My only future would probably be returning to the U.S. to start a business or teach. Even though the original shareholders' equity would appreciate considerably under Philips-managed TSMC, their prospects would also be less secure with the loss of professional management.</p>
<p class="en-para">In fact, for me, only by winning TSMC's "battle for control" would my professional future have hope. To resist Philips' demand to increase equity to 51%, my initial tactic was delay. Under the investment agreement, Philips' purchase price was determined by three factors given "equal consideration." Among these three factors, the second, "the company's earning power and profit forecast at transfer" and the third, "the price-to-book ratio of U.S. semiconductor companies listed on the U.S. stock market," both had considerable room for subjective judgment, so each side could hold its own view. Sure enough, in seven meetings between Philips and the Development Fund during 1989-1990, Philips always proposed a purchase price of only NT$10-20, while the Development Fund proposed NT$7-8. Such a large gap could not be bridged, and both sides stopped meeting for a year.</p>
