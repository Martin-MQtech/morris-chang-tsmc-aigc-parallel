<p class="en-para">In July 2013, just as we were preparing to begin mass production of twenty nanometer, Apple unexpectedly issued a bid request for sixteen nanometer. We submitted a bid. Apple's response was that it had decided to purchase sixteen-nanometer wafers from Samsung first.</p>
<p class="en-para">I grew anxious! We had invested so much manpower, capital, and resources in Apple over the preceding years. Were we to do only one node, only one or two years of business?</p>
<p class="en-para">I immediately sent Jeff an e-mail: 'To manufacture twenty nanometer for Apple, we invested over ten billion dollars in fabs and equipment. Most of those fabs and equipment can also be used for sixteen nanometer. Now you do not want our sixteen-nanometer wafers. What do you expect us to do?'</p>
<p class="en-para">Jeff called back to put my mind at ease. On August 27, 2013, he came to Hsinchu in person to explain: Samsung could supply sixteen nanometer immediately (they did not need to take the detour through twenty nanometer first), so Apple chose to buy from them. But once TSMC could supply sixteen nanometer smoothly, Apple would switch to TSMC.</p>
<p class="en-para">The outcome proved him correct. Moreover, TSMC began a new practice from this experience: developing both the next half-node and the full next node simultaneously. The steadily increasing R&amp;D budget and the growing R&amp;D team each year were of great help.</p>
