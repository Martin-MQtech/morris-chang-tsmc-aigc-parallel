<p class="en-para">Just as Apple and TSMC were communicating in both directions with growing intensity, suddenly on March 3, 2011, I received a call from Jeff Williams. 'Intel's top management has approached Tim Cook, Apple's president, and Tim has agreed. So I need to pause our negotiations for two months.'</p>
<p class="en-para">Although the request came suddenly, Intel in 2011 no longer possessed the formidable prestige it had commanded in the late twentieth century, when its name alone inspired respect. After hanging up, I reflected on a comparison between us and Intel. I believed that our technology was not inferior to Intel's—in 2011 I held that view; in the years that followed I came to believe we surpassed Intel. In manufacturing cost and customer service, we outperformed Intel. Apple had no reason to abandon TSMC for Intel.</p>
<p class="en-para">Confident as I was, I thought it not amiss to express our concern to Apple. During my trip to Silicon Valley in April, I asked to see Jeff on April 5. Jeff called back: he would not be in the office that day, but President Tim Cook was, and he had arranged for me to meet with him.</p>
<p class="en-para">On April 5 I went to Apple's headquarters and had lunch with Tim. He received me very warmly. We first went to the Apple cafeteria to get some food, then returned to his office to eat. From the start Tim told me not to worry. He did not mention Intel's technology or manufacturing cost. He simply said, in a detached tone, 'They are not good at contract manufacturing.'</p>
<p class="en-para">On May 5, exactly two months after Jeff's 'pause negotiation' call, Jeff phoned: 'Resuming negotiations.'</p>
