<p class="en-para">What about the price per wafer? After extensive discussions with Apple on specifications and careful cost and pricing calculations by TSMC's cost and pricing teams, we felt well prepared. On September 14, 2011, Jeff came to Hsinchu in person, and the price was settled that very day. That evening Jeff and I dined at a three-Michelin-star restaurant. Jeff joked, 'Ah, the price is set. If we had not agreed on a price, I suppose you would have taken us to McDonald's.'</p>
<p class="en-para">From a manufacturing and financial standpoint, the preparations above could be regarded as quite adequate. What about the technology side?</p>
<p class="en-para">In November 2010 Apple told us that it wanted twenty nanometer in 2014. We were already trial-producing twenty nanometer, but in my judgment twenty nanometer was merely a half-node—the true next node was sixteen nanometer. Pursuing twenty nanometer would be a detour. But Apple had asked, and I had no choice but to develop twenty nanometer for Apple first.</p>
<p class="en-para">The twenty-nanometer development was not entirely smooth. Between 2012 and 2013, I tried a rotating system among three co-chief operating officers—Sandi Jin, Tim Liu, and Morris Wei—who would take charge of the company's three major business divisions: R&amp;D, manufacturing, and marketing and business development. But whenever problems arose in the twenty-nanometer development, I would immediately recall Sandi, who was enjoying his new assignment, back to R&amp;D. That completely disrupted the originally designed rotation system.</p>
<p class="en-para">Although not entirely smooth, twenty nanometer was developed to Apple's specification. But the R&amp;D for sixteen nanometer was delayed.</p>
