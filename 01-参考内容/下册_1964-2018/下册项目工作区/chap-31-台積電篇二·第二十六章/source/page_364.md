二月十九日，我自硅谷打电话告知飞利浦布鲁克即将离去、我拟自兼总经理的消息。飞利浦稍表不安。我也知道他们为何不安——他们看到的台积电十年历史中，有六年是布鲁克当总经理，而我从来没有自己做过总经理。这就是为什么飞利浦在半导体业一直平庸，他们只看表面，没有真正了解为什么台积电这么成功。虽然他们表示不安，但我坚持要自兼总经理，他们也就接受了。
<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">On February 19, I called Philips from Silicon Valley to inform them that Brook was about to leave and that I intended to serve as general manager myself. Philips expressed some unease. I understood why—they had seen six of TSMC's ten years with Brook as general manager, while I had never personally held that role. This was precisely why Philips had remained mediocre in the semiconductor industry: they only observed the surface and did not truly understand what made TSMC successful. Although they expressed concern, I insisted on serving as general manager, and they ultimately accepted.</p>
</div>

四月中旬我们为布鲁克举办了欢送会，但气氛一点都不「欢」。欢送会上很少笑容。当布鲁克致词时，他凌厉地说：「我会常常到台湾来，我在台湾还有很多投资。」我觉得很奇怪，我不知道他在台湾有很多投资；我也很恼感，六年前我们给他的欢迎会里，他的美国口音跟着大家喊「一百亿」还在我耳中荡漾。今天却是一个截然不同的布鲁克。是台积电改变了他？还是台湾改变了他？还是他本来就是一个多变的人？
<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">In mid-April, we held a farewell gathering for Brook, but the atmosphere was far from cheerful. Smiles were rare. When Brook took the podium, he spoke sharply: "I will come to Taiwan often; I have many investments here." I found this puzzling—I was unaware he held significant investments in Taiwan. I also felt stung, for in the welcome party we had thrown for him six years earlier, his American-accented voice had echoed alongside ours chanting "one hundred billion." Today, he was an entirely different person. Had TSMC changed him? Had Taiwan changed him? Or had he always been a man of shifting loyalties?</p>
</div>

## 每年变动的「十五大」

从本书第三章〈总经理的学习〉，读者也许可以领会：在我三十三岁跃升为德仪业务单位总经理时，我已拥有相当深厚的技术和生产背景。所以我任总经理后的学习重点，起先是营销和市场，两三年后是策略。至今，我仍认为策略、技术、生产、营销和市场，是一个半导体企业总经理必须亲身经历过或至少深度了解的重要领域。事实上，在我五十四年总经理/董事长职业生涯（自一九六四年我任德仪晶体管部总经理算起，至二○一八年自台积电退休）中，在以上每一个「重点领域」内都曾度过不少愉快时光。
<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">Readers may recall from Chapter 3, "The General Manager's Learning," that when I was promoted to general manager of a TI business unit at age thirty-three, I already possessed substantial technical and manufacturing background. My learning focus after becoming general manager was initially marketing and sales, then strategy after two or three years. To this day, I maintain that strategy, technology, production, marketing, and sales are the critical domains a semiconductor general manager must have personally experienced or at least deeply understood. In fact, throughout my fifty-four-year career as general manager and chairman—from my appointment as general manager of TI's transistor division in 1964 to my retirement from TSMC in 2018—I have found genuine pleasure in each of these areas.</p>
</div>

所以在九七年布鲁克「净离」台积电、我自兼总经理时，我几乎有「如鱼得水」的兴奋感。公司的策略除「技术领先」外早已定调，而且执行相当顺利。技术和生产的进展也可圈可点。而且「无晶圆厂设计公司」正如雨后春笋般成立，争取它们成为我们的客户是九○年代的要务——我在这方面应有可为。
<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">So when Brook made his clean break in 1997 and I assumed the role of general manager, I felt an almost effortless sense of being exactly where I belonged. The company's strategy had long been set—technology leadership—and execution was proceeding smoothly. Progress in technology and production was commendable. Moreover, fabless design houses were emerging like mushrooms after rain, and winning them as clients was the imperative of the 1990s—an area where I believed I could make a meaningful contribution.</p>
</div>
