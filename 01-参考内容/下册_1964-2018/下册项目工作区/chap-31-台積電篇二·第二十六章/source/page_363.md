到了九六年底，布鲁克已经做了六年，这次他真的想回美国了。但他仍想留在台积电，做什么呢？起初他认为他可以住在美国，仍做总经理。我认为这是一个不可思议的建议，立刻否决了。经过两三次会议后，我答应让他管美国营销以及WaferTech，直接对我报告——那时WaferTech刚成立不久，大家还对它寄予厚望。布鲁克似乎也接受这个任务，那我们也就有了一个留住布鲁克的方案了。
<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">By the end of 1996, Brook had been with us for six years, and this time he genuinely wanted to return to the United States. Yet he still wished to stay at TSMC—in what capacity? Initially, he proposed living in the U.S. while remaining general manager. I considered this an untenable suggestion and vetoed it immediately. After two or three meetings, I agreed to let him oversee U.S. marketing and WaferTech, reporting directly to me. WaferTech had been established only recently, and everyone still harbored high hopes for it. Brook seemed to accept this assignment, and we appeared to have a plan to retain him.</p>

但是不然。布鲁克把他真正要的东西留到最后：「我要保留我的董事席。」他说。
<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">But that was not how it ended. Brook reserved his true demand for last: "I want to keep my board seat," he said.</p>

这，我不能给他。台积电自始就是一个专业经理人经营的公司，我自己的股权非常微小。在九七年，两位大股东（行政院开发基金及飞利浦）占董事会十席董事的七席，他们同意给一席给我，一席给总经理，还有一席留给其他原始股东的代表。现在布鲁克不做总经理了，我的计划是自兼一阵子后让曾繁城接任总经理。如果让布鲁克保留他的董事席，岂不造成一个奇怪的局面：新的总经理不是董事，而只管美国营销及美国厂的经理反倒是董事？
<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">That, I could not give him. TSMC had always been a professionally managed company, and my own equity stake was minuscule. In 1997, the two largest shareholders—the Administrative Yuan Development Fund and Philips—held seven of ten board seats. They had agreed to allocate one seat to me, one to the general manager, and one to a representative of other founding shareholders. With Brook no longer serving as general manager, my plan was to serve in that capacity temporarily before handing it to Zeng Fan-cheng. If I allowed Brook to retain his board seat, it would create an absurd situation: the new general manager would not be a director, while a manager responsible only for U.S. marketing and the American factory would be?</p>
</div>

我坚决拒绝布鲁克的董事要求。布鲁克静默了一下，冷冷地说：「那我还是要『净离』（clean break）吧。」当时我不太了解「净离」的意思。「离」就是「离」，还有什么「净离」？几个月后，布鲁克加入竞争者，我才了解「净离」深一层的用意：再没有什么牵挂，缘已尽了！
<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">I firmly rejected Brook's request for a board seat. Brook fell silent for a moment, then said coldly, "Then I will make a 'clean break.'" At the time, I did not fully understand the deeper meaning of "clean break." "Break" simply meant leaving—what else could it be? Months later, when Brook joined a competitor, I finally grasped its full implication: no ties remained; the relationship was entirely severed.</p>
</div>

九七年一月三十日，布鲁克告诉我他「净离」的决定，但他要到四月底才走。十天后（二月十日），我就去硅谷访问了九个大客户。对我而言，访问客户是「重拾旧技」——我在德仪事业部总经理任内就喜欢访问客户。这九个客户都很稳定，毕竟我们多年来「争取客户信任」已有相当根基。

<p class="en-para">On January 30, 1997, Brook informed me of his decision to make a clean break, though he would not leave until the end of April. Ten days later, on February 10, I traveled to Silicon Valley to visit nine key customers. For me, visiting customers was a matter of "restoring old skills"—I had enjoyed client visits during my tenure as general manager of a TI business unit. All nine customers were stable, given that we had built considerable trust over many years.</p>
