## 核心团队踟蹰

在一九八五年九月开始构想台積電时，我以为如果办成台積電，一定会有一位工研院高层主管带领一个IC计划核心团队来台積電。后来我开始对胡、史说台積電必须开关美、欧市场，所以第一任总经理应该是美国人；此后我发现胡已无兴趣来台積電，他那时已是交大校长、工研院院长的候选人，已无兴趣担任台積電总经理以下的任何职务；至于史钦泰，我也发现他的兴趣主要还是学术，志愿留在工研院。到台積電即将组成时，看起来，实验工厂厂长曾繁城是唯一可能的核心团队领导人。曾繁城在电子所的职级低所长两级。他的上司是电子所副所长。这位副所长也不愿加入台積電。

但是，在台积电准备开张前两星期，胡定华告知我：连曾繁城也有疑虑，他和核心团队都担心加入台積電后未来的前途。胡定华告诉我："你必须与曾谈。"

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">When I first conceived of TSMC in September 1985, I thought that if TSMC were successfully established, a senior ITRI manager would definitely lead an IC program core team to come to TSMC. Later, I began telling Hu and Shi that TSMC had to open up the U.S. and European markets, so the first general manager should be American. Afterward, I discovered that Hu was no longer interested in coming to TSMC—he was already a candidate for president of National Chiao Tung University and ITRI president, and he was no longer interested in any position below TSMC general manager. As for Shi Qin-tai, I also discovered that his interests were primarily academic; he wished to stay at ITRI. By the time TSMC was about to be formed, it appeared that Zeng Fan-cheng, the experimental factory director, was the only potential core team leader. Zeng Fan-cheng's rank at the Electronics Research Institute was two levels below the institute director. His immediate boss was the deputy institute director. This deputy director was also unwilling to join TSMC.</p>
<p class="en-para">However, two weeks before TSMC was preparing to open, Hu Ding-hua informed me: even Zeng Fan-cheng had doubts. He and the core team all worried about their future prospects after joining TSMC. Hu Ding-hua told me: "You must talk with Zeng."</p>
</div>

## 曾繁城的工会领袖姿态

我与曾谈了。后来我与他相熟后，曾经告诉我他那次他对我要求的姿态，简直是工会领袖对资方谈判的姿态！可怜我其实也是"劳方"，为了要办成台積電，只好充当"资方"，消释曾繁城的疑虑。

曾代表核心团队质疑两大点：第一，台積電如何与韩、日公司竞争？他没提美国公司，因为美国公司比韩、日公司更高一筹。韩、日公司我们已不能应付了。这质疑是我在募资期常要应付的。我重复了一遍我的两个倚靠：第一个倚靠是我们的"专业晶圆代工"商业模式。我们不与美、韩、日半导体公司竞争，它们反而是我们的客户。第二个倚靠是核心团队的高良率能力。对第一个倚靠，曾似乎半信半疑。对第二个倚靠，我的信心似乎高于核心团队对自己的信心。

但是，曾让我的辩论通过了。于是曾繁城提出第二个质疑。在此以前我的以为是：既然工研院以财团法人身份，素享高薪美誉，所以核心团队移到台積電，薪资不改；以后，当然再说。

但是，曾给了我一个比较复杂的解释。他说：台湾公司的习惯除发固定薪资外，每年都会视业绩发红利。当然，有的美国公司也发红利，但对一般员工而言，红利只是员工收入的小部分。照曾繁城那天所言，红利可以是收入的大部分。

我听懂了曾繁城的话后，觉得员工红利也不失为对员工的合理补贴。本来台湾工程师的收入远低于欧、美、日本。现在台積電要在世界竞争，应该也在员工收入上做相当程度的看齐。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">I talked with Zeng. Later, after we became well acquainted, he told me that his posture during that negotiation with me was simply that of a union leader negotiating with management! The pity was that I was actually also "labor." In order to make TSMC happen, I had no choice but to play "management" and dispel Zeng Fan-cheng's doubts.</p>
<p class="en-para">Zeng raised two major points on behalf of the core team. First: How would TSMC compete with Korean and Japanese companies? He didn't mention American companies, because American companies were a notch above Korean and Japanese ones. We could already barely handle Korean and Japanese companies. This was a question I frequently had to address during the fundraising period. I repeated my two pillars of reliance: The first was our "dedicated foundry" business model. We didn't compete with American, Korean, or Japanese semiconductor companies—they would actually be our customers. The second was the core team's high-yield capability. Regarding the first pillar, Zeng seemed half-believing. Regarding the second pillar, my confidence seemed higher than the core team's confidence in themselves.</p>
<p class="en-para">Nevertheless, Zeng let my arguments pass. Then Zeng Fan-cheng raised his second concern. Previously, I had assumed: since ITRI, as a foundation, enjoyed a reputation for high salaries, when the core team transferred to TSMC, their salaries would remain unchanged; and of course, things would be discussed again later.</p>
<p class="en-para">But Zeng gave me a more nuanced explanation. He said: Taiwanese company practice, in addition to paying a fixed salary, was to issue bonuses according to performance every year. Of course, some American companies also gave bonuses, but for ordinary employees, bonuses were only a small part of income. From what Zeng Fan-cheng said that day, it sounded like bonuses could be the majority of income.</p>
<p class="en-para">After I understood what Zeng Fan-cheng was saying, I felt employee bonuses were also a reasonable form of compensation for employees. Originally, Taiwanese engineers' incomes were far lower than those in Europe, America, and Japan. Now TSMC was going to compete globally; we should bring employee compensation to a relatively comparable level.</p>
</div>

## 建立"激励制度"

他回去告诉核心团队，核心团队也满意了。总的来说，我们又有总经理，又有核心团队，可以开张了。大约一年后，台積電发出首次员工红利；以后几十年员工红利极少中断；后来又加上员工认股。

二十九年后，大部分核心团队仍在公司，经历多次灾难，也与我并肩打过多次硬战。他们之中产生了一个副董事长、三个资深副总、五个副总。一点怀疑、一点后悔都没有。

每次我想起核心团队加入台積電前的踟蹰，以及曾繁城那天的工会领袖姿态，总不禁怀念初加入德仪的我（"自传上册"第十四章）——多么年轻！多么有信心！多么兴奋地赴那场"与命运的约会"！多么幼稚！多么天真！当时十七岁的我，一点踟蹰都没有，分核心团队加入时和加入德仪时差不多年轻，但他们的心情竟和我的心情大不一样。时、地、人都变了。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">He went back and told the core team, and the core team was also satisfied. In sum, we now had both a general manager and a core team—we could open for business. About a year later, TSMC issued its first employee bonus. In the decades since, employee bonuses have rarely been interrupted; later, employee stock options were added.</p>
<p class="en-para">Twenty-nine years later, most of the core team was still with the company, having weathered multiple crises and fought many hard battles alongside me. Among them emerged one vice chairman, three senior vice presidents, and five vice presidents. Without a trace of doubt or regret.</p>
<p class="en-para">Whenever I recall the core team's hesitation before joining TSMC, and Zeng Fan-cheng's union leader posture that day, I cannot help but reminisce about myself when I first joined TI ("Autobiography, Volume 1," Chapter 14)—how young! How confident! How excited I was to keep that "rendezvous with destiny"! How naive! How innocent! At seventeen, I had not a trace of hesitation. When portions of the core team joined TSMC, they were about the same age as when I had joined TI, yet their state of mind was so different from mine at that time. Times, places, and people had all changed.</p>
</div>
