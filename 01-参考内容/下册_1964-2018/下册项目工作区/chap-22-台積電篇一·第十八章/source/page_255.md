# 台积电篇一·第十八章｜开辟鸿蒙

## 定位

一九八六至八七年的冬天，台积电即将组成，台湾媒体在一度热烈报导"政府最大投资案"后，忽然平静下来。我也借这由绚烂趋平淡、而真正考验即将来临的过渡时期，对台积电的创始初期应采取的策略，做了一个检讨。

成立台积电的目的，基本是为工研院已进行十年的IC计划及继承IC计划的VLSI计划建立工业出路，所以创始期的核心团队，就是工研院VLSI计划实验工厂的成员。作为一个要能赚钱的半导体公司，他们的强点是：成熟技术高生产良率；他们的弱点是：没有IC设计技术，制程技术落后两、三代，而且实验工厂只有台湾客户，没有国外客户，甚至连与国外客户的接触都没有。

<p class="en-para">## Positioning</p>
<p class="en-para">In the winter of 1986-87, as TSMC was about to be formed, the Taiwanese media—after initially reporting fervently on the "government's largest investment project"—suddenly calmed down. I also took advantage of this transition period, when things were moving from excitement to calm and the real test was yet to come, to review the strategies TSMC should adopt in its early founding period.</p>
<p class="en-para">The purpose of establishing TSMC was fundamentally to create an industrial outlet for ITRI's ten-year IC program and its successor, the VLSI program. Thus, the founding core team consisted of members from the ITRI VLSI program's experimental factory. As a semiconductor company that needed to be profitable, their strengths were: high production yield with mature technology. Their weaknesses were: no IC design technology, process technology lagging two to three generations behind, and the experimental factory only had Taiwanese clients—no foreign clients, not even any contact with foreign clients.</p>

## 从"Foundry"到"专业晶圆代工"

我在第十六章已提及，考虑了工研院VLSI计划的强点及弱点，其实它的工业出路只有一条：舍弃设计，只为客户制造。早在七零年代，当时英特尔创办人之一摩尔已为此"不设计，只制造"事业取了一个名字：foundry。只是，摩尔的foundry只是一般又设计、又制造公司（IDM）的副业。把foundry成为专业，自始自台積電始。更具讽刺性的，当我找英特尔投资台積電时（第十七章），英特尔拒绝了，原因是不认为专业foundry能赚钱。

英文的foundry是一个中性名词，无语意亦无贬意，但中文一下子把foundry译成"晶圆代工"，大概因为台湾非常习惯"代工"。即使加上"专业"两字，也不能消除一般"代工"的低技术、低资本的形象。

其实台積電的"专业晶圆代工"自始就"高资本"，自始也不是"低技术"，因为即使我们创始时制程技术落后尖端技术两至三代，但我们的良率很高，而高良率反映了扎实的工程基础。

虽然我不喜欢中文"代工"附带的"低技术"形象，但"代工"是台湾习惯的名词，也不便改。台積電的定位在我心里倒毫无疑问："Dedicated Foundry"，中文翻译成"专业晶圆代工"。

<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">As mentioned in Chapter 16, considering the strengths and weaknesses of ITRI's VLSI program, there was really only one industrial outlet: abandon design, and manufacture only for customers. As early as the 1970s, Gordon Moore, one of Intel's founders, had already given this "don't design, just manufacture" business a name: foundry. However, Moore's foundry was merely a sideline for a company that both designed and manufactured (IDM). Making foundry a specialty—professional foundry—began with TSMC. More ironically, when I sought Intel's investment in TSMC (Chapter 17), Intel refused, its reason being that it did not believe professional foundry could be profitable.</p>
<p class="en-para">The English word "foundry" is a neutral term, with no semantic nor pejorative connotation. But Chinese immediately translated foundry as "晶圆代工" (wafer foundry), probably because Taiwan was very accustomed to the concept of "代工" (contract manufacturing). Even adding the word "专业" (dedicated/professional) couldn't eliminate the low-technology, low-capital image associated with typical "代工" (contract manufacturing).</p>
<p class="en-para">In fact, TSMC's "dedicated foundry" was "high-capital" from the start, and never "low-technology" from the start, because even though our process technology lagged the cutting edge by two to three generations at founding, our yield was very high, and high yield reflected solid engineering foundations.</p>
<p class="en-para">Although I didn't like the "low-technology" image that the Chinese term "代工" carried, "代工" was a term Taiwan was accustomed to and inconvenient to change. TSMC's positioning in my mind was unquestionable: "Dedicated Foundry," translated into Chinese as "专业晶圆代工" (dedicated wafer foundry).</p>
</div>
