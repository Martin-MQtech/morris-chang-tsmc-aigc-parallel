> **Marriage**
>
> I separated from my first wife in late 1981. In the two decades since, my career took me through Texas Instruments, General Instrument, ITRI, and TSMC. I lived in Dallas, NewYork, and Taiwan. I had moved from middle age into my later years, and my spirit had weathered many storms. I had no intention of remarrying—until I met Susan (Sophie) Chang in Taiwan. Her vitality, energy, and optimism suddenly made me feel young again. We decided to marry on January 23, 2001.
>
> It was the second marriage for both of us—starting anew, without the freshness of a first marriage that feels life is just beginning. We didn't wish to make a great fuss, so the ceremony took place in the small chapel at San Jose City Hall, California. That evening, three tables at an upscale Chinese restaurant in Silicon Valley celebrated with close relatives in California. Both of Sophie's parents attended. My father had passed away; my mother, ninety-one and in early-stage dementia, lived far away in New York and could not attend. She would not meet her new daughter-in-law until Sophie and I visited New York two months later.
>
> When we set our wedding date, I did not know a storm was about to hit TSMC—Q1 2001 orders had plunged nearly 25% from the previous quarter! And it looked as though this storm was only beginning, with no sign of abating. Once we learned of it, Sophie and I resolved not to change the wedding date but to postpone the honeymoon.
>
> The morning after our wedding, I rose early to catch the 7:00 a.m. flight from San Jose to Austin, connected to Dallas that afternoon, then Boston the next day, returning to San Jose on the third day for a client dinner. In three days, I flew nearly ten thousand miles to visit five major clients.
>
> On Saturday, January 27—fourth day after the wedding—I flew ten thousand miles back to Taipei, arriving early Monday morning, and went to work in Hsinchu that day.
>
> Sophie was understanding, but astonished—this, she realized, was the life she was signing up for.
