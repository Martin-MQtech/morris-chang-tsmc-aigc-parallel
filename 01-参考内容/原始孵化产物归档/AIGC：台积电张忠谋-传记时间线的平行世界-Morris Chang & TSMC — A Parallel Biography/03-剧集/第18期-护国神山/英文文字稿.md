## Opening

[SFX: waves against a shore, and in the distance the low, long blast of a freighter's horn]

June 5, 2018. Morris Chang retired.

[Main narrator]

He handed over the chairman's seat, and the TSMC he had guarded for thirty-three years. That day, the whole world thought a legend had reached its full stop.

[Main narrator, slower]

But no one could foresee that the story had not ended — it had only just entered its most thrilling chapter.

[Main narrator]

In Taiwan, people gave TSMC a nickname — "the sacred mountain that shields the nation."

Half of that name is pride, half is awe. Because what this mountain holds back is not wind and rain, but the storms of an entire era.

[Main narrator]

Where did that name come from? It is simple.

TSMC holds well over half of the world's most advanced chipmaking. When the whole world was frantic over "chip shortages," this company became the mountain that governments and companies everywhere lined up to ask for help.

[Main narrator, slower]

Germany needed chips, and came to Taiwan. Japan needed chips, and came to Taiwan. America needed chips, and came to Taiwan too. The world's cars, phones, and computers all trace back, somewhere, to this mountain.

[Main narrator]

This episode is the last of eighteen. We tell the story of the mountain that shields the island.

## Act One · A War Without Gunfire

The storm began with a war that had no gunfire.

[Main narrator]

In May 2019, the United States put Huawei on its export-control "Entity List."

Huawei was TSMC's second-largest customer. Its phone chips were made on TSMC's most advanced process. A Taiwanese company was suddenly caught between two great powers.

[Main narrator, slower]

On May 15, 2020, the United States tightened the rules further. The moment they were announced, TSMC had almost no room to choose — it had to comply, because nearly every step of its chipmaking depended on American equipment and technology.

[Main narrator]

On the very same day, TSMC announced something else: it would invest twelve billion dollars to build an advanced wafer fab in Arizona, in the United States.

Two pieces of news landed on the same day, like two faces of one coin: on one side, a cutoff; on the other, a new factory. TSMC was being pushed forward by the flood of the times.

[Main narrator, slower]

On May 18, 2020, TSMC announced: it would stop taking new orders from Huawei. Orders already taken had to be shipped out by September 14.

[Main narrator]

That day, TSMC closed a door — a door that had once been the tightest bond between it and its second-largest customer.

[Main narrator, slower]

September 14 became, that year, the heaviest date in the semiconductor industry.

[Main narrator]

Before that day, TSMC's production lines ran day and night, rushing for Huawei. It was a race against time — every chip had to come off the line and onto a cargo plane before the deadline.

When the last shipment crossed the Pacific, the door closed for good.

TSMC lost its second-largest customer, and Huawei lost its sharpest blade. Both companies were forced to turn around, and walk toward their own unknown roads.

And as TSMC's founder, Morris Chang, after retiring, stood on the wind's edge in a different role.

[Main narrator, slower]

From 2018 to 2023, he served seven times as Taiwan's "leader's representative" at the APEC economic leaders' meetings.

[Main narrator]

A retired old man became the most dignified envoy between this small island and the world. People only then realized: the "mountain" was never just those factory buildings — it was also a man, and the weight cast by a whole life.

## Act Two · Building Factories Beyond the Mountain

And TSMC itself was doing something it had never done in its history: building factories beyond Taiwan.

[Main narrator]

December 6, 2022. Phoenix, Arizona.

TSMC's Arizona fab welcomed its "first tool-in" ceremony. That day, the President of the United States, Joe Biden, came in person.

[Main narrator, slower]

Standing in the crowd, Morris Chang said something that quieted the whole world. He said:

"Globalization is almost dead. Free trade is almost dead. A lot of people still wish they would come back, but I don't think they will."

[Main narrator]

Those words, spoken by a ninety-one-year-old man, were quoted by media around the world. Because the man saying them was himself one of globalization's greatest beneficiaries.

On that same day, TSMC announced it would more than triple its Arizona investment — from twelve billion dollars to forty billion — to build a second fab.

[Main narrator, slower]

Standing before the cleanroom of TSMC Arizona that day, Morris Chang remembered a scene from many years ago.

[Main narrator]

In 1987, when he founded TSMC, he had in hand only a factory not yet built, and a group of people who were only half convinced. Thirty-five years later, here in this desert, the President of the United States, the CEO of Apple, and the giants of the entire semiconductor industry had all come to witness what his "pure foundry" bet had grown into.

[Main narrator, slower]

From an idea nobody believed in, to a company the American president would stand for in person — between the two lay the time of a whole generation.

[Main narrator]

Two years later, in November 2024, the U.S. government gave TSMC Arizona a grant of six point six billion dollars — part of America's "CHIPS Act," with a single purpose: to bring the most advanced chipmaking back to America.

Meanwhile, on the other side of the Pacific, another fab was rising.

[Main narrator, slower]

On November 9, 2021, TSMC announced a joint venture with Sony, in Kumamoto, Japan — Japan Advanced Semiconductor Manufacturing, or JASM. Among its shareholders were the auto-parts giant DENSO, and Toyota.

[Main narrator]

On February 24, 2024, the Kumamoto fab opened — TSMC's first wafer fab in Japan.

[Main narrator, slower]

On opening day, Japan's Prime Minister Fumio Kishida sent a congratulatory video. TSMC's founder Morris Chang, now ninety-two, was not there in person — but the fab bore his shadow everywhere.

[Main narrator]

Because more than thirty years earlier, one of TSMC's very first orders had been a memory chip from Japan. Thirty-some years later, TSMC brought its most mature technology back to the neighbor it had once left behind in semiconductors.

From the desert of Arizona to the fields of Kumamoto, TSMC's factories crossed both shores of the Pacific for the first time.

Some say it was TSMC protecting itself in an era of "de-globalization"; others say it was TSMC embedding itself into the chessboard of every major market on earth.

[Main narrator, slower]

Whichever it was, one fact grew ever clearer: this company had become too big for any nation to ignore.

## Act Three · The Wave of Artificial Intelligence

And just as the factories rose one by one, a far bigger wave came crashing in.

[Main narrator]

In 2023, artificial intelligence suddenly erupted.

[Main narrator, slower]

What lit the fuse was a product called ChatGPT, which had appeared a year earlier. It let the world see for the first time that a machine could "speak" like this.

[Main narrator]

From that day on, computing power became the scarcest resource of the age. And the foundation of computing power was the chips TSMC made.

That year, people lined up to buy a kind of chip called a GPU, to train large models. And the making of nearly all those GPUs pointed to one company — TSMC.

[Main narrator, slower]

The world's most valuable companies, from then on, began to revolve around TSMC. What they wanted was nothing else but TSMC's never-enough capacity.

[Main narrator]

And at the center of it all was an advanced packaging technology called CoWoS.

[Main narrator, slower]

Remember? In Episode Sixteen, we told how Morris Chang had quietly buried two seeds back in 2017: one called InFO, critical to smartphones; the other called CoWoS — in his own words, critical to artificial intelligence.

[Main narrator]

Six years later, that seed bloomed.

In July 2023, TSMC announced it would double its CoWoS capacity. And even then, it could not meet demand. From Nvidia's H100 and H200, to the later Blackwell, every AI chip had to pass through TSMC's CoWoS packaging before it could leave the factory.

And the detonator of it all was a company that makes no chips and only draws blueprints.

It has no factory and no production line, yet on TSMC's process it became the new king of the AI age.

Its name is Nvidia.

[Main narrator, slower]

The man who once turned down TSMC's CEO seat with "I already have a job" — Jensen Huang — is now TSMC's most important customer.

[Main narrator]

Fate, after a long circle, bound the two men and the two companies tightly together once more. One builds, one dreams; together they power the era's most surging computing.

[Main narrator, slower]

For now, remember one thing: the young man whom Morris Chang had once wanted — and who had declined — now stands with this "mountain," on the highest crest of the age.

[Main narrator]

Its full story, we leave for the next volume.

## Act Four · The Order of Dr. Sun Yat-sen

April 19, 2024. Morris Chang welcomed one of the most solemn moments of his life.

[Main narrator]

That day, he received Taiwan's highest honor — the Order of Dr. Sun Yat-sen.

[Main narrator, slower]

Named after Sun Yat-sen, it is the highest civil decoration of this land.

[Main narrator]

Those bestowing it said it was to honor him for building TSMC from nothing into the summit of the global semiconductor industry.

[Main narrator, slower]

That year, thirty-seven full years had passed since he founded TSMC.

[Main narrator]

That year, Morris Chang was ninety-three.

[Main narrator, slower]

Let us run the time backward, and glance over it once, quickly.

[Main narrator]

1937, the war against Japan broke out. At six, he fled with his parents, from Nanjing to Hong Kong. That was "the child who fled."

1949, he boarded a Pan Am flight for America. That was "from the Huangpu to the Charles River."

1954, he failed his doctoral exams twice, and wrote forty job applications. That was "the forty letters."

1958, he joined Texas Instruments, and stayed twenty-five years. Those were "the space-race years" and "the decade at the summit of semiconductors."

1983, he left Texas; two years later, he returned to Taiwan. That was "leaving Texas, and the invitation home."

1987, he founded TSMC. That was "the pure-foundry revolution."

[Main narrator, slower]

Then came the temptation of memory, the steadiness of the counter-cycle, the pain of the handover, the gilded years, Apple coming knocking, the guardian of Moore's Law, the handover and retirement...

[Main narrator]

Until today — the mountain that shields the island.

And the summit of it all is not that medal, but the mountain he built with his own hands.

## Coda · A Parallel View

Back to the question: how did this mountain come to be?

[Main narrator]

Some say it was Morris Chang's doing alone.

But Morris Chang himself, when he retired, said another thing. He said TSMC had become a company the world "needs" — and the world's need, especially in artificial intelligence, is growing fast.

[Main narrator, slower]

"Needs" — that word is the key to the whole answer.

[Main narrator]

A mountain becomes a "shield" not because any single person is great, but because, when the flood of the age came, an entire generation made the same choice.

Morris Chang chose pure foundry; the engineers chose to polish yields day and night; the customers chose to hand over their most expensive orders; and this small island chose to stake its whole fortune on semiconductors.

[Main narrator, slower]

A generation's choices, laid into a mountain by time.

[Main narrator]

Morris Chang said his most successful work was the last one — TSMC.

But TSMC's success was never his alone. It belongs to the engineers who stood in cleanrooms for a lifetime, to the customers who chose to trust in the hardest moments, to the decision not to lay people off in the storm, and to the courage of this island in staking its destiny on the idea of "pure foundry."

[Main narrator, slower]

So when people ask who built this mountain —

the answer is time.

[Main narrator]

From 1931 to 2024. From a child who fled war, to the builder of the mountain that shields an island. Morris Chang spent ninety-three years telling one truth —

a true legend is never the story of one person. It is countless ordinary people, in the same direction, day after day, laying stone upon stone.

[Main narrator, slower]

A "sacred mountain" is never carried up by one person — it is a generation's choices, laid into a mountain by time.

[Main narrator]

That is the mountain that shields the island.

And that is the final line of the eighteen episodes of Morris Chang's Parallel World.

[Main narrator, slower]

One timeline, another perspective.

[Main narrator]

In these eighteen episodes, what we told was never just Morris Chang alone.

We told how a child who fled war found his footing in the flood of the age; how a company grew into a mountain on a small island; and how one industry changed the direction of the whole world.

[Main narrator, slower]

Thank you for walking this road with us.

[Main narrator]

The story ends here. But the mountain is still there — higher, day by day.

And somewhere inside that mountain, a young engineer in a cleanroom is, right now, still pushing a process node forward — just as he did, sixty years ago.

[Main narrator, time capsule]

His year: 2024 — at ninety-three, he receives the nation's highest honor, the Order of Dr. Sun Yat-sen.

The world's year: TSMC has become the focal point of a contest between two great powers; the wave of artificial intelligence is sweeping the globe.

One timeline — the year he retired, no one could foresee that this company would become the shared destiny of a small island.

## Knowledge Extension · Historical Background Cards

1. **The Huawei cutoff**: In May 2019 the U.S. put Huawei on the Entity List; after export controls tightened on May 15, 2020, TSMC stopped new Huawei orders on May 18, 2020 (Huawei was its second-largest customer), with a September 14 shipping deadline.
2. **Arizona fab**: Announced May 15, 2020 at $12B; on December 6, 2022 the first tool-in ceremony was held with President Biden, and TSMC raised the investment to $40B; in November 2024 it received a $6.6B grant under the U.S. CHIPS Act.
3. **Kumamoto JASM**: Announced November 9, 2021 as a joint venture with Sony (shareholders include DENSO and Toyota); the Kumamoto fab opened February 24, 2024 — TSMC's first fab in Japan.
4. **AI wave and CoWoS**: In 2023 AI erupted, and Nvidia's H100/H200/Blackwell GPUs all require TSMC's CoWoS advanced packaging; in July 2023 TSMC announced it would double CoWoS capacity and still fell short of demand.
5. **Order of Dr. Sun Yat-sen and "globalization is almost dead"**: On April 19, 2024, Morris Chang received Taiwan's highest honor; on December 6, 2022, at the Arizona tool-in ceremony, he said "globalization is almost dead and free trade is almost dead."
