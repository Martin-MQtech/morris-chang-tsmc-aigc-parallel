> **Old Steed in the Stable, Ambitions Still Grand**
>
> Returning to the CEO role in June 2009 at seventy-eight, I was often asked why I came back. TSMC was at a critical juncture—forty-nanometer yield problems unresolved, client relationships strained, morale at rock bottom. Someone had to step up.
>
> "An old steed in the stable still dreams of galloping a thousand miles"—this line from Cao Cao captured my state of mind perfectly.
>
> Within months, I addressed the yield issues by empowering the production team (not micromanaging them), resolved the Nvidia dispute through a personal visit to Jensen Huang's home over pizza, converted over two thousand contract workers to full-time employees, merged the advanced and mainstream business groups, recalled Shang-yi Chiang as head of R&D, and established a target of spending eight percent of revenue on R&D. We then made our defining bet—aggressively ramping capacity for forty and twenty-eight nanometers, positioning TSMC to ride the coming smartphone wave.
>
> The Qualcomm twenty-eight nanometer crisis of 2012—where demand surged from forty thousand to three hundred thousand wafers—tested us. But we persevered, and twenty-eight nanometer became TSMC's gateway to the smartphone era.
