# 德仪篇·第六章｜实现「美国梦」

实现「美国梦」——

「美国梦」这个名词，在我长住美国时（一九四九—一九九五年）相当普遍。我稍加研究后，发现这个名词大约来自十九世纪末、二十世纪初欧洲到美国的移民。他们到美国时贫穷、无畏惧、有希望的自由劳动者。他们的「美国梦」是要在美国至少做到中产階級——而实现「美国梦」的途径，往往是高等教育。

刚到美国时，我是一个穷外国学生——和美国的穷人一样，同有「美国梦」。「美国梦」的终点只是中产阶级的收入——那我在希凡尼亚开始工作时就已实现「美国梦」。但现在（一九七二年）更不一样了：以收入和财富言，我已在全美顶尖百分之一以内；以事业地位言，我掌管数万员工，是全球半导体业的领袖；以社会地位言，我和妻也在达拉斯上流社会内。

有了一点钱的一个好处，就是可以帮助父母亲。父母亲于一九五〇年到美国——起初父亲还到哥伦比亚大学商学院读了一个管理硕士，以为有了这个学位可以找到适宜的工作。但得到学位后求职的结果，只得到两个聘雇机会，薪水都很低。他决定不接受。一直到一九五五年在百老汇住家附近顶了一家礼物杂货小店。父母亲两人亲自打理——父亲整年应付外界，母亲整年招呼顾客。双亲已有疲态。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">Realizing the "American Dream" —</p>
<p class="en-para">The term "American Dream" was quite common throughout my long residence in the United States (1949–1995). After a little research, I found that the term likely originated with European immigrants to America at the turn of the twentieth century. They arrived poor, fearless, hopeful, free laborers. Their "American Dream" was to reach at least the middle class in America — and the path to achieving it was often higher education.</p>
<p class="en-para">When I first arrived in America, I was a poor foreign student — like America's poor, I too had an "American Dream." If the endpoint of the "American Dream" was merely middle-class income, then I had already achieved mine when I started working at Sylvania. But by 1972, things were quite different: by income and wealth, I was in the top 1% of the nation; by professional status, I led tens of thousands of employees as the head of the global semiconductor industry; by social standing, my wife and I had entered Dallas high society.</p>p class="en-para">One benefit of having some money was the ability to help my parents. My parents came to America in 1950 — at first my father even enrolled at Columbia Business School for a master's in management, believing the degree would lead to a suitable job. But upon graduating, he received only two offers, both at low salaries. He declined them. Not until 1955 did he take over a gift and grocery shop near their Broadway home. My parents ran it themselves — my father managing external affairs year-round, my mother greeting customers year-round. Both were showing signs of weariness.</p>
</div>

### Cheat Sheet · 商业语汇

1. **Top 1% income threshold**
**中文解释**：顶尖百分之一收入门槛，指进入全美收入最高的百分之一行列；一九七二年的门槛约在三万美元以上——对一位四十一岁的企业高管来说，这是「美国梦」最直观的答案。
**商业造句**: *Making the top 1% at forty-one was not the point of the American Dream — it was the numerical confirmation of a promise he had made to himself as a twenty-year-old immigrant.*

2. **MBA underemployment**
**中文解释**：硕士学位低聘，指高学位持有者因就业市场、年龄歧视或文化障碍而无法找到匹配学历的工作；张忠谋父亲的故事是移民家庭最为心酸的现实——知识和学位并不直接等于经济回报。
**商业造句">The Columbia MBA who ended up running a grocery store was not a failure of education — it was a failure of timing, language, and an economy that did not yet know how to absorb foreign talent.*

3. **Social ladder through education**
**中文解释**：教育攀升社会阶梯，指通过高等教育实现阶层跃迁的「美国梦」经典路径；张忠谋在自传中承认自己的路线是这条——并且他在父母未能完成的路上走了更远。
**商业造句">His father's broken American Dream became his son's inheritance — not of wealth, but of the stubborn belief that education could open doors that the world kept closed.*

### 修辞赏析

<span class="rhetoric-note">
<span class="zh">张忠谋在自传中插入了一段关于父亲的心酸叙事：一位哥伦比亚大学商学院硕士，在美国求职只得到两个低薪聘雇机会，最后选择自己经营一家小店。这个细节是张忠谋自传中最为心碎的「隐藏叙事」——它不仅解释了张忠谋对教育的执着（因为他亲眼看见父亲的高学历如何被浪费），也解释了他对「机会」的珍视（因为他知道父亲不是不够优秀，只是没有机会）。父亲的不得志，是张忠谋这一代人「美国梦」叙事中最常见的副产品。</span>
<span class="en">Chang inserts a poignant narrative about his father: a Columbia Business School master's who, job-hunting in America, received only two low-salary offers and ultimately chose to run a small shop. This is the crushed "hidden narrative" of the autobiography — it explains not only Chang's devotion to education (having witnessed a father's advanced degree go to waste) but also his cherishing of "opportunity" (knowing his father was not untalented, merely unlucky). A father's frustration was the most common byproduct of the "American Dream" narrative for Chang's generation.</span>
</span>

### 父亲的天伦礼物

一九七一年，张忠谋首次拥有价值一百万美元德仪股票。他决定赠与父母十分之一——十万美元。在那样重重安全网下，父母决定退休。一九七二年他们出顶小店。「以后父亲过了九年、母亲过了二十几年健康、自由、舒适的生活——一直到父亲在一九八一年中风，母亲在二〇〇〇年代渐渐失记忆力。」张忠谋写道：「一九七二年是我人生的一个重要瞻前顾后点。我已实现了我的『美国梦』，前途看起来一片光明——下一步应该是德仪公司总裁的职位。」

<p class="en-para">In 1971, Chang first acquired $1 million in TI stock. He gave one-tenth — $100,000 — to his parents. With this and other safety nets, they retired. In 1972 they sold the shop. "Father enjoyed nine more years, and mother more than twenty years, of healthy, free, comfortable life — until Father had a stroke in 1981 and mother gradually lost her memory in the 2000s." Chang wrote: "1972 was an important looking-back-and-forward point in my life. I had realized my 'American Dream'; the road ahead looked bright — the next step should have been the presidency of TI."</p>
