定位

一九八六至八七年的冬天，台积电即将组成，台湾媒体在一度热烈报导"政府最大投资案"后，忽然平静下来。我也借这由绚烂趋平淡、而真正考验即将来临的过渡时期，对台积电的创始初期应采取的策略，做了一个检讨。

成立台积电的目的，基本是为工研院已进行十年的IC计划及继承IC计划的VLSI计划建立工业出路，所以创始期的核心团队，就是工研院VLSI计划实验工厂的成员。作为一个要能赚钱的半导体公司，他们的强点是：成熟技术高生产良率；他们的弱点是：没有IC设计技术，制程技术落后两、三代，而且实验工厂只有台湾客户，没有国外客户，甚至连与国外客户的接触都没有。

## Positioning

In the winter of 1986-87, as TSMC was about to be formed, the Taiwanese media—after initially reporting fervently on the "government's largest investment project"—suddenly calmed down. I also took advantage of this transition period, when things were moving from excitement to calm and the real test was yet to come, to review the strategies TSMC should adopt in its early founding period.

The purpose of establishing TSMC was fundamentally to create an industrial outlet for ITRI's ten-year IC program and its successor, the VLSI program. Thus, the founding core team consisted of members from the ITRI VLSI program's experimental factory. As a semiconductor company that needed to be profitable, their strengths were: high production yield with mature technology. Their weaknesses were: no IC design technology, process technology lagging two to three generations behind, and the experimental factory only had Taiwanese clients—no foreign clients, not even any contact with foreign clients.

从"Foundry"到"专业晶圆代工"

我在第十六章已提及，考虑了工研院VLSI计划的强点及弱点，其实它的工业出路只有一条：舍弃设计，只为客户制造。早在七零年代，当时英特尔创办人之一摩尔已为此"不设计，只制造"事业取了一个名字：foundry。只是，摩尔的foundry只是一般又设计、又制造公司（IDM）的副业。把foundry成为专业，自始自台积电始。更具讽刺性的，当我找英特尔投资台积电时（第十七章），英特尔拒绝了，原因是不认为专业foundry能赚钱。

英文的foundry是一个中性名词，无语意亦无贬意，但中文一下子把foundry译成"晶圆代工"，大概因为台湾非常习惯"代工"。即使加上"专业"两字，也不能消除一般"代工"的低技术、低资本的形象。

其实台积电的"专业晶圆代工"自始就"高资本"，自始也不是"低技术"，因为即使我们创始时制程技术落后尖端技术两至三代，但我们的良率很高，而高良率反映了扎实的工程基础。

虽然我不喜欢中文"代工"附带的"低技术"形象，但"代工"是台湾习惯的名词，也不便改。台积电的定位在我心里倒毫无疑问："Dedicated Foundry"，中文翻译成"专业晶圆代工"。

ReadShift 双语翻译

As mentioned in Chapter 16, considering the strengths and weaknesses of ITRI's VLSI program, there was really only one industrial outlet: abandon design, and manufacture only for customers. As early as the 1970s, Gordon Moore, one of Intel's founders, had already given this "don't design, just manufacture" business a name: foundry. However, Moore's foundry was merely a sideline for a company that both designed and manufactured (IDM). Making foundry a specialty—professional foundry—began with TSMC. More ironically, when I sought Intel's investment in TSMC (Chapter 17), Intel refused, its reason being that it did not believe professional foundry could be profitable.

The English word "foundry" is a neutral term, with no semantic nor pejorative connotation. But Chinese immediately translated foundry as "晶圆代工" (wafer foundry), probably because Taiwan was very accustomed to the concept of "代工" (contract manufacturing). Even adding the word "专业" (dedicated/professional) couldn't eliminate the low-technology, low-capital image associated with typical "代工" (contract manufacturing).

In fact, TSMC's "dedicated foundry" was "high-capital" from the start, and never "low-technology" from the start, because even though our process technology lagged the cutting edge by two to three generations at founding, our yield was very high, and high yield reflected solid engineering foundations.

Although I didn't like the "low-technology" image that the Chinese term "代工" carried, "代工" was a term Taiwan was accustomed to and inconvenient to change. TSMC's positioning in my mind was unquestionable: "Dedicated Foundry," translated into Chinese as "专业晶圆代工" (dedicated wafer foundry).

两个策略，一个价值

今日（二零二零年）的台积电以尖端技术（七纳米、五纳米、三纳米）见称。因为台积电领先世界的尖端半导体技术，才成为我所称的"地缘政治策略家的兵家必争之地"。最近有友人问我：

"听说你们创始时的策略是市场营销，而不是达到尖端技术。我以为我一定听错了。"

他没有听错。创办时最大问题是没有客户。一厂开始时的产能是少少的每月一千片（六吋晶圆），第一年整年都没填满。创始时我们就准备建二厂，二厂初期的预计产能是每月一万两千片，从每月几百片跳到每月一万两千片，不但需要想象力，还需要策略以及说服力，才能说服董事会拨款给我们。

所以我们的第一策略是：开关美国，其次欧洲市场。美、欧市场是当时全世界最大的半导体市场。台积电核心团队只熟悉台湾市场。与美、欧市场比，台湾市场真是微乎其微。谁能领导核心团队开关美、欧市场？我认为自己是最佳人选，但在台积电成立时，我任工研院院长才一年多，正火热地要改革工研院，绝无时间或精力再兼一个创新、开关的任务。替代方案就是：雇一个富有半导体经营能力的美国人做总经理。

Today (2020) TSMC is renowned for its cutting-edge technology (7nm, 5nm, 3nm). It is precisely because of TSMC's world-leading advanced semiconductor technology that it has become what I call "a place that geopolitical strategists fight over." Recently a friend asked me:

"I heard that your founding strategy was marketing, not achieving cutting-edge technology. I thought I must have misheard."

He had not misheard. The biggest problem at founding was having no customers. Fab 1 started with a modest capacity of a thousand wafers per month (6-inch), and wasn't fully utilized for the entire first year. At founding, we were already planning to build Fab 2, whose initial projected capacity was twelve thousand wafers per month. Jumping from a few hundred per month to twelve thousand per month required not only imagination but also strategy and persuasiveness to convince the board to appropriate funds to us.

So our first strategy was: open up the U.S. market, then the European market. The U.S. and European markets were the largest semiconductor markets in the world at that time. TSMC's core team was only familiar with the Taiwanese market. Compared to the U.S. and European markets, the Taiwanese market was truly negligible. Who could lead the core team to open up the U.S. and European markets? I considered myself the best candidate, but when TSMC was founded, I had been ITRI president for only a little over a year and was passionately reforming ITRI. I absolutely had no time or energy to also take on the pioneering, market-opening mission. The alternative was: hire an American with semiconductor management capability to be general manager.

第二策略与价值观

第二策略是：加强核心团队已有的高良率能力。高良率是我们创台时的长处，也是我们选择"专业晶圆代工"的原因，但核心团队的高良率建立在当时台湾市场需求的廉价品上（例如电子表IC）。现在我们要开关高、中档美、欧市场。他们对于品质、可靠度、制程监控（Process Control）、制程改变控制（Change Control）等等，要比台湾市场讲究得多。我有相当信心我们核心团队既有的高良率建立在扎实工程基础上，现在要更上一层。

除了这两个策略外，我从我做"客制品"的经历体验到：千金难买客户对你的信任。现在，台积电只做"客制品"。我们必须一开始就赢得客户的信任。不轻易答应，但答应的，一定要不计代价做对。对供应商、对社会，甚至对竞争者，也要诚信以对。不但对客户要诚信，我不相信一个公司能做一个人又是另一面。

The second strategy was: strengthen the core team's existing high-yield capability. High yield was our strength at founding, and the reason we chose the "dedicated foundry" model. But the core team's high yield had been built on low-cost products for the Taiwanese market (e.g., electronic watch ICs). Now we were going to open up the high-end and mid-range U.S. and European markets. Their demands regarding quality, reliability, process control, change control, and so on were far more rigorous than those of the Taiwanese market. I had considerable confidence that our core team's existing high yield was built on solid engineering foundations, and now needed to go one level higher.

Beyond these two strategies, I had learned from my own experience doing "custom products" as TI: customer trust is a thousand pieces of gold cannot buy. Now, TSMC did only "custom products." We had to win customers' trust from the very beginning. Don't promise easily, but once promised, deliver at all costs. To suppliers, to society, even to competitors—conduct with integrity. Not only to customers must we be honest; I don't believe a company can act one way to people and another way otherwise.

商誉竞争

当年我对经建会报告成立台积电，并且说台积电要以"好的服务"竞争时，中央银行总裁曾经哈哈了一声："台湾公司要凭商誉竞争，倒是第一次听闻。"现在，我们真的要做了。

起始台积电的两个策略，后来有增加，最重要的增加就是十二年后（一九九九年）的"技术领先"，也随市场和竞争环境而有所改变，但起始的价值观——诚信，却一直没变过。

Back when I reported to the Council for Economic Planning and Development on establishing TSMC, and said that TSMC would compete on "good service," the Central Bank governor laughed out loud: "A Taiwanese company competing on reputation—that's the first I've heard of it." Now, we were really going to do it.

TSMC's initial two strategies were later augmented—the most important addition being "technology leadership" twelve years later (1999)—and they evolved with the market and competitive environment. But the founding value—integrity—never changed.

戴克斯——第一任总经理

一九八六年秋，台积电募资有谱，我就开始物色美国人总经理。我接触了三、四位我熟悉的德仪前下属，现在已在别的半导体公司任要职。竟无一人有兴趣，连来台湾看一看的兴趣都没有。

就在那时，戴克斯和我接触了。我认识戴克斯，但不熟。一九八五年春，我们曾一起参加过一次美国半导体业协会理事会。那时我代表通用器材；他代表GE半导体公司。一年半后，我已在台湾，即将组成台积电；他仍在GE，但即将失业。他即将失业的原因是：GE总裁威尔许决定关闭GE半导体公司。

威尔许是一九八一九九一年至二零零一年的GE总裁，也是一个风云人物，一度被誉为"二十世纪后半最佳企业总裁"。GE是一个许多事业的集合体。在威尔许接任总裁初期，他检讨了每一事业，对一个在它行业营收排名不高的事业（例如GE半导体公司在半导体行业的营收排名也许是第十几名或第二十几名），威尔许只有一个命令："五年内做到第一或第二名；否则，关门！"对戴克斯而言，要他做到半导体业第一或第二等于要他爬喜马拉雅山，所以结论是关门。拖到一九八六年秋，威尔许的五年时限亦将到临，戴克斯亦将失业，所以戴对台积电总经理这机会大有兴趣。

In the fall of 1986, with TSMC's fundraising looking promising, I began scouting for an American general manager. I approached three or four former TI subordinates whom I knew well, who were now holding senior positions at other semiconductor companies. Not a single one was interested—they weren't even interested in coming to Taiwan to take a look.

Just then, Douglas Dexter made contact with me. I knew Dexter, but not well. In the spring of 1985, we had both attended a meeting of the U.S. Semiconductor Industry Association board of directors. At that time I represented General Electric (G.E.) equipment; he represented GE's semiconductor division. A year and a half later, I was in Taiwan, about to form TSMC; he was still at GE, but about to lose his job. The reason he was about to be unemployed: GE president Jack Welch had decided to shut down GE's semiconductor business.

Welch was GE's president from 1981 to 2001, a legendary figure once hailed as "the best corporate president of the second half of the twentieth century." GE was a collection of many businesses. Early in Welch's tenure as president, he reviewed each business. For a business whose revenue ranking in its industry was not high (e.g., GE Semiconductor's ranking in the semiconductor industry was perhaps fifteenth or twentieth), Welch had only one command: "Become first or second within five years—otherwise, shut it down!" For Dexter, asking him to make GE Semiconductor first or second in the industry was like asking him to climb Mt. Everest, so the conclusion was to shut it down. This dragged on until the fall of 1986, when Welch's five-year deadline was approaching and Dexter was about to lose his job. So Dexter was very interested in the opportunity to be TSMC's general manager.

飞利浦的审核

我在一九八六年十月亲访戴克斯于他居住地——北卡罗来纳州图兰市，相谈甚投契。戴很认同"专业晶圆代工"商业模式以及开关美欧市场策略；戴也在年底来台湾与"五人小组"五人小组相会。"五人小组"没有什幺意见，只是戴和我都觉得很奇怪，在晚餐席上"五人小组"除了在介绍时与戴寒暄几句外，只互相中文谈笑，而没有对戴发一言或问一句话。

根据当时的"股东协议"，总经理由最大股东（开发基金）提名，董事会通过后任命。飞利浦在董事会十席内占三席，所以我也请飞利浦在台湾的两位董事与戴克斯面谈，两位都很支持。我以为我已做得很周到了，想不到一星期后我就接到第三位飞利浦董事（克列斯曼）的电话，他数落了我一顿，警告我"必须小心"（Careful）维护飞利浦权益，而且坚持面试戴克斯。我只得嘱戴于一九八七年一月去荷兰见克列斯曼。见戴面后，克列斯曼倒很爽快同意。戴克斯也在二月初来到台湾报到。

In October 1986, I personally visited Dexter at his home—Raleigh, North Carolina. We hit it off well. Dexter strongly identified with the "dedicated foundry" business model and the strategy to open up U.S. and European markets. Dexter also came to Taiwan at the end of the year to meet with the "Five-Person Committee." The committee had no objections, but both Dexter and I thought it was strange: at the dinner, apart from exchanging a few pleasantries during introductions, the committee members only chatted with each other in Chinese and didn't speak a word to Dexter or ask him a single question.

Under the then-existing "Shareholders Agreement," the general manager was nominated by the largest shareholder (the Development Fund) and appointed upon board approval. Philips held three of the board's ten seats, so I also arranged for Philips' two directors in Taiwan to interview Dexter. Both were very supportive. I thought I had been quite thorough, but barely a week later I received a call from a third Philips director (Krigsman), who lectured me, warning me to "be careful" to protect Philips' rights and insisting on interviewing Dexter himself. I had no choice but to ask Dexter to go to the Netherlands in January 1987 to meet Krigsman. After meeting Dexter, Krigsman readily approved. Dexter came to Taiwan to report for duty in early February.

核心团队踟蹰

在一九八五年九月开始构想台积电时，我以为如果办成台积电，一定会有一位工研院高层主管带领一个IC计划核心团队来台积电。后来我开始对胡、史说台积电必须开关美、欧市场，所以第一任总经理应该是美国人；此后我发现胡已无兴趣来台积电，他那时已是交大校长、工研院院长的候选人，已无兴趣担任台积电总经理以下的任何职务；至于史钦泰，我也发现他的兴趣主要还是学术，志愿留在工研院。到台积电即将组成时，看起来，实验工厂厂长曾繁城是唯一可能的核心团队领导人。曾繁城在电子所的职级低所长两级。他的上司是电子所副所长。这位副所长也不愿加入台积电。

但是，在台积电准备开张前两星期，胡定华告知我：连曾繁城也有疑虑，他和核心团队都担心加入台积电后未来的前途。胡定华告诉我："你必须与曾谈。"

When I first conceived of TSMC in September 1985, I thought that if TSMC were successfully established, a senior ITRI manager would definitely lead an IC program core team to come to TSMC. Later, I began telling Hu and Shi that TSMC had to open up the U.S. and European markets, so the first general manager should be American. Afterward, I discovered that Hu was no longer interested in coming to TSMC—he was already a candidate for president of National Chiao Tung University and ITRI president, and he was no longer interested in any position below TSMC general manager. As for Shi Qin-tai, I also discovered that his interests were primarily academic; he wished to stay at ITRI. By the time TSMC was about to be formed, it appeared that Zeng Fan-cheng, the experimental factory director, was the only potential core team leader. Zeng Fan-cheng's rank at the Electronics Research Institute was two levels below the institute director. His immediate boss was the deputy institute director. This deputy director was also unwilling to join TSMC.

However, two weeks before TSMC was preparing to open, Hu Ding-hua informed me: even Zeng Fan-cheng had doubts. He and the core team all worried about their future prospects after joining TSMC. Hu Ding-hua told me: "You must talk with Zeng."

曾繁城的工会领袖姿态

我与曾谈了。后来我与他相熟后，曾经告诉我他那次他对我要求的姿态，简直是工会领袖对资方谈判的姿态！可怜我其实也是"劳方"，为了要办成台积电，只好充当"资方"，消释曾繁城的疑虑。

曾代表核心团队质疑两大点：第一，台积电如何与韩、日公司竞争？他没提美国公司，因为美国公司比韩、日公司更高一筹。韩、日公司我们已不能应付了。这质疑是我在募资期常要应付的。我重复了一遍我的两个倚靠：第一个倚靠是我们的"专业晶圆代工"商业模式。我们不与美、韩、日半导体公司竞争，它们反而是我们的客户。第二个倚靠是核心团队的高良率能力。对第一个倚靠，曾似乎半信半疑。对第二个倚靠，我的信心似乎高于核心团队对自己的信心。

但是，曾让我的辩论通过了。于是曾繁城提出第二个质疑。在此以前我的以为是：既然工研院以财团法人身份，素享高薪美誉，所以核心团队移到台积电，薪资不改；以后，当然再说。

但是，曾给了我一个比较复杂的解释。他说：台湾公司的习惯除发固定薪资外，每年都会视业绩发红利。当然，有的美国公司也发红利，但对一般员工而言，红利只是员工收入的小部分。照曾繁城那天所言，红利可以是收入的大部分。

我听懂了曾繁城的话后，觉得员工红利也不失为对员工的合理补贴。本来台湾工程师的收入远低于欧、美、日本。现在台积电要在世界竞争，应该也在员工收入上做相当程度的看齐。

I talked with Zeng. Later, after we became well acquainted, he told me that his posture during that negotiation with me was simply that of a union leader negotiating with management! The pity was that I was actually also "labor." In order to make TSMC happen, I had no choice but to play "management" and dispel Zeng Fan-cheng's doubts.

Zeng raised two major points on behalf of the core team. First: How would TSMC compete with Korean and Japanese companies? He didn't mention American companies, because American companies were a notch above Korean and Japanese ones. We could already barely handle Korean and Japanese companies. This was a question I frequently had to address during the fundraising period. I repeated my two pillars of reliance: The first was our "dedicated foundry" business model. We didn't compete with American, Korean, or Japanese semiconductor companies—they would actually be our customers. The second was the core team's high-yield capability. Regarding the first pillar, Zeng seemed half-believing. Regarding the second pillar, my confidence seemed higher than the core team's confidence in themselves.

Nevertheless, Zeng let my arguments pass. Then Zeng Fan-cheng raised his second concern. Previously, I had assumed: since ITRI, as a foundation, enjoyed a reputation for high salaries, when the core team transferred to TSMC, their salaries would remain unchanged; and of course, things would be discussed again later.

But Zeng gave me a more nuanced explanation. He said: Taiwanese company practice, in addition to paying a fixed salary, was to issue bonuses according to performance every year. Of course, some American companies also gave bonuses, but for ordinary employees, bonuses were only a small part of income. From what Zeng Fan-cheng said that day, it sounded like bonuses could be the majority of income.

After I understood what Zeng Fan-cheng was saying, I felt employee bonuses were also a reasonable form of compensation for employees. Originally, Taiwanese engineers' incomes were far lower than those in Europe, America, and Japan.
