## 一九八八年——我任工研院院长最后一年

一九八八年是台湾重要的一年，也是我任工研院院长的第三年、也是最后一年。

一月，蒋经国总统去世，李登辉副总统继任为总统。七月，原国科会主委陈履安替代李达海为经济部部长。陈部长三年前以「台湾有许多人才，为什么去美国找工研院院长？」为由，反对我任工研院院长，当时我不很在意，因为国科会不是工研院的「主管机关」。现在陈任我「主管机关」的部长，对我当然是恶耗。

七月，李国鼎政委退休，转任资政。这位受老蒋总统赏识、又在蒋经国手下忠诚服务的「四朝老臣」，在一九八八年已七十八岁。李政委是政府内最赏识我的一人。他的退休，以及退休前的渐渐失势，当然对我不利。

还有，「三顾茅庐」雇我的徐贤修，在一九八八年也面临退休。

听徐董事长说，李总统常自傲他的科技知识，我也知道在我接任工研院院长前，李登辉还是副总统时，陈履安主委就安排了工研院材料所林垂宙所长，经常对李副总统报告世界科技界动态。所以，当李总统在就任两个月后就宣告他要视察工研院，我倒抱了相当好奇及期待——他真的对促进台湾工业技术这么有兴趣？

在我趋前迎接李总统时，他立刻对我考试：「张院长，最近在某某（国外）科技月刊发表的某某新发现，你知道吗？」我不知道，我的惶恐表情换来他的不屑脸色。

接着是参观，以及简报。李总统没有打断我们的简报（他深知台湾不打断简报的「礼貌」），但在我结束最后的纲要、正想邀请总统去餐厅用午餐时，他忽然说：「慢着。」我停住了。他问：「你们与中央研究院及各大学的研究有什么联系？」

假如我是一个先进国家研究机构的院长，而这国家的总统问我跟国家最高研究机关有什么联系，我想我可以侃侃而谈。但这是一九八八年的台湾，我们的工业技术相当落后，我已做了两年半工研院院长，一直努力想把工研院和工业界连接起来，现在李总统问的「联系」，恰是相反方向——无关工业界实际需要的方向。

老实说，当时我不清楚工研院与中央研究院以及各大学有什么联系，因为我不认为有这联系，假如有，有什么重要性。我也不记得怎么回答李总统。总之，对李总统，我的回答一定是很令他窝囊。

政府人事的递易——总统、经济部部长、科技政委、工研院董事长——对工研院院长的位置，其实还是次要。只要看我的前任方贤齐院长，以「无为而治」见称，经历了好几轮政府人事变动，却毫发无损。但我一到工研院，就以「改革者」自命；那么，我的命运，就要看我的「改革」是否成功？以及我「兴波起浪」激发起来的情绪了。

两个答案都是负面的。我的三个改革——「十年内一半经费来自民间企业」和「三%员工留职察看」失败；「衍生公司」只是后来成功，当时却有相当阻力及负面反应。至于改革激起的情绪呢？问题可大了。无论院内、院外，都说我没有「人和」。

<p class="en-para"><b>1988 — My Final Year as ITRI President</b></p>
<p class="en-para">In January, President Chiang Ching-kuo passed away; Vice President Lee Teng-hui succeeded him. In July, former NSC Minister Chen Lian replaced Li Da-hai as Minister of Economic Affairs. Minister Chen had opposed my ITRI appointment three years earlier on the grounds that "Taiwan has plenty of talent — why look in America?" At the time I hadn't taken it seriously, since the NSC was not ITRI's "competent authority." Now Chen was the minister of my "competent authority" — decidedly bad news for me.</p>
<p class="en-para">Also in July, Minister K.T. Li retired and became a Senior Advisor. This "four-dynasty elder," valued by the elder Chiang and loyally serving under Chiang Ching-kuo, was 78 years old in 1988. Minister Li was the single most appreciative sponsor I had in government. His retirement and gradual loss beforehand of influence were obviously detrimental to me.</p>
<p class="en-para">Moreover, Hsu Hsiu-hsien — the man who had "paid three visits" to recruit me — also faced retirement in 1988.</p>
<p class="en-para">Chairman Hsu told me that President Lee often boasted of his scientific knowledge. I also knew that before I became ITRI President, when Lee was still Vice President, Minister Chen had arranged for ITRI Materials Institute Director Lin Chui-chou to brief Vice President Lee regularly on global technology trends. So when President Lee announced his visit to ITRI just two months after taking office, I was genuinely curious and eager — was he truly that interested in advancing Taiwan's industrial technology?</p>
<p class="en-para">As I stepped forward to greet President Lee, he immediately quizzed me: "President Chang, have you read the recent discovery published in [some foreign] science monthly?" I had not. My visible dismay drew a look of disdain.</p>
<p class="en-para">Then came the tour and briefings. President Lee did not interrupt our briefings (he well knew Taiwan's norm of "politeness" about not interrupting), but as I finished the summary and was about to invite him to lunch, he suddenly said: "Hold on." I stopped. He asked: "What connections do you have with Academia Sinica and the universities?"</p>
<p class="en-para">Had I been the head of a research institution in an advanced country, and the president asked about my links with the national academy, I could have talked at length. But this was 1988 Taiwan. Our industrial technology was quite backward. I had spent two and a half years as ITRI President striving to connect ITRI with industry. Now President Lee asked about "connections" in precisely the opposite direction — a direction irrelevant to industry's real needs.</p>
<p class="en-para">Frankly, I was not clear what connections ITRI had with Academia Sinica and the universities, because I didn't think such connections mattered. If they existed, what was their importance? I don't even remember how I answered. In any case, my answer must have been thoroughly unimpressive to President Lee.</p>
<p class="en-para">The churn of government personnel was, in the end, secondary to the ITRI presidency itself. Consider my predecessor, President Fang Xianqi, famous for "governing by non-action." He weathered several rounds of government personnel changes without a scratch. But the moment I arrived at ITRI, I cast myself as a "reformer." So my fate would depend on whether my reforms succeeded — and on the emotions I stirred up by "making waves."</p>
<p class="en-para">Both answers were negative. Two of my three reforms had failed. "Spin-offs" would succeed only later; at the time, they faced considerable resistance and negative reaction. As for the emotions my reforms provoked? Both inside and outside ITRI, people said I lacked "harmony with people."</p>

### Cheat Sheet · 商业语汇

1. **"Governing by non-action" (无为而治)**
**出自**：老子政治哲学，指领导者以最小干预达到治理效果；张忠谋以此形容其前任方贤齐的治理风格。
**商业造句**：*Fang Xianqi's "governing by non-action" stood in sharp contrast to Chang's activist reform agenda — and it was Fang who survived every political storm unscathed.*

2. **"Making waves" (兴波起浪)**
**中文解释**：指在组织内引发争议或动荡；张忠触自知自己以「改革者」自命，必然兴波起浪，但低估了浪头的大小。
**商业造句**：*Chang's willingness to "make waves" in a consensus-driven culture was both his greatest strength and his fatal political vulnerability.*

3. **"Harmony with people" (人和)**
**中文解释**：中国传统文化中「天时、地利、人和」的「人和」——指人际关系的和谐；在工研院改革中，「人和」成为反对改革的理由。
**商业造句**：*The charge that Chang lacked "harmony with people" was a cultural euphemism for a more basic truth: his reforms threatened too many entrenched interests.*
