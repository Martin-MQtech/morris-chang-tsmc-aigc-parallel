## 改革二：衍生公司，把人也移过去

一个研究案的成果大部分都存在研究者的脑袋里，所以最有效的「技术移转」，应该把研究者都移转到要用这技术的公司去。

一谈到移转人，马上触到工研院的敏感神经。如前文所述，工研院是一个高薪、工作有保障的准政府机构；大部分工研人自视为「研究人」，而不是「工业人」。即使他们的研究成果已转移至工业界，他们还是要待在工研院做研究；在他们心目中，工业公司与他们搭不上关系。

但即使这是同仁的心情，在我任院长的三年内，我还是尽力推动衍生公司，因为我认为衍生公司是达成工研院使命——「加速发展工业技术」的最好做法。工研院在我任院长前的十二年，只衍生了一家公司：联华电子公司（联电），转移了三十多名员工；但在我任院长的三年，我促成了五家衍生公司：

- **台积电**——自电子所转移一百多位员工。
- **盟立自动化公司**——自机械所转移一百多位员工，是台湾第一家「自动化」公司。
- **台湾光罩公司**——自电子所转移三十多位员工。
- **长荣超合金**——自材料所转移四位员工。
- **亿威电子**——自电子所转移二十多位员工。

事实上，虽然在徐贤修、俞国华找我做工研院院长时，他们从未提起，我也没有想到做了工研院院长后要衍生一家半导体公司；但在我就任两周后，李政委邀我去他办公室，在那会议中就提出衍生一家半导体公司（后来名为台积电）的可能性。后来的发展又是异常地快，在我院长任内，筹备台积电及成立台积电后的经营几乎占了我一半时间。

多年后看来，衍生这五家公司，尤其台积电，是我工研院院长任内的最大——也许唯一——成就。但在我任内，这个现在很明显的成就却在当时看不出来。

<p class="en-para"><b>Reform 2: Spin-offs — Transferring People Along with Technology</b></p>
<p class="en-para">The results of a research project reside mostly in the researchers' heads, so the most effective "technology transfer" is to transfer the researchers themselves to the company that will use the technology.</p>
<p class="en-para">The moment you talk about transferring people, you touch ITRI's raw nerve. As noted earlier, ITRI was a high-paying, secure quasi-government agency. Most ITRI people considered themselves "researchers," not "industrialists." Even when their research results had been transferred to industry, they wanted to stay at ITRI doing research. In their minds, industrial companies had nothing to do with them.</p>
<p class="en-para">But even if this was the prevailing sentiment, during my three-year presidency I still pushed for spin-offs, because I believed spin-offs were the best way to fulfill ITRI's mission: "to accelerate the development of industrial technology." In the twelve years before my presidency, ITRI had spun off only one company: United Microelectronics Corporation (UMC), transferring about thirty employees. But during my three-year presidency, I facilitated five spin-offs:</p>
<p class="en-para">• <b>TSMC</b> — over 100 employees transferred from the Electronics Institute.</p>
<p class="en-para">• <b>Mitel Automation</b> — over 100 employees from the Mechanical Institute; Taiwan's first "automation" company.</p>
<p class="en-para">• <b>Taiwan Mask Corporation</b> — over 30 employees from the Electronics Institute.</p>
<p class="en-para">• <b>Ever Superalloy</b> — four employees from the Materials Institute.</p>
<p class="en-para">• <b>EW Electronics</b> — over 20 employees from the Electronics Institute.</p>
<p class="en-para">In fact, although neither Hsu Hsiu-hsien nor Premier Yu had ever mentioned it when recruiting me, nor had I considered it myself, that becoming ITRI President would mean spinning off a semiconductor company — just two weeks after taking office, Minister Li invited me to his office and proposed the possibility of spinning off a semiconductor company (later named TSMC). Subsequent developments came with unusual speed. During my presidency, preparing for TSMC's founding and managing its early operations consumed nearly half my time.</p>
<p class="en-para">Viewed years later, spinning off these five companies — especially TSMC — was the greatest, perhaps the only, achievement of my ITRI presidency. But during my tenure, this now-obvious achievement was far from apparent at the time.</p>

### Cheat Sheet · 商业语汇

1. **Spin-off (衍生公司)**
**中文解释**：由母机构分离出去、独立运营的新公司；张忠谋认为衍生公司是技术移转最有效的方式——因为技术存在于研究者的头脑中。
**商业造句**：*Chang's conviction that spin-offs were the most effective technology transfer mechanism reflected a deep insight: knowledge is embodied in people, not in documents or patents.*

2. **TSMC (Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company，台湾积体电路制造公司)**
**中文解释**：全球第一家专业晶圆代工厂，成立于一九八七年；台积电的诞生彻底改变了半导体产业的分工模式。
**商业造句**：*TSMC's creation as a pure-play foundry — manufacturing chips for others rather than designing its own — was the most consequential spin-off in the history of the semiconductor industry.*

3. **Quasi-government agency (准政府机构)**
**中文解释**：具有政府机构的稳定性和保障，但有一定弹性的组织型态；工研院的「准政府」性格既是优势（稳定揽才），也是改革阻力。
**商业造句**：*The quasi-government structure gave ITRI the stability to attract talent, but also created the cultural inertia that made Chang's reforms so difficult to implement.*
