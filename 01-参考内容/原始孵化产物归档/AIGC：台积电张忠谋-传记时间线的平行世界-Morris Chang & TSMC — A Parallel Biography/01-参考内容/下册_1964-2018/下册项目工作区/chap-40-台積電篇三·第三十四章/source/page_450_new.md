<p class="en-para">A few weeks later I called him again, and he gave the same answer: 'I already have a job.'</p>
<p class="en-para">In 2024, when the company Jensen founded, NVIDIA, has become one of the world's most valuable companies and Jensen himself a billionaire worth hundreds of billions of dollars, readers may think I was attempting to 'reach above my station' in 2013. In fact, at the end of 2013 NVIDIA's market capitalization was only nine billion dollars, while TSMC's was already ninety billion—ten times NVIDIA's. Jensen's personal wealth in 2013 was only in the single-digit percentage range of NVIDIA's market cap. I was not reaching above my station.</p>
<p class="en-para">Jensen was honest in his answer—he 'already had a job.' His job was to build NVIDIA into what it is today, eleven years later.</p>
<p class="en-para">Sandi Jin was a very smart semiconductor R&amp;D talent with strong technical direction sense and leadership. But he was also sensitive. He joined TSMC in 1997 as the head of R&amp;D, and from the start I charged him with the mission of 'technology leadership.' I deeply respected him. But when Morris Cai became CEO in 2016, Sandi chose to retire from TSMC. I resumed the CEO role in 2019 and brought him back over 'a bowl of beef noodles' (see Chapter 32). In 2012 I had appointed three co-COOs, and Sandi was one of them, but I never implemented the 'three-person rotation' system. Sandi was evidently somewhat disappointed—I believe because he was originally familiar with R&amp;D and was transferred to an unfamiliar domain.</p>
<p class="en-para">In 2013 Sandi sensed that, in planning my own retirement, I might make further organizational adjustments, and that he might not be the central figure in the next round. He asked me not to announce any organizational changes before he reached the mandatory retirement age of sixty-seven. I agreed.</p>
<p class="en-para">Sandi retired on October 31, 2013. We held a warm retirement dinner for him. I also presented him with a plaque inscribed 'Merit to TSMC'—the only plaque I have ever used that phrasing.</p>
