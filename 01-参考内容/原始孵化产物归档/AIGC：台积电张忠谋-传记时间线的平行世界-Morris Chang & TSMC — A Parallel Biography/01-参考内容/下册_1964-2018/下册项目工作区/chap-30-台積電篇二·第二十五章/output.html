作序与写自传

一九九五年初，友人虞有澄寄了一本他写的、但尚未出版的《我看英代尔》给我，并嘱我为他作序。我问他要他说：两千字左右吧。

我花了一整天的时间读《我看英代尔》，读后百感交集。一九九五年正是英代尔"如日中天"的年代。有澄很早就加入英代尔，所以他讲的英代尔故事的前一段正是我在德州仪器竭力与英代尔竞争的时候（见第九章）。当时胜负未分，但我离开德仪后，英代尔领先了。当年这段"德仪（TI）对英代尔（Intel）"的故事，无论是公司对公司对国家，在历史上不断出现，也带来不少可歌可泣的事迹。

我读了虞着《我看英代尔》后，又花了一整天时间为有澄写序。几十年来的中文"长篇"，写起来还算顺手。

想不到这篇序竟成了我成为作者的"进身阶"。虞书出版后几个月，天下文化创办人高希均教授就来看我，劝我写自传。其实我认识高教授已有好几年，但他大概一直以为我一身洋气，不可能写中文，直到看了我为虞写的序后，才决定"试"一下。我贸然答应了。

接下来是几乎两年的前熬。回忆是享受，动笔却是煎熬。但那时我只六十几岁，体力旺盛，有时感情来了，边写边泣，可以写到深夜二、三时，第二天照样上班。终于在九七年年底，《张忠谋自传：上册——一九三一一一九六四》完成。九八年三月出版。

书发表会里，有人问高希均创办人，对我的"中文打几分"？高教授耸从他的肩膀，不经意地说："七十分左右。"

虽然高教授只打七十分，"自传上册"倒很受欢迎。它是二十几年来台湾最畅销书之一，直至今日，我还不时地拿两、三万元的版税。美国文豪海明威对想做作家青年的忠告："简单的字句，真实的感情"，似乎很有道理。

## Writing a Foreword and Writing an Autobiography

In early 1995, my friend You Yicheng sent me a pre-publication copy of his book "My View of Intel" and asked me to write a foreword. I asked him how long it should be. He said: "About two thousand words."

I spent an entire day reading "My View of Intel," and after reading it, a hundred feelings welled up. 1995 was the heyday of Intel. You Yicheng had joined Intel very early, so the earlier part of the Intel story he told was precisely the time when I was fiercely competing with Intel at Texas Instruments (see Chapter 9). At that time, the winner had not yet been decided, but after I left TI, Intel took the lead. This backthen story of "TI vs. Intel," whether company-to-country or country-to-country, has appeared repeatedly in history and given rise to many epic and moving stories.

After reading You's "My View of Intel," I spent another entire day writing the foreword for him. It was a rare "long" Chinese piece for me in decades, but it flowed smoothly enough.

I never expected this foreword would become my "stepping stone" to becoming an author. A few months after You's book was published, Mr. Commonwealth Publishing founder Professor Kao Hsi-chun came to see me and urged me to write an autobiography. Actually, I had known Professor Kao for several years, but he probably always thought I was too "Westernized" to write in Chinese. Only after reading the foreword I wrote for You did he decide to "test" me. I rashly agreed.

What followed was nearly two years of suffering. Reminiscing was enjoyable, but the actual writing was torment. But at that time I was only in my early sixties, with abundant energy. Sometimes when emotions came over me, I would write through tears until 2 or 3 in the morning, then go to work as usual the next day. Finally, at the end of 1997, "The Autobiography of Morris Chang: Volume 1—1931-1964" was completed. It was published in March 1998.

At the book launch, someone asked founder Professor Kao what "score" he would give my Chinese. Professor Kao shrugged his shoulders nonchalantly and said: "About 70 points."

Although Professor Kao only gave it 70 points, "Autobiography Volume 1" turned out to be very popular. It was one of Taiwan's best-selling books for over 20 years. To this day, I still occasionally receive twenty to thirty thousand NT dollars in royalties. The American literary giant Ernest Hemingway's advice to aspiring writers—"simple words, true feelings"—seems to make a lot of sense.

对台积电内部讲授"经营"

九六、九七年，公司规横渐大，中阶管理人员中已出现不少我不认识的面孔，我想：应该趁这时机对中、高级经理做一系列的"经营台积电"讲授；现在讲，可能十分之二、三听课的人还会注意听，甚至传授给他们的手下；现在不讲，将来公司更大，没有机会了。

于是我开课了，每星期一小时。所有高、中阶的主管都被邀请参加，共七、八十人。我很喜欢教书，但又不喜欢准备。每次上课，我都有一个题目，然后以自然会话方式讲这题目。黑板或白板是我唯一工具。有时我的教材缺乏整理和次序，但我认为一个好学生应该在自己脑子中做这整理，这样他才更有收获。

这样的讲授，大约也举行了二、三十次吧。有收获吗？有。但恐怕不在经营技术上。毕竟台积电的组织是"功能型"的——研发部、制造部……等等。各部的经营都相当专业，而我的讲授却是"一般性"的——价值、愿景、策略……等等。最大的收获是：我们在这快速成长的关键年头，把台积电的价值观，也是我们的"经营理念"落实了。

在台积电三十一年，我和公司同仁都犯了不少错误，但绝少触犯这十条"经营理念"；在极少数触犯的案例上，我们都施予严厉的惩处。

In 1996-97, the company was growing rapidly, and among middle-level managers there were already many faces I didn't recognize. I thought: I should take this opportunity to give a series of "Managing TSMC" lectures to middle and senior managers. If I lectured now, perhaps 20-30% of the audience would still pay attention and even pass it on to their subordinates. If I didn't lecture now, when the company got even bigger, there would be no opportunity.

So I started the class, one hour every week. All senior and middle-level managers were invited, totaling 70-80 people. I very much enjoyed teaching, but disliked preparation. Each class I had a topic, which I would discuss in a natural conversational manner. The blackboard or whiteboard was my only tool. Sometimes my material lacked organization and order, but I believed a good student should do this organization in his own mind—that way he would gain more.

This kind of lecturing was held perhaps twenty to thirty times. Was there gain? Yes. But probably not in management technique. After all, TSMC's organization was "functional"—R&D Department, Manufacturing Department, etc. Each department's operations were quite professional, while my lectures were "general"—values, vision, strategy, etc. The biggest gain was: during this critical year of rapid growth, we instilled TSMC's values—our "business philosophy."

During my 31 years at TSMC, my colleagues and I made many mistakes, but we rarely violated these ten "business philosophies." In the rare cases where they were violated, we imposed severe punishment.

当交大"荣誉管理讲座"教授

我在台积电内部讲授"经营"的消息，很快地传到同在新竹的交通大学。张俊彦校长及管理科学系朱博涌教授都很热心要我开一门有学分的课，朱教授还自愿做我的"助教"。"经营管理专题"课就在九八年秋季学期出现。大约有一百位学生报名，其中一大半是EMBA学生，一小半是管理科学系大学生或别的学系学生，还有几位旁听生。每星期一堂课，每堂课两小时，当中休息几分钟。我一共教了十二堂，还有两堂考试——考试都由朱教授负责，打分也由他负责。我只负责讲十二堂。

这十二堂的题目是：（一）企业愿景；（二）企业文化；（三）组织结构与计；（四）决策与授权；（五）功能性组织；（六）沟通；（七）激励；（八）绩效评估；（九）学习曲线与市场占有率；（十）策略与年度规画；（十一）开会；（十二）世界级企业。

这十二堂课包含了我所有经营经验及心得，从德仪到通用到工研院到台积电。当然，许多经验和心得是负面的，但我认为负面的教训往往比正面的经验更铭心刻骨。知道不做什么与知道做什么一样重要。

在我开始这门课时，我对我的约一百位学生说："假如十年后，有十位学生觉得我在此讲的对他有一点用处，对我这已是很好的回馈。"

如今二十余年过去，我还未遇见任何人对我说："啊，我是你当年的交大学生。"更没有人对我说："啊，你当年讲的对我很有用。"事实上，想想我自己，不要讲小学了，从初中到博士班，我也有过一百位以上老师，其中好几位影响我后来的人生。我曾经告诉过他们吗？没有。大部分在课程结束后，就变成陌生人了。

News of my internal "management" lectures at TSMC quickly spread to nearby National Chiao Tung University (NCTU) in Hsinchu. President Zhang Junyan and Professor Zhu Boyong of the Department of Management Science were very enthusiastic about having me teach a for-credit course. Professor Zhu even volunteered to be my "teaching assistant." The "Special Topics in Business Management" course appeared in the fall semester of 1998. About a hundred students enrolled, of whom more than half were EMBA students, less than half were undergraduate students from the Department of Management Science or other departments, and a few were auditors. One class per week, two hours per class, with a short break in between. I taught twelve sessions total, plus two exams—both exams were administered and graded by Professor Zhu. I was only responsible for the twelve lectures.

The twelve session topics were: (1) Corporate Vision; (2) Corporate Culture; (3) Organizational Structure and Design; (4) Decision Making and Delegation; (5) Functional Organization; (6) Communication; (7) Motivation; (8) Performance Appraisal; (9) Learning Curve and Market Share; (10) Strategy and Annual Planning; (11) Meetings; (12) World-Class Enterprise.

These twelve sessions contained all my management experience and insights, from TI to GE to ITRI to TSMC. Of course, much of the experience and many insights were negative, but I believe negative lessons are often more indelibly memorable than positive experiences. Knowing what not to do is as important as knowing what to do.

When I started this course, I told my approximately 100 students: "If, ten years from now, ten students feel that what I taught here was of some use to them, that would already be a great reward for me."

Now more than twenty years have passed, and I have still not met anyone who said to me: "Ah, I was your student at NCTU." Still less has anyone said: "Ah, what you taught back then was very useful to me." In fact, thinking about myself—not to mention elementary school—from middle school through Ph.D. programs, I too had over a hundred teachers, several of whom influenced my later life. Did I ever tell them? No. Most became strangers after the course ended.

后记

后记：补充一段在此章之初，我写到当年这段"现存强权（德仪）对新兴挑战者（英代尔）"的故事时，所感受到的强烈讽刺意味：四、五十年后的英代尔，这个几十年半导体霸主，正在面对新兴的台积电。虽然鹿死谁手尚未可测，但台积电占有优势却是明显的事实，只要台积电不自踩自己脚，应该胜算在握。难道这是我的"复仇记"吗？我没有这个意思。反正上次英代尔领先时，我已离开德仪；这次尽管我情系台积电，但无论谁得胜，我也已经退休多时了。

Epilogue: A supplementary note. Earlier in this chapter, when I wrote about the irony of the "established power (TI) vs. emerging challenger (Intel)" story from those days: forty or fifty years later, Intel, the semiconductor hegemon for decades, is now facing an emerging TSMC. Although it is still uncertain who will prevail, TSMC holds a clear advantage—as long as TSMC doesn't shoot itself in the foot, victory should be within grasp. Is this my "revenge story"? I have no such intention. After all, when Intel took the lead last time, I had already left TI. This time, although my heart is with TSMC, whoever wins, I retired long ago.
