张忠谋

台积电篇一·第十七章｜募资

寻找第二主角

俞国华院长指定台积电"五人小组"时，政府已决定出资48%。但是，俞院长有一个条件：我必须先找一家有IC技术的跨国公司，要他们做第二主要投资者；然后，政府才能去找台湾民间，台湾民间也才有信心凑足余额。

俞院长的预言百分之百正确。后来，即使飞利浦同意投资27.6%后，政府还是花了九牛二虎之力，才凑足剩余的24.1%。

一九八五年是世界半导体业历史到那时为止最不景气的一年——成长率是负16%。大部分半导体公司都求生存。在这时刻，我找他们投资一家以前从未尝试过的"专业晶圆代工"公司，而且这家公司位在当时美、日、欧半导体公司都认为是"半导体不毛之地"的台湾！

ReadShift 双语翻译

When Premier Yu Kuo-hua appointed the "Five-Person Committee" for TSMC, the government had already decided to contribute 48% of the capital. However, Premier Yu set one condition: I had to first find a multinational company with IC technology to serve as the second lead investor; only then would the government approach Taiwanese private investors, and only then would they have confidence to raise the remainder.

Premier Yu's prediction proved 100% correct. Later, even after Philips agreed to invest 27.6%, the government still had to expend tremendous effort to raise the remaining 24.1%.

1985 was the worst year in the history of the global semiconductor industry up to that point—growth rate was negative 16%. Most semiconductor companies were merely surviving. At this moment, I approached them to invest in a "dedicated foundry" company—a business model never attempted before—and one located in Taiwan, which American, Japanese, and European semiconductor firms all considered a "semiconductor barren land"!

四处碰壁

在八五年十二月及翌年一月，我还是试了。虽然那时我已相信飞利浦有投资诚意，但一切条件都还未开始谈，我不知道谈得成否。况且，我认为多找几家公司来与飞利浦竞争，绝对对我们有利。我写信或打电话给英特尔、德仪、摩托罗拉、松下、新力、迪吉多、超微、汤姆生等公司的总裁。除了松下是孙运璇先生特别要我接触，其余都是我在德仪及通用时期熟悉的。大部分都有回信，但信是千篇一律的"无兴趣"。

ReadShift 双语翻译

In December 1985 and January 1986, I still tried. Although by then I believed Philips had genuine investment interest, no terms had been discussed yet, and I didn't know if negotiations would succeed. Moreover, I believed that having multiple companies compete with Philips would definitely be to our advantage. I wrote letters or made phone calls to the CEOs of Intel, Texas Instruments, Motorola, Matsushita, Sony, DEC, AMD, and Thomson. Except for Matsushita, which Mr. Sun Yun-suan specifically asked me to approach, the rest were companies I had known well from my TI and GE days. Most responded, but the letters were uniformly "not interested."

德仪与英特尔的拒绝

除了德仪和英特尔。德仪是我的老东家，缘虽断但情犹在。德仪的半导体总经理席克（第十章曾提到他）邀我去达拉斯见面。我去了，对他及几位他的幕僚做了一个简报。当时他就说希望不大，但答应考虑并与总裁夏伯特讨论。几个月后，我与飞利浦已即将成局，席克又邀请我再去达拉斯。这次他没有幕僚陪同，单独告诉我德仪不准备投资。

英特尔的故事相似。我写信给英特尔总裁，也是我的老朋友摩尔。摩尔邀我与他的技术及生产总监巴瑞特（Craig Barrett）相会。我做了半小时左右的简报，并且回答了他的问题。后来几星期音讯全无，我就打电话找巴瑞特，找了好几个地方才找到他。他说：英特尔无兴趣投资。

现在（一九八六年五月）只剩飞利浦了——虽然飞利浦还不知道他们是唯一表达兴趣的投资者。

ReadShift 双语翻译

Except for TI and Intel. TI was my former employer—the bond was broken but the affection remained. TI's semiconductor general manager Sheek (mentioned in Chapter 10) invited me to Dallas. I went and gave a briefing to him and several of his staff. At the time he said the chances weren't great, but agreed to consider it and discuss with president Sheppard. Months later, when my negotiations with Philips were nearly concluded, Sheek invited me to Dallas again. This time he had no staff accompanying him and told me privately that TI would not invest.

Intel's story was similar. I wrote to Intel's president, my old friend Gordon Moore. Moore arranged for me to meet his technology and production director Craig Barrett. I gave about a half-hour briefing and answered his questions. Then weeks went by with no word, so I called Barrett, tracking him down through several locations. He said: Intel was not interested in investing.

By now (May 1986), only Philips remained—though Philips didn't yet know they were the sole investor to express interest.

飞利浦

飞利浦成立于一八九一年，以电灯泡起家。在二十世纪，它陆续进入家电、被动元件、半导体、光电、医疗器材等业。在一九八零年代，它是世界最大跨国公司之一，也是一家有IC技术的公司。

但是在半导体业内，无论从营收规模、技术强度或获利率角度看，飞利浦都非世界第一流。我把它排在二流行列中的第一排。这样排法虽非褒，但亦非贬。因为在我心目中，当时一流的第一排为英特尔独占；德仪、摩托罗拉只是一流的第二排。二流的第一除飞利浦外，还有人。二流的第二排以及三流，人更多。

ReadShift 双语翻译

Philips was founded in 1891, starting with light bulbs. Throughout the twentieth century, it successively entered home appliances, passive components, semiconductors, optoelectronics, and medical equipment. In the 1980s, it was one of the world's largest multinational corporations, and a company with IC technology.

However, within the semiconductor industry—whether measured by revenue scale, technology strength, or profitability—Philips was not world-class first tier. I ranked it in the first row of the second tier. This ranking was neither praise nor criticism. Because in my mind, the first row of first tier was occupied solely by Intel; TI and Motorola were merely the second row of first tier. The first row of second tier, besides Philips, had others. The second row of second tier and third tier had even more.

飞利浦的动机

这个二流一排的半导体公司，为什幺在这半导体业萧条年头，别的半导体公司都无兴趣投资台积电之时，单独有兴趣？答案有二。其一：半导体并非飞利浦主业，虽然八五年半导体萧条，但飞利浦别的业务并不萧条，所以总公司仍有足够现金可供投资。答案之二更为重要：那时中国大陆尚未开放，飞利浦总部已决定把台湾作为他们的低工资生产基地。他们本来在台湾就有IC装配测试厂，现在准备在台湾大肆建立被动元件、光电等生产基地；他们正在想如何才能获得台湾政府的青睐，恰巧碰到台湾政府招外商投资台积电，台积电就成为他们台湾扩充计划的第一炮。

把镜头移后几年。果然，在八零年代晚期及九零年代初期，飞利浦在台湾大肆扩充被动元件及光电等生产基地，雇用员工最高达四万余人。但是，九零年代中期大陆开始开放，外国公司蜂拥而入，大陆成为新的世界低工资生产基地。飞利浦也弃旧抱新，很快地从台湾搬到大陆去，在台湾只留下行销人员。今日飞利浦在台湾的员工不到一千人；对台积电的投资，也在获厚利后于二零零八年终结——但这些都是以后的故事。

ReadShift 双语翻译

Why was this tier-2 tier-1 semiconductor company—alone among all semiconductor companies—interested in investing in TSMC during an industry downturn, when others had no interest? There were two answers. First: semiconductors were not Philips' core business. Although the semiconductor sector was in recession in 1985, Philips' other businesses were not, so headquarters still had sufficient cash for investment. The second answer was more important: mainland China had not yet opened up, and Philips headquarters had already decided to make Taiwan their low-wage production base. They already had an IC assembly and test factory in Taiwan, and were now planning to aggressively build production sites for passive components, optoelectronics, and more. They were wondering how to gain the favor of the Taiwanese government—and then, by coincidence, the Taiwanese government sought foreign investment for TSMC. TSMC became the first shot of their Taiwan expansion plan.

Fast forward a few years. Indeed, in the late 1980s and early 1990s, Philips aggressively expanded its passive component and optoelectronics production bases in Taiwan, employing up to 40,000 workers at its peak. But by the mid-1990s, mainland China began opening up; foreign companies poured in, and mainland China became the world's new low-wage production base. Philips also abandoned the old for the new, quickly relocating from Taiwan to mainland China, leaving only marketing staff in Taiwan. Today, Philips employs fewer than a thousand people in Taiwan; its investment in TSMC, after reaping substantial profits, ended in 2008—but these are all stories for later.

初访荷兰

回到一九八五年。李政委告知我飞利浦有兴趣投资后，我就与飞利浦接触，接着遵他们嘱写投资建议书，并在十一月三日至六日访问荷兰飞利浦。我曾在一九六九年以德仪集成电路总经理身份访问荷兰飞利浦。那时德仪稳居世界IC业领袖，飞利浦技术落后，想买德仪的"技术授权"。我受飞利浦之邀，去考察他们是否有接受德仪技术的能力。那次访问，我以"征服者"姿态出现。

但这次却大不相同。这次我是"恳求者"，恳求飞利浦投资。角色转换，沧海桑田，使我晚上在旅舍中唏嘘不已。尤其，这次我并没有见到对方的主角——飞利浦IC总经理克列斯曼（Krigsman），他在美国。

这次访问飞利浦，给我最深印象是飞利浦正在巨额投资建两个IC厂。他们在奈梅亨建IC生产厂，同时在恩荷芬建IC"研发厂"；引导我参观的人告诉我："研发厂的规模与生产厂相似。"亲眼看到这样巨额的投资在进行式中（必在两亿美元以上），我比较放心我们的要求（四千万美元）会被接受。

ReadShift 双语翻译

Back to 1985. After Commissioner Li informed me of Philips' investment interest, I made contact with Philips, then followed their instructions to write an investment proposal, and visited Philips in the Netherlands from November 3 to 6. I had visited Philips in 1969 as TI's integrated circuit general manager. At that time, TI stood firmly as the world leader in the IC industry; Philips was behind technologically and wanted to purchase TI's "technology license." I accepted Philips' invitation to assess whether they had the capability to absorb TI's technology. On that visit, I appeared as a "conqueror."

But this time was completely different. This time I was a "supplicant," begging Philips to invest. The role reversal—the vast change of circumstances—made me sigh in my hotel room at night. Moreover, this time I did not meet the key person on their side—Philips' IC general manager Krigsman. He was in the United States.

What impressed me most during this Philips visit was that they were making enormous investments to build two IC factories. They were building an IC production facility in Nijmegen, and simultaneously an IC "R&D fab" in Eindhoven; the person guiding me told me: "The R&D fab's scale is similar to the production fab." Witnessing such massive investment firsthand (it must have exceeded $200 million), I felt more confident that our request ($40 million) would be accepted.

七个月谈判的开启

十二月二日，我去加州森尼韦尔（Sunnyvale），首次晤克列斯曼，自此展开长达七个月的谈判。在此谈判中，我代表台积电，我背后的决策者是"五人小组"，我主要助手是胡定华、史钦泰以及政府常用的联鼎法律事务所的陈国慈律师。飞利浦方面则有许多谈判者——技术、业务方面由克列斯曼负责，台湾飞利浦技术长罗益强君助理；财务、法务另有荷兰飞利浦专人负责。

ReadShift 双语翻译

On December 2, I went to Sunnyvale, California, and met Krigsman for the first time, launching a negotiation that would last seven months. In this negotiation, I represented TSMC. Behind me, the decision-makers were the "Five-Person Committee." My main assistants were Hu Ding-hua, Shi Qin-tai, and lawyer Chen Guoci of the Lian Ding law firm, commonly used by the government. On the Philips side, there were many negotiators—technology and business aspects were handled by Krigsman, assisted by Mr. Luo Yiqiang, CTO of Philips Taiwan; finance and legal affairs were handled by dedicated personnel from Philips Netherlands.

谈判重点一——公司命名

在第一次对院长简报"共同晶圆厂"时，我已暂时命名新公司为 Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company（TSMC）。我觉得这名字表达了公司的要点：（一）在台湾成立（Taiwan）＋（二）做半导体（Semiconductor）＋（三）做制造（Manufacturing）。后来的投资协议都以英文为主文；一直到公司登记，才有中文名字问题。我的第一个反应就是直译英文："台湾半导体制造公司"，但发现此名已为人登记，所以改成"台湾积体电路制造公司"，简称"台积电"。英文名字及英文简写都不必改，现在TSMC已闻名世界了。

ReadShift 双语翻译

When I first briefed the Premier on the "Common Wafer Fab," I had tentatively named the new company Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC). I felt this name captured the company's essentials: (1) established in Taiwan (Taiwan) + (2) in the semiconductor business (Semiconductor) + (3) in manufacturing (Manufacturing). Subsequent investment agreements used English as the main text; the Chinese name issue didn't arise until company registration. My first instinct was a direct translation: "Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company," but discovered this name was already registered by someone else, so we changed it to "Taiwan Integrated Circuit Manufacturing Company," abbreviated as "TSMC." The English name and acronym didn't need to change—and now TSMC is world-famous.

谈判重点二——营业范围

开始与飞利浦谈判，我就强调"专业晶圆代工"商业模式。飞利浦不但同意，而且说如果台湾要办设计公司或IDM型的公司，他们不会考虑投资，因为设计公司或IDM型公司会是他们的竞争者。既有这样共识，我们在"投资协议纲要"（Heads of Agreement）中，更清楚地界定"专业晶圆代工"的营业范围：

台积电不设计或推销自己的产品，但依照客户的设计，为客户制造IC晶圆，并提供或安排封装测试服务。

ReadShift 双语翻译

From the start of negotiations with Philips, I emphasized the "dedicated foundry" business model. Philips not only agreed, but said that if Taiwan wanted to establish a design house or IDM-type company, they would not consider investing, because a design house or IDM-type company would be their competitor. With this consensus, we more clearly defined the business scope of "dedicated foundry" in the "Heads of Agreement":

TSMC does not design or sell its own products, but manufactures IC wafers according to customers' designs, and provides or arranges packaging and testing services.

谈判重点三——技术

首晤克列斯曼（飞利浦首席谈判员）时，他就要求台积电"完全以飞利浦技术为基础"，我立刻拒绝。我从德仪时期就知道，技术是一个半导体公司的灵魂。怎幺能在公司还未组成时，就先出卖他的灵魂？接着，克列斯曼要求飞利浦以技术做股，我也拒绝了。我们要的全额现金投资（李政委还认为我要得太少，因为他预计在台湾民间募资会有困难），任何折扣都会使台湾民间募资更困难。

但是我们的确也要他的技术协助。在一九八六年一月初，工研院的技术团队（副院长胡定华、电子所所长史钦泰、副所长章青驹、组长曾繁城）去恩荷芬领略飞利浦的技术。他们回来的报告是："可以帮助我们自己的开发。"

更重要的是：飞利浦与十几家大型半导体公司交换授权；假使飞利浦拥有二五%以上台积电股权，台积电也在飞利浦的保护伞下。也就是说：台积电不会受到这十几家大公司专利权的干扰。

技术开发的帮助，以及专利权的"保护伞"，都要钱买。飞利浦也毫不客气，要求高价。经过不少折冲，我们（"五人小组"）同意在先进技术（一微米及更先进）营收内，台积电付予飞利浦三分之一权利金。工研院已开发的技术（二微米及以上），台积电不必付权利金。

ReadShift 双语翻译

At my first meeting with Krigsman (Philips' chief negotiator), he demanded that TSMC be "entirely based on Philips technology." I immediately refused. Since my TI days, I had known that technology is the soul of a semiconductor company. How could we sell the soul of a company before it was even formed? Next, Krigsman demanded that Philips contribute technology as equity. I also refused. We wanted full cash investment (Commissioner Li even thought I was asking for too little, because he anticipated difficulties raising funds from Taiwanese private investors)—any discount would make Taiwanese private fundraising even more difficult.

But we did need their technical assistance. In early January 1986, an ITRI technology team (Vice President Hu Ding-hua, Electronics Research Institute Director Shi Qin-tai, Deputy Director Zhang Qingju, and Section Chief Tseng Fan-cheng) went to Eindhoven to survey Philips' technology. Their report upon return: "Can assist our own development."

More importantly: Philips had cross-licensing agreements with over a dozen major semiconductor companies. If Philips owned more than 25% of TSMC's equity, TSMC would also be under Philips' protective umbrella. In other words: TSMC would not be harassed by the patent rights of these dozen-plus major companies.

Help in technology development, and the patent "umbrella," both cost money. Philips, without courtesy, demanded a high price. After considerable negotiations, we (the "Five-Person Committee") agreed that TSMC would pay Philips a one-third royalty on revenue from advanced technology (one micron and below). For technology already developed by ITRI (two microns and above), TSMC would pay no royalty.

谈判重点四——独立性

一旦避免了"以飞利浦技术"做基础，我就要强调台积电的独立性。我不要台积电成为飞利浦的子公司，政府也不要台积电成为"国营"或"国有"公司。所以在"协议纲要"上我们明白说明：

台积电是一个独立事业，她的成长和获利的优先考量，与独立的美国IT C公司一样。

与独立美国ITC公司相比拟，是刚自美国来台湾的我决定的。

ReadShift 双语翻译

Having avoided being "based on Philips technology," I then emphasized TSMC's independence. I did not want TSMC to become a Philips subsidiary, and the government did not want TSMC to become a "state-run" or "state-owned" enterprise. So in the "Heads of Agreement" we clearly stated:

TSMC is an independent enterprise. Its priority considerations for growth and profitability are the same as those of an independent American ITC company.

The comparison to an independent American ITC company was my own decision, as someone who had just come to Taiwan from the United States.

谈判重点五——人事权

人事权在没有异议下达成协议：

董事会十人：政府指派四人，飞利浦指派三人，其余股东指派两人，总经理兼董事一人。

如政府持股在二五%以上，政府指定董事长。最大股东提名总经理，由董事会决定。

总经理为公司行政首长（CEO）。

第二大股东提名财务副总。

监察人三名。政府及飞利浦各指定一人，其他股东指定一人。

总经理并列，而且总经理是行政首长，这些规定都属于标准欧美公司体制——也是飞利浦及我习惯的体制。

ReadShift 双语翻译

Personnel authority was agreed upon without dissent:

The board would consist of ten members: four appointed by the government, three appointed by Philips, two appointed by other shareholders, and one serving as both general manager and director.

If the government held more than 25% of shares, the government would appoint the chairman. The largest shareholder would nominate the general manager, subject to board approval.

The general manager would be the company's chief executive (CEO).

The second largest shareholder would nominate the financial vice president.

Three supervisors: one each appointed by the government and Philips, and one by other shareholders.

That the general manager would also be a board member, and that the general manager would be the chief executive—these provisions were all standard European and American corporate governance—the system both Philips and I were accustomed to.

谈判重点六——飞利浦享受股票承购权

开启飞利浦谈判不久，赵耀东主委就对我说："他们（飞利浦）一定会要求股票承购权。"赵主委富有与国外公司合资经验，他的话也让我有心理准备。果然，在一切都已大致谈定，双方准备签署"协议纲要"时，飞利浦突然提出股票承购权，建议如下：

（一）最早一九八九年九月三十日，飞利浦可提出承购要求，增加飞利浦持股至五一%，"政府股东"及"其他股东"必须转让股票给飞利浦；如"其他股东"不愿，则"政府股东"必须转让给飞利浦。

Chairman Zhao had extensive experience in joint ventures with foreign companies, and his words gave me mental preparation. I accepted (1) but rejected (2). My counter-proposal to Philips was:

(2) Transfer price determined by three factors given equal consideration: (A) the company's net asset value at transfer. (B) The company's earning power and profit forecast at transfer. (C) The price-to-earnings ratio of American semiconductor companies listed on U.S. stock exchanges.

My counter-proposal stunned Philips. It was May 27, 1986. The Philips president had already scheduled a visit to Taiwan on June 9 to sign the "Heads of Agreement." Almost everything else had been settled, and the Philips negotiation team did not want to let this potentially moot point (if TSMC failed, Philips wouldn't want to increase its stake) block their first step in making Taiwan a primary production base.

After hasty negotiations, the final transfer price conditions were: (A) the company's net asset value at transfer. (B) The company's earning power and profit forecast at transfer. (C) The price-to-book ratio of American semiconductor companies listed on U.S. stock exchanges.

The final agreement was not far from my counter-proposal. At that time, (C) the price-to-book ratio was approximately 2.5x to 10x, so the final agreement was far more favorable to the Development Fund than Philips' original proposal. Philips had always been renowned for its negotiation skills, but on this matter, we won.

谈判重点七——营业计划

我与胡定华、史钦泰等工研院同仁，花了不少时间准备台积电十年详细营业计划。克列斯曼仔细地看了，认为我们的售价太乐观。飞利浦很可能是我们未来客户，他做此言，我认为大有自私意味在，但我无意与他做冗长辩论。我们稍事调整计划内的售价，但仍呈示不错的获利及投资回收。详细的营业计划附录在"协议纲要"里。

在这个计划里，我们前五年的营收年复合成长率为六四%，营业利益率除了在第一年以外，可以由单位数成长到一九九二零年的二五%左右。而实际上，台积电前五年的营收年复合成长率为一三零%，营业利益率除了第一年以及产业不景气的一九九零年以外，都是双位数。

"营业计划"的假设是我们只有一厂及二厂，所以从九二年起，二厂成熟后，营收就不再成长。"真实"的数字代表我们后来继续建三厂、四厂。

"投资协议纲要"与"技术合作协议"

七个月的谈判，有时顺利，有时痛苦，但我一直抱着"非谈成不可"的态度。如果谈不成，飞利浦不投资，我们就办不成台积电，工研院的IC计划也会走到山穷水尽地步。抱着这样"可忍则忍"的心怀，终于谈成了与飞利浦的"投资协议纲要"。"五人小组"同意后，我与飞利浦总裁在一九八六年六月签署。

有了飞利浦的投资承诺，我们满足了俞院长的台湾募资条件，政府也就展开了台湾募资。几个月后，股款凑足（一亿四千五百万美元，相当于新台币五十五亿元），全体投资人在十月签署"股东协议书"。这"股东协议书"完全保存了"投资协议纲要"的要点。

也就是说，七个月的谈判造就了台积电，也决定了台积电头六年的经营模式。一直到一九九二年十月，台积电决定申请上市，原始股东在"股东协议书"的私自约定不可能在上市时成立。而且原始股东（包括行政院开发基金及飞利浦）嗅到了上市后股票增值的诱惑，才决定解除原来的"股东协议"。
