## 布鲁克接任总经理

一九九零年秋魏谋辞职时，刚巧我自台积电募资时就属意的总经理人选——布鲁克出现，填补了魏谋遗留下来的空缺。

布鲁克是我德仪多年下属。我在一九六六年就认识他，他也是本书第六章里"一个危机，两个英雄"的英雄之一。在"危机"后他在我手下扶摇直上，做到主管MOS的副总裁位。直到一九八三年德仪消费者产品失败而士气低落、人心背离之时，布鲁克离开德仪，跑到一家不很成功的中型半导体公司快捷半导体任该公司总裁。

他在快捷做了几年后，快捷的股东斯伦贝谢公司（Schlumberger）仍不满意的业绩，要把快捷卖掉。布鲁克想把快捷卖给日本电脑及半导体公司富士通（Fujitsu）。但在一九八零年代，日本半导体业很强盛，几乎有超过美国半导体业之势，深为美国政府所忌。因此，美国政府把快捷半导体与富士通的合并计划反对掉了。斯伦贝谢急于脱手，就以低价把快捷卖给另一家美国公司——国家半导体（National Semiconductor）。在买卖的过程中，国家半导体并没有挽留布鲁克。布鲁克就此失业。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">In the fall of 1990, when Wei Mou resigned, as it happened the general manager candidate I had favored since TSMC's fundraising days—Brook—appeared, filling the vacancy left by Wei Mou.</p>
<p class="en-para">Brook had been my subordinate at TI for many years. I met him in 1966. He was also one of the two "heroes" in "One Crisis, Two Heroes" from Chapter 6 of this book. After the "crisis," he rose rapidly under me, reaching the position of vice president in charge of MOS. Until 1983, when TI's consumer products failure caused low morale and talent drain, Brook left TI and went to a middling semiconductor company—National Semiconductor (he became president of the company).</p>
<p class="en-para">After a few years at National Semiconductor, the company's shareholder, Schlumberger, was still dissatisfied with the performance and wanted to sell the company. Brook wanted to sell the company to the Japanese computer and semiconductor company Fujitsu. But in the 1980s, the Japanese semiconductor industry was so strong it nearly surpassed the American semiconductor industry, which aroused deep concern from the U.S. government. Therefore, the U.S. government opposed the merger plan between National Semiconductor and Fujitsu. Schlumberger, eager to exit, sold off National Semiconductor at a low price to another American company—National Semiconductor (the name was confusing, but it was a different entity). During this transaction, National Semiconductor did not retain Brook. Brook was unemployed.</p>
</div>

## 合适的人选

布鲁克虽是工程出身，但他的长处是市场、行销及经营。他的这些长处正与台积电初期的策略契合，所以在一九八六年台积电募资成功，要找总经理时，我的第一批人选就有他在内。但那时他不愿离开快捷半导体。一九九零年我再去找他时，他已失业两年，当然态度不同，但他虽然接受我的邀请，却对我声明：他不认台积电总经理职位为他长期归宿，而且他也不愿长期住在台湾，但答应我做一年——一年后再说。

一九九零年十二月，我们正式宣布布鲁克为任总经理。我不久前才发生的工研院院长、董事长交接典礼的风光，许多媒体记者都来了，第二天报纸上也有相当显著的登载；那么，现在台积电的新总经理也应有相似的威风吧？我们开了一个晚会，也邀请了不少记者，但是，到的只有一个！我相当沮丧，也对人事经理把晚会及邀请传达得很草率感到不满。人事经理倒很老实地告诉我："不要以为我们是工研院。台湾像我们这样的公司，不知道有几百个。"

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">Although Brook was trained as an engineer, his strengths were in marketing, sales, and operations. These strengths aligned perfectly with TSMC's initial strategies. So when TSMC's fundraising succeeded in 1986 and we were looking for a general manager, Brook was among my first choices. But at that time he was unwilling to leave National Semiconductor. When I approached him again in 1990, he had been unemployed for two years, so naturally his attitude was different. But although he accepted my invitation, he made it clear: he did not consider the TSMC general manager position his long-term home, and he did not want to live in Taiwan long-term. But he agreed to do it for one year—and then we'd see.</p>
<p class="en-para">In December 1990, we officially announced Brook as the new general manager. I had just recently had a grand ITRI presidentchairman transition ceremony—many media reporters came, and the next day's newspapers had quite prominent coverage. Surely TSMC's new general manager should have similar fanfare? We held a reception and invited quite a few reporters, but only one showed up! I was quite dejected and unhappy with the sloppiness of the event and the invitations. The personnel manager told me frankly: "Don't think we're ITRI. There are hundreds of companies like us in Taiwan."</p>
</div>

## "一百亿"呼喊

使我振奋的倒是这晚会的另一个镜头。举行晚会时一九九零年即将结束，当年的营收为新台币二十二亿元。我带领到会的二、三十位台积电高级主管高呼："一百亿！"第一次呼喊时，声量已经很大，但布鲁克——晚会的主角，根本不懂我们喊了什么，急着他旁边的人，我等他听懂后，再带领大家呼喊第二次"一百亿！"布鲁克也喊了，他的美国口音特别令人注意。我再喊一次"一百亿"，大家回应"一百亿！"真的声量如雷，墙壁都似乎震动了。

一九九年底，我已有很强的信心："筚路蓝缕"时代已结束，我们已走上成功之路。不要说一百亿元是一个可达到的目标，即使一千亿元也不是奢求了。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">What actually lifted my spirits was another moment from the reception. During the reception, 1990 was about to end, with revenue for the year at NT$2.2 billion. I led the twenty to thirty TSMC senior managers present in shouting: "One hundred billion!" At the first shout, the volume was already loud, but Brook—the guest of honor—had no idea what we were shouting, and asked the person next to him. I waited until he understood, then led everyone in shouting "One hundred billion!" a second time. Brook shouted too, and his American accent was particularly noticeable. I shouted "One hundred billion" once more, and everyone responded "One hundred billion!" The volume was truly thunderous, the walls seemed to shake.</p>
<p class="en-para">By year-end 1990, I already had a strong confidence: the "trailblazing" era was over, and we were on the path to success. Not to mention that one hundred billion was an achievable target—even one thousand billion would not be an extravagant wish.</p>
</div>

## 黄金年代即将开始

写此书时，已九十岁矣！回忆此生，如以每十年为一单位，则每单位都有高潮和低潮，兴奋和沮丧，期待和失落。读者读至此，应该亦可领会。但如要我挑选我的黄金时代，我毫不犹豫挑选六十至八十五岁那两个半单位。在那时段，我充分保持了青、中年就享受的健康；我精力充沛，每星期工作和上学阅读或写作时间大概总在六十小时以上；出国频繁，大部分是开关新客户或拜访现有客户，但也不少次参加国际会议，和聆听各国政要或专家学者讨论国际大事。

健康和精力固然是黄金时代的必要条件。有了健康和精力后，我深信我的专业知识、经验和判断力在六十到八十五岁时期都已成熟。德仪给了我世界级的训练，虽然在颇快情形下辞职，但它给我的训练，我至今感恩！通用和投资人的经验，也让我看到科技业的金融面——这是我在德仪二十五年没有看到的；工研院又教我不少台湾的"人情世故"，是我后来经营台积电不可或缺的经验。

比知识、经验、判断力更重要的，我坚强了自己的斗志。六十岁以后，三次辞职后，我认为已经退无可退，而必须坚强自己的斗志。其实我六十至八十五岁这时的困难和阻力也不少，但我不再认输不再放弃。

当然，构成我"黄金时代"最重要因素是：台积电成功了！台积电从一个几乎没人看好的开始，在三十年光景，它成为一个没人能忽略的公司。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">Writing this book at the age of ninety! Looking back on my life, if we take each decade as a unit, every unit had its highs and lows, excitement and frustration, anticipation and disappointment. Having read this far, the reader should understand. But if I were to pick my golden era, I would unhesitatingly pick those two and a half units from ages sixty to eighty-five. During that period, I fully maintained the health I had enjoyed since youth and middle age. I was energetic, with weekly work and serious reading or writing time totaling about sixty hours. I traveled abroad frequently—mostly to open up new clients or visit existing ones, but also attending international conferences and listening to political leaders and experts from various countries discussing world affairs.</p>
<p class="en-para">Health and energy were of course necessary conditions for a golden era. With health and energy, I firmly believe my professional knowledge, experience, and judgment all matured during the sixty to eighty-five period. TI gave me world-class training. Although I resigned under hasty circumstances, I remain grateful for the training it gave me! My GE and venture capital experience also showed me the financial side of the technology industry—something I had not seen during my twenty-five years at TI. ITRI taught me much about "human relations and worldly wisdom" in Taiwan, experience indispensable to my later management of TSMC.</p>
<p class="en-para">More important than knowledge, experience, and judgment was the fact that I strengthened my fighting spirit. After age sixty, after three resignations, I believed I could retreat no further and had to fortify my fighting spirit. In truth, there were plenty of difficulties and obstacles during this sixty to eighty-five period, but I refused to admit defeat and refused to give up.</p>
<p class="en-para">Of course, the most important factor constituting my "golden era" was: TSMC succeeded! From a beginning that almost no one believed in, within thirty years, it became a company that no one could ignore.</p>
</div>
