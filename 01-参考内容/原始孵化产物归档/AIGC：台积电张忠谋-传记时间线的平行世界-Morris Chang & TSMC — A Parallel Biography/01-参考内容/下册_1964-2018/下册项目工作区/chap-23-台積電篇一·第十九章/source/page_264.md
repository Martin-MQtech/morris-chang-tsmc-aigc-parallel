# 台积电篇一·第十九章｜筚路蓝缕

## 一厂

一九八七年二月底，台积电公司登记完成，就在"一厂"开张营业。一厂是一座两层楼加地下室的工研院建筑。工研院本拟以此作为新的IC实验工厂，现在衍生了台积电，就把此建筑物，连同已买进的若干制造设备，一起租赁给台积电。一楼是工厂。工研院已买进的制造设备并不完整，台积电在头两年花了四千多万美元添置制造设备，把产能凑加到每月一万三千片六吋晶圆。

地下室是员工餐厅，一楼部分及二楼是办公室。除了作业员全时在一楼工厂工作外，其余员工在一、二楼有办公桌，几位高层经理有各自办公室，还有几间公用会议室。这么多人都在一层楼上，相当挤。我的正职仍为工研院院长，虽非常关心台积电，且常来"一厂"开会，但的确不需要在台积电有办公室，当然也不领台积电报酬。一直到一九八八年年底，我成为工研院董事长，就不领工研院任何薪资，只领台积电薪资。

<p class="en-para">## Fab 1</p>
<p class="en-para">At the end of February 1987, TSMC completed its company registration and opened for business in "Fab 1." Fab 1 was a two-story ITRI building with a basement. ITRI had originally intended this building as a new IC experimental factory. Now that TSMC had been spun out, ITRI leased this building to TSMC along with certain manufacturing equipment already purchased. The first floor was the factory. The manufacturing equipment ITRI had purchased was incomplete; TSMC spent over $40 million in the first two years adding manufacturing equipment, bringing capacity up to 13,000 six-inch wafers per month.</p>
<p class="en-para">The basement was the employee cafeteria; portions of the first floor and the second floor were offices. Except for operators who worked full-time on the factory floor, the rest of the employees had desks on the first and second floors. Several senior managers had their own offices, plus a few shared conference rooms. So many people on one floor rather cramped. My primary position was still ITRI president. Although I was very concerned about TSMC and often came to "Fab 1" for meetings, I really didn't need an office at TSMC and of course drew no salary from TSMC. It wasn't until the end of 1988, when I became ITRI chairman, that I stopped receiving any salary from ITRI and only drew TSMC compensation.</p>
</div>

## 戴克斯上任一把火

在工作积极度上，戴克斯一点都不负我的期望。一上任，他积极推动我的两个策略：开关美国市场和巩固生产能力。他先聘雇美国行销经理，此人名匹列切（Steve Pletcher），是戴在GE时的行销经理。匹住在佛罗里达州，而我们的未来客户大部分都在西岸，所以台积电的第一年，匹几乎每周都飞加州拜访客户，住在加州旅馆里，周末又飞回佛州家。如此一年余，直到第二年（一九八八年），我们才在加州圣荷西城租了办公室，并招雇新的行销工程师。在第一年内，匹每两、三个月来台湾一次，对戴及我报告他的进度。在第一年内他没有带回订单，但每次我问他对我们的"专业晶圆代工"是否可行？他总竖起他的大拇指，乐观地说一定可行。

<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">In terms of work initiative, Dexter did not disappoint my expectations at all. Upon taking office, he aggressively pushed my two strategies: opening up the U.S. market and strengthening production capabilities. He first hired an American marketing manager named Steve Pletcher, who had been Dexter's marketing manager at GE. Pletcher lived in Florida, while most of our future customers were on the West Coast, so for TSMC's first year, Pletcher flew to California almost every week to visit clients, staying in California motels and flying back to Florida on weekends. This continued for over a year, until the second year (1988), when we rented an office in San Jose, California and hired new engineering marketing staff. During the first year, Pletcher came to Taiwan every two to three months to report his progress to Dexter and me. He didn't bring back any orders in the first year, but whenever I asked him whether our "dedicated foundry" model would work, he would always give a thumbs-up and optimistically say it definitely would.</p>
</div>

## 魏谋与核心团队缺口

戴克斯第二个重要聘雇是营运副总魏谋。魏谋德裔美籍，半导体工程博士，在德仪研发单位实验线当主任工程师。我在德仪时魏也在，但他的职阶低我好几级，所以我并不认识他。现在戴克斯把他找来，要我面试，我觉得他无论知识或经验，都足以执行我的第二策略——巩固生产能力，所以同意雇他。他成为以曾繁城为首、从工研院过来的核心团队的上司。起先，曾繁城有点不舒服，但几个月后，也看出在生产技术上，魏有可指导曾及核心团队之处，所以曾原来的不舒服也就消失了。

但是核心团队有一个缺洞——无人做行销。本来工研院的实验工厂就没有行销人员，所有的生意都是客户闻名而来。现在，台积电的名气不如工研院，我们要登客户门求生意。事实上，一九八七年台积电百分之百营收，一九八八年七六%营收均来自台湾客户。我的美欧市场策略，匹列切的大拇指，一直到两年后才有效著效果。这两年，台积电靠蔡志群的行销。后来蔡志群也成为长期台积人，直到二零二二年初，才从台积电亚太行销业务主管职务退休。

虽然戴克斯态度积极而且乐观，但是，没有生意还是没有生意。虽然我们有每月几千片（六吋）产能，第一年每月通常只有几百片生意。记得魏谋加入后几星期，我问他："何时可以填满产能？"他恨恨地回答："和遥遥无期。"

第一年结账，营收四百万美元，只是募资时营业计划的三分之一；营业亏损五百四十万美元，倒低于营业计划的亏损七百三十万美元。这是因为年中就预测营收不能达标，处处节省之故。

这是相当暗淡的一年，以后还有几年暗淡；但是，第一个大突破就在转弯角上。

<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">Dexter's second key hire was COO Wei Mou. Wei Mou was German-American, a Ph.D. in semiconductor engineering who had served as a staff engineer at TI's R&amp;D pilot line. When I was at TI, Wei was also there, but his rank was several levels below mine, so I didn't know him. Now Dexter brought him in for me to interview. I felt that his knowledge and experience were sufficient to execute my second strategy—strengthening production capabilities—so I agreed to hire him. He became the boss of the core team that had come from ITRI with Zeng Fan-cheng at its head. At first, Zeng Fan-cheng felt somewhat uncomfortable, but after a few months, he could see that in production technology, Wei had things to teach Zeng and the core team, so Zeng's initial discomfort dissipated.</p>
<p class="en-para">However, the core team had a critical gap—no one doing marketing. Originally, ITRI's experimental factory had no marketing personnel at all; all business came to them by reputation. Now, TSMC's reputation was not as strong as ITRI's; we had to go to customers' doors to solicit business. In fact, in 1987, 100% of TSMC's revenue came from Taiwanese clients; in 1988, 76% still came from Taiwan. My U.S.-Europe market strategy and Pletcher's thumbs-up didn't show significant results until two years later. During these two years, TSMC relied on Cai Zhi-qun's marketing. Later, Cai Zhi-qun also became a long-term TSMC employee, retiring only in early 2022 from his position as head of TSMC's Asia-Pacific marketing business.</p>
<p class="en-para">Although Dexter's attitude was aggressive and optimistic, nonetheless, no business was no business. Although we had capacity of several thousand wafers (6-inch) per month, in the first year we typically only had a few hundred wafers of business per month. I remember a few weeks after Wei Mou joined, I asked him: "When can we fill capacity?" He replied ruefully: "It's nowhere in sight."</p>
<p class="en-para">First-year revenue was $4 million, only one-third of the business plan's projection at fundraising. Operating loss was $5.4 million, which was actually less than the projected $7.3 million loss, because mid-year we forecast revenue would miss target and cut costs everywhere.</p>
<p class="en-para">This was a rather bleak year, and there would be more bleak years to come; but the first major breakthrough was just around the corner.</p>
</div>
