## 英特尔来敲门

一九八七年十一月，我忽然接到友人虞有澄兄的通知，说安迪·葛洛夫要来台湾，顺便想参观台积电。

虞有澄是当时英特尔资深副总；葛洛夫是当时英特尔执行副总，次年即将接总裁位；而英特尔那时虽尚未是最高营收半导体公司（德仪仍是），但技术领先群伦，已大有业界龙头气势。我与虞及葛洛夫都熟悉，当然不会轻慢虞的要求。只是我纳闷：英特尔去年才拒绝了我们的募资要求，现在我们需要他们的投资仍办成了公司，怎么他们忽然有兴趣要参观我们？难道他们那么快就要用我们了？

当然我要好好接待葛洛夫，但是他要来那天恰巧我已有先约要在美国，所以我仔细交代戴克斯，好在戴与葛洛夫也相当熟悉。

我返台后，戴对我报告：葛的参观非常顺利。看到我们的工厂后，葛惊呼："居然你们真的有一座运转的六吋晶圆厂！"戴让葛看我们的良率纪录，葛也相当佩服，并且透露了一点英特尔"可能用得着台积电"的意愿。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">In November 1987, I suddenly received word from my friend You Yicheng (Eugene) that Andy Grove would be coming to Taiwan and wanted to visit TSMC.</p>
<p class="en-para">You Yicheng was then Intel Senior Vice President; Grove was then Executive Vice President of Intel and would become president the following year. Although Intel was not yet the highest-revenue semiconductor company (TI still was), its technology led by a wide margin and it had already developed an aura of industry leader. I knew both You and Grove well, and of course would not take You's request lightly. But I puzzled: Intel had rejected our fundraising request only last year, and now that we had formed the company without their investment, why were they suddenly interested in visiting us? Could they really need us that quickly?</p>
<p class="en-para">Of course I needed to receive Grove well, but it happened that on the day he was to visit I already had a prior commitment in the U.S. So I gave Dexter careful instructions, and fortunately Dexter also knew Grove fairly well.</p>
<p class="en-para">After I returned to Taiwan, Dexter reported to me: Grove's visit went very smoothly. Upon seeing our factory, Grove exclaimed: "You actually do have an operating six-inch fab!" Dexter showed Grove our yield records, which Grove quite admired, and even disclosed a little of Intel's "possible need for TSMC."</p>

## 英特尔的考验

几个月后，英特尔真的来与我们谈生意了。第一个生意是一.五微米的微控制器，英特尔两代（约三年）前的产品。英特尔因为要把他们的生产资源用在更先进的产品上，所以要我们代工。对我们来说，一.五微米正是我们最先进的技术，所以这是很好的机会让我们量产刚在开发的技术。

葛洛夫非常重视生产。在英特尔自己的工厂里，假使两个厂生产同一产品，他要求这两厂必须使用"完全相同"（copy exactly）的制程。现在要我们生产他三年前出炉的产品，他还是不肯把他的制程告诉我们，而要用我们自己的、刚开发出来的制程"试制"他的产品，让他在良率、品质及可靠度上决定是否及格。这个考试我们通过了。

再下来的对我们更为重要。英特尔要我们建立整套世界级生产制度，包括统计制程控制（Statistical Process Control）、品质控制、设备预防维修等等。我们花了一、两年，但在二厂开始量产时（一九九零年），也完成了。英特尔交给我们第一个产品一年后，又在次年把一.二微米DRAM交给我们代工，要我们走同样的步骤。

英特尔的两宗生意，在台积电创始后的第二及第三年发生。对台积电生产团队，这是一个醍醐灌顶的洗礼！从一个虽良率很高，但只做本地低阶产品的团队，他们证明了他们"继续学习"的能力，他们开始进入"世界级"了。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">A few months later, Intel really came to talk business with us. The first deal was a 1.5-micron microcontroller, Intel's product from about two generations (approximately three) years earlier. Intel needed us to manufacture it because they wanted to deploy their production resources on more advanced products. For us, 1.5 microns was our most advanced technology, so this was a great opportunity to mass-produce our newly developed technology.</p>
<p class="en-para">Grove took manufacturing extremely seriously. In Intel's own fabs, if two fabs made the same product, he demanded that they use "identical" (copy exactly) processes. Now, having us manufacture a product that had debuted three years ago, he still refused to tell us his process. Instead, he wanted us to "trial manufacture" his product using our own, newly developed process, and then he would judge whether we were up to standard in yield, quality, and reliability. We passed this exam.</p>
<p class="en-para">What followed was even more important for us. Intel required us to establish a complete world-class production system, including Statistical Process Control, quality control, equipment preventive maintenance, and more. This took us a year or two, but by the time Fab 2 began volume production (1990), it was complete. A year after Intel gave us the first product, the following year they also gave us a 1.2-micron DRAM to manufacture, going through the same steps.</p>
<p class="en-para">Intel's two deals occurred in TSMC's second and third years. For TSMC's production team, this was a revelatory baptism! From a team that, despite having high yield, only made low-end local products, they demonstrated their "ability to continue learning" and began entering the "world-class" ranks.</p>
</div>

## 英特尔的验证

英特尔后，飞利浦、德仪、摩托罗拉陆续找我们代工。德仪和摩托罗拉找我们代工的原因和英特尔相似。飞利浦则是因为我们良率比飞利浦自己厂的良率高。我为台积电募资时看到的荷兰飞利浦新厂，其实是克列斯曼的"最后一搏"。后来向台积电购买的价格比自己制造成本低，克列斯曼的职位也敲起警钟。

英特尔的两宗生意，也是对我们"价值观"的考验。有相当长的时期，这生意赔本。但我们的价值观是"不计代价，实现承诺"，咬紧牙关做下去。英特尔也委托另一家公司代工。那家公司因赔本而半途放弃，总经理还以大拇指及食指做成一个圆圈（象征银钱），对英特尔说："这是我们的价值。"与台积电多么不同！

对我，英特尔的生意是一个莫大的鼓励。它验证了"专业晶圆代工"商业模式；也验证我们"巩固生产能力"及"进军美、欧市场"策略的正确性；它也成功考验了我们的价值观。在创立的三、四年内，台积电已塑造了它未来成功的雏型。八八、八八、八年的业绩还不是很好，但在我心中，台积电的成功已不是一个问号。

<div class="rebook-translation">
<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">After Intel, Philips, TI, and Motorola successively came to us for foundry services. TI and Motorola's reasons for coming were similar to Intel's. Philips came because our yield was higher than that of Philips' own fab. The new Philips fab in the Netherlands that I had seen while fundraising for TSMC was actually Krigsman's "last stand." Later, buying from TSMC cost less than manufacturing it themselves, and Krigsman's position began to ring alarm bells.</p>
<p class="en-para">Intel's two deals were also a test of our "values." For a considerable period, these deals were conducted at a loss. But our value was "deliver on promises at all costs," so we gritted our teeth and carried on. Intel also outsourced manufacturing to another company. That company gave up midway because of losses; even the general manager made a circle with his thumb and forefinger (symbolizing money) and told Intel: "This is what we value." How different from TSMC!</p>
<p class="en-para">For me, Intel's business was an enormous encouragement. It validated the "dedicated foundry" business model; it validated the correctness of our "strengthen production capabilities" and "enter U.S. and European markets" strategies; and it successfully tested our values. Within three or four years of founding, TSMC had shaped the prototype of its future success. The performance in '88, '89, '90 was not yet great, but in my heart, TSMC's success was no longer a question mark.</p>
</div>
