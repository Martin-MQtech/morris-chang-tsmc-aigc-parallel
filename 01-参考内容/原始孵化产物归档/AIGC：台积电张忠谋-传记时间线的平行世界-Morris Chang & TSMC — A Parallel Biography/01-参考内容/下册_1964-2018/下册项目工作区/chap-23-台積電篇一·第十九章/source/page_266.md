## 戴克斯犯错

戴克斯是一个不错的总经理，但在第一年底，他犯了一个错误——他低估了飞利浦在台积电的影响力。根据"投资协议"，飞利浦有权任命台积电财务副总。在台积电创始时，飞利浦派了一位以前在飞利浦高雄厂的财务主任H君过来。H对戴克斯报告。第一年底，戴克斯对H表现不满，要换人。他应该先与飞利浦高层商量，但他没有这样做。他直接请H回飞利浦。

H不服，写信给台积电的董监事，诉说戴克斯的不是。此时戴克斯和我想请飞利浦再派一位财务长过来。但飞利浦不肯，他们要先追究戴的错。搞了几个月后，戴在压力下辞职，飞利浦另自荷兰总部派一位财务长来台积电。

戴克斯在压力下辞职，使我相当灰心。两年前，我在三、四个人拒绝我后，才找到戴克斯。戴完全认同我主导的商业模式、策略和价值，而且执行不遗余力。一年后就失去他，一时也找不到适当的人。幸而他已雇了营运副总——魏谋，就让魏谋试做总经理吧！

魏谋是一个工程师，十分认同"巩固生产能力"策略，七、八分认同"专业晶圆代工"模式，对"诚信"价值观也无意见，但他相当轻视市场及行销，好像以为只要有技术、能制造，客户就会自己找上门。反正我们已有美国及台湾行销经理。起初我有点担心匹列切，但匹确实告诉我：他至少短期内不会离开。

戴克斯个人的逆运很快好转。他离台几个月后，忽然飞利浦宣布：已聘戴克斯为Signetics（飞利浦的美国子公司）总经理。Signetics的规模，比当时台积电至少大好几倍，而且位在美国——戴克斯的祖国。所以对戴克斯而言，Signetics不失为好的归宿。

<p class="en-para">Dexter was a decent general manager, but by the end of his first year, he made a mistake—he underestimated Philips' influence within TSMC. Under the "Investment Agreement," Philips had the right to appoint TSMC's financial vice president. When TSMC was founded, Philips dispatched a former finance manager from its Kaohsiung factory, Mr. H, to TSMC. H reported to Dexter. At the end of the first year, Dexter was dissatisfied with H's performance and wanted to replace him. He should have first consulted with senior Philips management, but he didn't. He directly asked H to return to Philips.</p>
<p class="en-para">H不服 (was not convinced) and wrote to TSMC's directors and supervisors, complaining about Dexter's wrongdoing. By this time, Dexter and I wanted to ask Philips to send another CFO. But Philips refused; they wanted to first hold Dexter accountable for his mistake. After several months of deadlock, Dexter resigned under pressure, and Philips sent another CFO from its Netherlands headquarters to TSMC.</p>
<p class="en-para">Dexter's resignation under pressure made me quite disheartened. Two years earlier, only after three or four people had rejected me did I find Dexter. Dexter completely identified with the business model, strategies, and values I championed, and executed them tirelessly. Now after only one year I had lost him, and I couldn't immediately find a suitable replacement. Fortunately, he had already hired the COO—Wei Mou—so let Wei Mou try being general manager!</p>
<p class="en-para">Wei Mou was an engineer who strongly identified with the "strengthen production capabilities" strategy, identified about 70-80% with the "dedicated foundry" model, and had no issue with the "integrity" value. But he rather looked down on marketing and sales, as though he thought that as long as you had technology and could manufacture, customers would come knocking on their own. In any case, we had both American and Taiwanese marketing managers. At first I worried a bit about Pletcher, but Pletcher assured me that he would not leave, at least in the short term.</p>
<p class="en-para">Dexter's personal fortunes soon took a for the better. A few months after he left Taiwan, Philips suddenly announced: it had hired Dexter as general manager of Signetics (Philips' American subsidiary). Signetics was at least several times larger than TSMC at the time, and it was located in the U.S.—Dexter's home country. So for Dexter, Signetics was not a bad destination.</p>
