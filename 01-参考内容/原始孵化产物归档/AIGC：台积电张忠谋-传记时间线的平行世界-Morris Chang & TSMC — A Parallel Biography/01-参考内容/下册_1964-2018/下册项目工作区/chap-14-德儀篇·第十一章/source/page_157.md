## 美日记忆体良率差异分析

一九八一年，德仪日本美浦厂开始生产德仪休士顿厂已在生产的记忆体。忽然，消息传来——美浦厂的良率是休士顿厂的一倍以上（那时休士顿厂良率大约百分之二十几，美浦厂却做到四、五〇%）。

夏伯特震惊了！命令休士顿厂立即改进！把休士顿的工程师弄得人仰马翻，不知道做什么才能改进。我作为「品质与生产力总监」也被嘱「看一下」。

其实我早已预测到日本厂良率会比美国厂高，只是实际的差异比我想象的更大。既然被嘱「看一下」，我就与日本厂和休士顿厂的同事（都是我的旧属）讨论了几次。我的结论如下：

差异在TAG——

先从作业员说起，日本厂的作业员流动率每年二人左右，「除非她要结婚了，否则她不会离开」；休士顿作业员在经济景气好、找别的工作容易时流动率高达每年25%，即使在经济不景气时也高达10%。高流动率造成公司常要聘雇无经验的作业员，但是这些无经验作业员经手的晶圆往往是零良率。还有，当我进入日本工厂时，作业员都专心工作，毫不理会我；但当我进入德州工厂时，作业员都转过头来看我——日本作业员显然比较专心工作。

再谈生产线领班，日本厂雇进的领班，至少有工程技职教育，还有许多还是正式大学工程毕业生；美国已雇不到工程毕业的领班。

<p class="en-para"><b>Japan-US Memory Yield Gap Analysis</b></p>
<p class="en-para">In 1981, TI's Miho, Japan fab began producing memory devices that TI's Houston fab was already making. Suddenly, news arrived — Miho's yields were more than double Houston's (Houston was at roughly 20%-plus yields; Miho achieved 40%-50%).</p>
<p class="en-para">Shepherd was shocked! He ordered Houston to immediately improve! Houston engineers were turned upside down, with no idea what to do to improve. As "Quality and Productivity Director," I was also asked to "take a look."</p>
<p class="en-para">Actually, I had long predicted that Japanese fab yields would be higher than American — but the actual gap was even larger than I'd imagined. Having been asked to "take a look," I discussed several times with colleagues at both the Japan and Houston fabs (all former subordinates). My conclusion:</p>
<p class="en-para">The difference was in TAG —</p>
<p class="en-para">Starting with operators: Japan had an annual operator turnover rate of around 2%. "Unless she gets married, she won't leave." Houston operators had turnover rates as high as 25% annually in good economic times when other jobs were easy to find, and even in bad times it ran 10%. High turnover meant the company constantly hired inexperienced operators, but wafers handled by these inexperienced operators often had zero yield. Also, when I entered the Japanese plant, operators focused on their work and completely ignored me. But when I entered the Texas plant, operators turned their heads to look at me — Japanese operators were clearly more focused on their work.</p>
<p class="en-para">As for line supervisors: Japan hired supervisors with at least engineering vocational education, and many were full university engineering graduates. America could no longer hire engineering graduates as supervisors.</p>

### Cheat Sheet · 商业语汇

1. **Yield gap as mirror (良率差距作为一面镜子)**
**中文解释**：美浦厂的良率是休士顿厂的两倍以上——这个数字背后的不是技术差距，而是人力资本差距（作业员流动率、领班教育水平、工程师素质）；在半导体制造中，「良率」是一个综合性的组织能力指标——它反映的不是单一环节，而是整个工厂的人力品质系统。
**商业造句">The yield gap between Miho and Houston was not fundamentally a technology gap — it was a human capital gap that revealed itself through turnover rates, supervisor education, and engineer quality.*

2. **Operator turnover rate as quality killer (作业员流动率作为良率杀手)**
**中文解释**：日本厂作业员流动率约2%/年 vs. 休士顿厂高达25%/年——高流动率是新进无经验作业员比例的直接来源；在制造业中，「作业员流动率」是良率波动的第一大来源——新入职工人经手晶圆往往是零良率。
**商业造句">The 25% annual operator turnover at Houston versus 2% at Miho was the single most devastating driver of yield loss — inexperienced hands on wafers meant zero yield.*
