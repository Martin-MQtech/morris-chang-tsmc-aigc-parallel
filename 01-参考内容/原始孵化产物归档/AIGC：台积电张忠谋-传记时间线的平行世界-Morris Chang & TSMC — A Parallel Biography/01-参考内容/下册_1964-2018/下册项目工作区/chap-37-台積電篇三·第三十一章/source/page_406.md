## 任命蔡力行（二○○五年）

在二○○五年，我郑重考虑的只有两位——曾繁城和蔡力行。

曾繁城在他的工研院的长官都不愿参加台积电的情形下，带领工研院的「核心团队」到台积电（见第十八章），建立了不少汗马功勢。除此之外，他还建立了一个重要的功劳，就是引进了好几位优秀的旅美华裔人才，蔡力行就是其中之一。

事实上，蔡力行相当早（一九八九年底）就认知台积电潜力而参加台积电。论人情、论资历，应该让曾繁城先有机会做CEO，但在半导体这个无情的、变化迅速的行业中，又怎能只讲人情和资历？蔡力行当时正值盛年（五十四岁，小曾繁城七年），在台积电也已建立了不少功劳。他的潜力也为我、曾繁城以及许多同仁所欣赏。

二○○五年五月，我提请董事会任命蔡力行为台积电CEO。

## 专任董事长

此后四年，我的工作生活有相当大的改变。我放弃了公司给在新竹的公寓。如无特别事情，每星期大约只去新竹办公室一天。这一天内，固定的时程是CEO单独对我的简报（约两小时），以及财务长、法务长对我的简报（约各一小时）。我也主持每月一次的「经营委员会」午餐。当然，我最重要的工作是准备及主持每季一次的董事会。在那时期，董事会都在台北办事处的会议室召开。

在待遇上，我自动放弃了分红，只保持薪水。台积电高级人员的待遇一直以分红为主，尤其在那时期，分红主要是以股票面值发给。所以我的放弃分红，在待遇上是一个相当大的损失，但这本来就是「专任」董事长的一部分。

<p class="en-para">In 2005, there were only two candidates I seriously considered—Fan-Cheng Tseng and Ming-Lieh Tsai.</p>
<p class="en-para">Fan-Cheng Tseng had led ITRI's 'core team' to TSMC—despite the reluctance of his superiors at ITRI (see Chapter 18)—and achieved considerable distinction. Beyond that, he had a further important contribution: he recruited several outstanding overseas Chinese talents to TSMC, Ming-Lieh Tsai among them.</p>
<p class="en-para">In fact, Ming-Lieh Tsai had recognized TSMC's potential quite early and joined in late 1989. By considerations of courtesy and seniority, Fan-Cheng Tseng should have had the first opportunity to become CEO. But in the ruthless, fast-changing semiconductor industry, could one rely solely on courtesy and seniority? Tsai was then in his prime at fifty-four, seven years younger than Tseng, and had already made significant contributions at TSMC. His potential was appreciated by me, by Tseng, and by many colleagues.</p>
<p class="en-para">In May 2005, I asked the board to appoint Ming-Lieh Tsai as TSMC's CEO.</p>
<p class="en-para">Over the next four years, my work life changed considerably. I gave up the company apartment in Hsinchu. Unless something urgent arose, I went to the Hsinchu office only about one day a week. On that day, my fixed schedule was a one-on-one briefing from the CEO (about two hours), followed by briefings from the chief financial officer and the general counsel (about one hour each). I also chaired the monthly management committee lunch. Most importantly, I prepared for and presided over the quarterly board meetings, which were then held in the conference room at TSMC's Taipei office.</p>
<p class="en-para">On compensation, I voluntarily gave up my dividend and retained only my salary. TSMC's senior executives had always been compensated primarily through dividends, especially at that time when dividends were paid mainly in stock at par value. So giving up dividends was a significant loss in compensation, but that was inherently part of the role of a full-time chairman.</p>
