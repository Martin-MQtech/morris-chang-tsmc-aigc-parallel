## 二○○八至二○○九年金融危机与变相裁员

二○○七年底我在华府威受到全球经济与金融业的「山雨欲来」，终于在二○○八年夏季真正来临到台积电的订单簿上。我在第二十九章曾叙述「台积电从接订单到交货大约需四、五个月」，所以二○○八年夏季我们就知道第四季的营收会相当差。这次市场萎縮程度甚至高于二○○一年第一季互联网泡沫破裂时的萎縮。二○○八年第三季的营收是创纪录的三十亿美元，但第四季营收只有二十亿美元。二○○九年第一季的营收更差，只剩十一亿三千美元。营业利益更大幅缩水，看来二○○九年第一季已到达盈亏边缘。

这样大的萎縮，即使对有相当经验的经理人来说都会是一个大考验。但对担任CEO才三年、而这三年又相当顺利的蔡力行，尤其如此。通常具有积极性格的经理人，在危机时刻都会有本能性的反应——除了全面节省开支外，往往就是「裁员」！

我对「裁员」在德仪时已有相当定见及经验（见第六章「一九七○年裁员」节）。我也获悉力行在考虑裁员，所以特別告诫他：如果你要裁员，务必把此议题提到董事会来，让董事会决定。

但是力行没有这样做。大概是认为我可以「免责」吧。他以「绩效评估」为由，资遣了「绩效评估」最差的八百余人。台积电一直都实施「绩效评估」，但绩效最差的员工最多只会被「留职观察」。我一直认为「留职观察」对被评估的同仁是一个正面行为——可以帮助他们调到适任的职务。

<p class="en-para">The 'gathering storm' I had sensed in Washington at the end of 2007 truly arrived on TSMC's order book in the summer of 2008. As I noted in Chapter 29, TSMC requires roughly four to five months from order to delivery, so by summer 2008 we knew the fourth quarter would be quite weak. The magnitude of this market contraction even exceeded that of the first quarter of 2001 dot-com bubble collapse. TSMC's third-quarter 2008 revenue was a record three billion dollars, but the fourth quarter fell to two billion. The first quarter of 2009 was even worse, dropping to only 1.13 billion dollars. Operating profit shrank dramatically as well; it appeared that TSMC was on the brink of losses in the first quarter of 2009.</p>
<p class="en-para">Such a severe contraction would be a formidable test even for an experienced manager. For Ming-Lieh Tsai, who had been CEO for only three years and had enjoyed an untroubled tenure, it was especially difficult. Managers with proactive temperaments typically react instinctively in a crisis: beyond across-the-board cost cutting, the common response is layoffs.</p>
<p class="en-para">I already had strong views and experience regarding layoffs from my days at TI (see Chapter 6, 'The 1970 Layoffs'). I also learned that Tsai was considering layoffs, so I specifically cautioned him: if you decide to lay off employees, you must bring the issue before the board and let the board decide.</p>
<p class="en-para">But Tsai did not do so. He likely believed I could be held blameless. On the grounds of 'performance evaluation,' he laid off more than eight hundred employees who had received the lowest ratings. TSMC had always conducted performance evaluations, but the lowest-rated employees had never been terminated—they had only been placed on a performance improvement plan. I had always regarded the improvement plan as a constructive measure, designed to help employees transition to roles better suited to their abilities.</p>
