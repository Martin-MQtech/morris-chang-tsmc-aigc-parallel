## 收回CEO职（二○○九年）

管理（management）及治理（governance）

「假淘汰、真裁員」事件给了我很深的感触。身为主管的同仁对公司的制度似乎还不够了解。

在此书已不只提过一次：董事会是公司的最高管理及治理权威，所有最重要的决定都需要通过董事会。做公司重要决定不董事会的权，而且是董事会的责。那么什么是重要决定？每次董事会，同仁们习以为常地提许多资本投资案件。CEO或总经理也常提人事案。难道裁员没有比一个资本投资案重要？还有，我的确一直以为「绩效评估」应该是一个正面制度——一个对公司、员工都好的制度，但这次却沦为裁員的工具。

这次事件的受害者——蔡力行——他做的决定后来被我反轉。但他也最可能继承我在台积电职务的人。我该怎么处理他？

让他做「新事业」总经理吧！同薪、同阶。当时台积电的「新事业」是太阳能和LED。那时已成立一个新事业单位，现在请现任CEO转任这大家都看好的「未来科技业」的总经理，也可以让他新领域发挥一番，同时保留了继承我职位的机会。如果他像我当年一样，他不会为一个挫折灰心，他会继续准备「东山再起」。

想定了这个办法，我在五月下旬与董事们个别通电话征求他们同意，他们都同意了。

六月十一日董事会全体无异议通过：我重兼CEO职务，蔡力行任新事业总经理。

【注释】

[1] 为蔡力行保留继承我职务的机会并没有圆满的结局。外在因素使得台积电的「新事业」并不成功——大陆大力投资太阳能，造成太阳能产能过剩；LED又是少数厂商把持知识产权的局面。力在努力数年后，于二○一四年离开台积电，转中华电信公司董事长，又于二○一七年转联发科CEO。台积电栽培的人才最后为别家公司所用，对台积电来说是非常可惜的事。

<p class="en-para">Management and Governance</p>
<p class="en-para">The 'fake elimination, real layoffs' incident left a deep impression on me. Managerial employees did not seem to understand the company's systems well enough.</p>
<p class="en-para">As I have stated more than once in this book, the board of directors is the company's highest management and governance authority; all the most important decisions require board approval. Making important company decisions is not the CEO's right but the board's responsibility. So what constitutes an important decision? At every board meeting, managers routinely present numerous capital investment proposals. The CEO or general manager also frequently submits personnel matters. Were layoffs not more significant than a capital investment proposal? Moreover, I had always believed that 'performance evaluation' should be a constructive system—one that benefits both the company and its employees. But this time it had been reduced to a tool for layoffs.</p>
<p class="en-para">The victim of this incident—Ming-Lieh Tsai—had made decisions that I later reversed. Yet he was also the person most likely to succeed me at TSMC. How should I handle him?</p>
<p class="en-para">I would make him general manager of the 'New Business' division—same salary, same rank. At the time, TSMC's 'new business' encompassed solar energy and LEDs. A new business unit had already been established. Now I would ask the sitting CEO to transfer to the general manager of this 'future technology' venture that everyone看好, allowing him to make his mark in a new area while preserving his chance to succeed me. If he was like me in those days, he would not be discouraged by a single setback. He would continue to prepare for his return.</p>
<p class="en-para">Once I had settled on this approach, I called each director individually in late May to seek their approval, and they all agreed.</p>
<p class="en-para">On June 11, the board unanimously approved: I would resume the CEO role, and Ming-Lieh Tsai would become general manager of the New Business division.</p>
<p class="en-para">[Note] The arrangement to preserve Tsai's path to succession did not end well. External factors prevented TSMC's 'new business' from succeeding—the mainland China's heavy investment in solar energy created overcapacity, and the LED sector was dominated by a handful of firms holding key patents. After several years of effort, Tsai left TSMC in 2014 to become chairman of Chunghwa Telecom, and in 2017 became CEO of MediaTek. The talent cultivated by TSMC ultimately benefited another company, a regrettable outcome for TSMC.</p>
