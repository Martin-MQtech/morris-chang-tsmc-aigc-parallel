# 台积电篇二·第二十三章｜记忆体的诱惑──世界先进和德碁

## 记忆体的诱惑

本书读者应该对记忆体这名词不陌生。记忆体是德仪半导体重产品之一，在第九章内"记忆体的奋斗"一节，我详述我在德仪经手记忆体事业的甜酸苦辣；老实说，在那时期，不如意的日子超过如意的日子；所以在办台积电时，我已不想碰记忆体。想不到，命运捉弄人，在台积电创办七年后，我又被推人记忆体的漩涡中。到底是怎么发生的？

虽然记忆体对我没有什么吸引力，但正想进入半导体业者特别有诱惑力，因为粗看起来，进入记忆体的门槛似乎较其他半导体产品为低。记忆体的市场大，竞争者少。全球IC市场80%是逻辑，有几百家竞争者；20%是记忆体，只有几家竞争者，而且大部分"记忆体"都是标准型的"商品"，只要依样炮制，就卖得出去。

但是，我几十年经营半导体公司的经验告诉我：虽然记忆体竞争者少，但竞争者都是强大，而且大部分是专做记忆体的公司——七零、八零年代是日本公司，九零年代以后是韩国公司。他们绝对不好惹。

我的经验也告诉我："客制化"产品（Custom Products）通常比标准型商品容易赚钱。还有很重要的一点：记忆体的设计和制程开发必须密切配合。八零年代米德（Carver Mead）与康威（Lynn Conway）把逻辑设计标准化，点出了逻辑晶圆代工的可能。但记忆体设计从未标准化。所以，要成立一家有竞争力的记忆体公司，必须具备设计与制程都强的能力。

<p class="en-para">## The Lure of Memory</p>
<p class="en-para">Readers of this book should be familiar with the term "memory." Memory was one of TI's major semiconductor products. In Chapter 9, in a section titled "The Struggle of Memory," I detailed the ups and downs of my experience running the memory business at TI. Frankly, during that period, there were more bad days than good. So when founding TSMC, I had no desire to touch memory again. Who would have thought—fate plays tricks on people—seven years after founding TSMC, I was pushed back into the memory vortex. How did this happen?</p>
<p class="en-para">Although memory held no attraction for me, it was particularly alluring for those wanting to enter the semiconductor business, because at first glance the barrier to entry into memory seemed lower than for other semiconductor products. The memory market was large with few competitors. 80% of the global IC market was logic, with hundreds of competitors; 20% was memory, with only a few competitors. Moreover, most "memory" products were standard "commodities"—you could just replicate them and they'd sell.</p>
<p class="en-para">But my decades of experience running semiconductor companies told me: although memory had fewer competitors, those competitors were formidable, and most specialized in memory—Japanese companies in the 70s and 80s, Korean companies after the 90s. They were absolutely not to be trifled with.</p>
<p class="en-para">My experience also taught me: "custom" products usually make money more easily than standard commodities. There was another very important point: memory design and process development had to work hand in glove. In the 1980s, Carver Mead and Lynn Conway standardized logic design, pointing to the possibility of logic wafer foundry. But memory design was never standardized. So to build a competitive memory company, one needed to be strong in both design and process technology.</p>
</div>

## 德碁的起源

在一九八八、八九，全球记忆体市场已经好几年缺货，记忆体价格居高不下，日、韩的记忆体公司都赚了翻了，此时台湾就有两位关键人士，中了记忆体的诱惑。

一位是宏碁集团施振荣董事长。一九八八年七月，施董事长告诉我：宏碁电脑需要大量记忆体，记忆体是它成本的一大块，所以施董事长预备在宏碁集团下成立一家记忆体公司，既可自给，又可省钱，甚至还可卖给别的客户赚钱。

但是设计和制程技术呢？施董事长说可以从德仪移转。当然这要付德仪相当高的技术费。董事长的计画犯了我的大忌：技术不自主！但是在他告诉我他的记忆体计画时，我已接近任工研院院长的终点。我在工研院的三大改革，至少两个已失败；我的副院长已辞职，我已"众叛亲离"；我的两个上司：经济部部长和行政院院长，都已说我"不善处理人事"。

在这样的处境下，我实在不想质疑台湾电脑界的大老施董事长。于是，我只讲了几句不著边际的话。事后证明，我这样做是对的，因为七个月后（一九八九年二月），施要对经济部长陈履安报告他的记忆体公司（PEGE）计画，陈部长嘱我参与，我听了德仪移转技术的详细条件后，觉得实在苛刻，就在会中表达。陈部长立刻打断我的话说："这是宏碁的事，你不必过问。"后来陈部长做结论时说："政府既不投资，乐观其成。"

<span class="rebook-translation__label">ReadShift 双语翻译</span>
<p class="en-para">In 1988-89, the global memory market had been in shortage for several years. Memory prices stayed high, and Japanese and Korean memory companies were making a killing. At this time, two key figures in Taiwan fell to the lure of memory.</p>
<p class="en-para">One was Acer Group Chairman Shi Zhengrong (Stan Shih). In July 1988, Chairman Shi told me: Acer needed large quantities of memory; memory accounted for a big chunk of its cost. So Chairman Shi planned to establish a memory company under the Acer Group—both self-serving and cost-saving, and potentially selling to other customers for profit.</p>
<p class="en-para">But what about design and process technology? Chairman Shi said it could be transferred from TI. Of course, this would require paying TI quite high technology fees. Chairman Shi's plan violated my cardinal rule: technological non-autonomy! But when he told me about his memory plan, I was nearing the end of my tenure as ITRI president. Two of my three major ITRI reforms had failed; my vice president had resigned; I had been "deserted by friends and followers." My two superiors—the Minister of Economic Affairs and the Premier—had both said I was "poor at handling people."</p>
<p class="en-para">In this situation, I really didn't want to question Chairman Shi, an elder of Taiwan's computer industry. So I said only a few noncommittal words. It turned out that my approach was correct. Seven months later (February 1989), Shi reported to Economic Minister Chen Lian-an on his memory company (PEGE) plan. Minister Chen asked me to participate. After hearing the detailed terms of TI's technology transfer, I felt they were truly harsh and said so at the meeting. Minister Chen immediately cut me off: "This is Acer's business; you need not interfere." Later Minister Chen concluded: "The government won't invest; we're happy to see it succeed."</p>
</div>
