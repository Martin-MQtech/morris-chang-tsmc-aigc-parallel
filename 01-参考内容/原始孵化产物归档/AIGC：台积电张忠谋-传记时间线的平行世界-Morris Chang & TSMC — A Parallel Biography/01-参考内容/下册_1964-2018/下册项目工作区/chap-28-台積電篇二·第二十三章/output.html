记忆体的诱惑

本书读者应该对记忆体这名词不陌生。记忆体是德仪半导体重产品之一，在第九章内"记忆体的奋斗"一节，我详述我在德仪经手记忆体事业的甜酸苦辣；老实说，在那时期，不如意的日子超过如意的日子；所以在办台积电时，我已不想碰记忆体。想不到，命运捉弄人，在台积电创办七年后，我又被推人记忆体的漩涡中。到底是怎幺发生的？

虽然记忆体对我没有什幺吸引力，但正想进入半导体业者特别有诱惑力，因为粗看起来，进入记忆体的门槛似乎较其他半导体产品为低。记忆体的市场大，竞争者少。全球IC市场80%是逻辑，有几百家竞争者；20%是记忆体，只有几家竞争者，而且大部分"记忆体"都是标准型的"商品"，只要依样炮制，就卖得出去。

但是，我几十年经营半导体公司的经验告诉我：虽然记忆体竞争者少，但竞争者都是强大，而且大部分是专做记忆体的公司——七零、八零年代是日本公司，九零年代以后是韩国公司。他们绝对不好惹。

我的经验也告诉我："客制化"产品（Custom Products）通常比标准型商品容易赚钱。还有很重要的一点：记忆体的设计和制程开发必须密切配合。八零年代米德（Carver Mead）与康威（Lynn Conway）把逻辑设计标准化，点出了逻辑晶圆代工的可能。但记忆体设计从未标准化。所以，要成立一家有竞争力的记忆体公司，必须具备设计与制程都强的能力。

## The Lure of Memory

Readers of this book should be familiar with the term "memory." Memory was one of TI's major semiconductor products. In Chapter 9, in a section titled "The Struggle of Memory," I detailed the ups and downs of my experience running the memory business at TI. Frankly, during that period, there were more bad days than good. So when founding TSMC, I had no desire to touch memory again. Who would have thought—fate plays tricks on people—seven years after founding TSMC, I was pushed back into the memory vortex. How did this happen?

Although memory held no attraction for me, it was particularly alluring for those wanting to enter the semiconductor business, because at first glance the barrier to entry into memory seemed lower than for other semiconductor products. The memory market was large with few competitors. 80% of the global IC market was logic, with hundreds of competitors; 20% was memory, with only a few competitors. Moreover, most "memory" products were standard "commodities"—you could just replicate them and they'd sell.

But my decades of experience running semiconductor companies told me: although memory had fewer competitors, those competitors were formidable, and most specialized in memory—Japanese companies in the 70s and 80s, Korean companies after the 90s. They were absolutely not to be trifled with.

My experience also taught me: "custom" products usually make money more easily than standard commodities. There was another very important point: memory design and process development had to work hand in glove. In the 1980s, Carver Mead and Lynn Conway standardized logic design, pointing to the possibility of logic wafer foundry. But memory design was never standardized. So to build a competitive memory company, one needed to be strong in both design and process technology.

德碁的起源

在一九八八、八九，全球记忆体市场已经好几年缺货，记忆体价格居高不下，日、韩的记忆体公司都赚了翻了，此时台湾就有两位关键人士，中了记忆体的诱惑。

一位是宏碁集团施振荣董事长。一九八八年七月，施董事长告诉我：宏碁电脑需要大量记忆体，记忆体是它成本的一大块，所以施董事长预备在宏碁集团下成立一家记忆体公司，既可自给，又可省钱，甚至还可卖给别的客户赚钱。

但是设计和制程技术呢？施董事长说可以从德仪移转。当然这要付德仪相当高的技术费。董事长的计画犯了我的大忌：技术不自主！但是在他告诉我他的记忆体计画时，我已接近任工研院院长的终点。我在工研院的三大改革，至少两个已失败；我的副院长已辞职，我已"众叛亲离"；我的两个上司：经济部部长和行政院院长，都已说我"不善处理人事"。

在这样的处境下，我实在不想质疑台湾电脑界的大老施董事长。于是，我只讲了几句不着边际的话。事后证明，我这样做是对的，因为七个月后（一九八九年二月），施要对经济部长陈履安报告他的记忆体公司（PEGE）计画，陈部长嘱我参与，我听了德仪移转技术的详细条件后，觉得实在苛刻，就在会中表达。陈部长立刻打断我的话说："这是宏碁的事，你不必过问。"后来陈部长做结论时说："政府既不投资，乐观其成。"

In 1988-89, the global memory market had been in shortage for several years. Memory prices stayed high, and Japanese and Korean memory companies were making a killing. At this time, two key figures in Taiwan fell to the lure of memory.

One was Acer Group Chairman Shi Zhengrong (Stan Shih). In July 1988, Chairman Shi told me: Acer needed large quantities of memory; memory accounted for a big chunk of its cost. So Chairman Shi planned to establish a memory company under the Acer Group—both self-serving and cost-saving, and potentially selling to other customers for profit.

But what about design and process technology? Chairman Shi said it could be transferred from TI. Of course, this would require paying TI quite high technology fees. Chairman Shi's plan violated my cardinal rule: technological non-autonomy! But when he told me about his memory plan, I was nearing the end of my tenure as ITRI president. Two of my three major ITRI reforms had failed; my vice president had resigned; I had been "deserted by friends and followers." My two superiors—the Minister of Economic Affairs and the Premier—had both said I was "poor at handling people."

In this situation, I really didn't want to question Chairman Shi, an elder of Taiwan's computer industry. So I said only a few noncommittal words. It turned out that my approach was correct. Seven months later (February 1989), Shi reported to Economic Minister Chen Lian-an on his memory company (PEGE) plan. Minister Chen asked me to participate. After hearing the detailed terms of TI's technology transfer, I felt they were truly harsh and said so at the meeting. Minister Chen immediately cut me off: "This is Acer's business; you need not interfere." Later Minister Chen concluded: "The government won't invest; we're happy to see it succeed."

世界先进的起源

第二位中了记忆体诱惑的是当时工研院副院长史钦泰。钦泰是我在规画台积电时亲密度仅次于胡定华的同事，但在工研院衍生公司这课题上，他的看法与我很不一样。我认为衍生公司，人要移过去；他却不放人，认为工研院的半导体计画不只衍生一个公司，而是要衍生一个产业。

一个妈妈，要生许多孩子，而且这许多孩子要各有特色；不然，他们会互相竞争、互相残杀。这样的妈妈，工研院能做吗？

假使有人问我，我的答复会是斩钉截铁的"不能"。但是，我在八八年十二月已被调任为无权、不领薪、也没有什幺事的工研院董事长，钦泰已不必问我。大概他知道如果问我，我会反对，所以也不问。

另一方面，工研院的"美洲技术顾问团"在八九竭力推荐记忆体为工研院"下一个半导体计画"。于是，以记忆体为载具的"次微米计画"就在九零年上路。这计画有一特点：自始就预备衍生一家记忆体公司。所以，它的"实验室"就盖在新竹科学园区，实际上是一座厂房——以便"衍生"新公司后立刻可成为工厂。

我在九零年才陆续知道"次微米计画"的内容，立刻就认为这是一个野心极大的计画。它的目的是五年后（一九九五年）衍生一个记忆体公司；既然要衍生一个公司，这公司当然要有竞争力，那幺，从制程技术面来说，"次微米计画"必须紧紧跟随"摩尔定律"——这是它的前身（VLSI计画）在十年内都没做到的事；除制程技术外，记忆体公司还需要记忆体设计技术。工研院一直就没有好好发展设计技术，而且发展的只有简单的逻辑设计，几乎从未涉猎记忆体设计。

The second person to fall to the lure of memory was then-ITRI Vice President Shi Qin-tai (Shih Ching-tai). Qin-tai was my closest colleague in planning TSMC after Hu Ding-hua. But on the topic of ITRI spin-offs, his view was quite different from mine. I believed that when spinning off companies, people should move over. He didn't want to release people; he believed ITRI's semiconductor program should spin off not just one company but an entire industry.

One mother giving birth to many children—and these many children each having distinctive characteristics, otherwise they would compete with each other and kill each other. Could ITRI be that mother?

Had anyone asked me, my answer would have been a resounding "no." But in December 1988, I had been reassigned as ITRI chairman with no power, no salary, and nothing to do, so Qin-tai no longer needed to ask me. He probably knew I would oppose it, so he didn't bother asking.

On another front, ITRI's "Americas Technical Advisory Committee" vigorously pushed memory as ITRI's "next semiconductor program" in 1989. Thus, the memory-vehicle "Submicron Program" was launched in 1990. This program had one characteristic: from the start, it was designed to spin off a memory company. So its "laboratory" was built in Hsinchu Science Park—actually a fab building—so that after the new company was spun off, it could immediately become a fab.

I didn't learn the details of the "Submicron Program" until 1990. I immediately saw this as an extremely ambitious program. Its goal was to spin off a memory company five years later (1995). Since a company was to be spun off, it would of course need competitiveness. From a process technology standpoint, the "Submicron Program" had to keep pace with "Moore's Law"—something its predecessor (the VLSI Program) had not accomplished in ten years. Beyond process technology, a memory company also needed memory design technology. ITRI had never properly developed design technology, and what it had developed was only simple logic design—it had barely touched memory design.

李健熙的早餐

一九八九年一月，我刚卸任工研院院长职；施振荣董事长已告诉我他想自德仪购买记忆体技术、成立德碁记忆体公司的大致构想；但"次微米计画"还在史钦泰副院长心中酝酿，我尚不知情。此时我忽然接到韩国三星电子公司台北办事处的电话邀请：董事长李健熙正在台湾，想邀请我早餐。

当时大部分美国半导体公司已退出记忆体业，日本公司仍占据大部分记忆体市场，但因为《广场协议》和自，日币在一九五至八八年的三年内升值了一倍有余，所以日本记忆体公司已渐渐不支；相反地，韩国三星公司渐露锋芒。

我为了好奇而赴约——毕竟台积电不做记忆体，为什幺李健熙要请我吃早餐？

我同时也邀施振荣和史钦泰一同前往。邀施，是因为我也知道他正在想办记忆体公司；邀史，是因为他是我成立台积电的重要助手，也是工研院研发重心的代表人物。见面后发现，原来这次李健熙的角色是"说客"。他认为记忆体业需要很大资本、很多人才。台湾出不起这资本、聚不到这人才；所以，还是死了进入记忆体业的心吧！为了要让我们见识三星已投入的资本和人才，他邀请我们前往韩国访问三星。

我事后才知道，李健熙在邀我早餐时，已知道施董事长要办德碁（李的情报可能来自德仪管道）。至于为什幺用我做媒介？大概他从我在德仪的经历以及之后在台积电创立"专业晶圆代工"商业模式，知道我是一个"内行的半导体经营者"。

In January 1989, I had just stepped down as ITRI president. Chairman Shi Zhengrong had already told me his general vision of buying memory technology from TI to form a Tepro memory company. But the "Submicron Program" was still brewing in Vice President Shi Qin-tai's mind, and I was unaware of it. Then I suddenly received a phone call from the Taipei office of Korea's Samsung Electronics: Chairman Lee Kun-hee was in Taiwan and wanted to invite me to breakfast.

At that time, most American semiconductor companies had exited the memory business. Japanese companies still dominated most of the memory market, but because of the Plaza Accord, the Japanese yen had more than doubled in value against the USD between 1985 and 1988, so Japanese memory companies were gradually losing ground. Conversely, Korea's Samsung was emerging.

I attended out of curiosity—after all, TSMC didn't do memory; why would Lee Kun-hee want to buy me breakfast?

I also invited both Shi Zhengrong and Shi Qin-tai to come along. I invited Shi Zhengrong because I also knew he was thinking of starting a memory company. I invited Shi Qin-tai because he was an important aide in establishing TSMC and a representative figure of ITRI's R&D center of gravity. At the meeting, it turned out that Lee Kun-hee's role this time was as a "lobbyist." He believed the memory industry required huge capital and much talent. Taiwan couldn't afford the capital or gather the talent—so give up the idea of entering the memory industry! To show us the capital and talent Samsung had already invested, he invited us to visit Samsung in Korea.

I learned afterward that Lee Kun-hee already knew about Chairman Shi's plan to establish Tepro when he invited me to breakfast (Lee's intelligence may have come through TI channels). As for why he used me as an intermediary—probably from my experience at TI and afterward in creating the "dedicated foundry" business model at TSMC, he knew I was a "knowledgeable semiconductor operator."

访问三星

总之，我和史、施接受了李健熙的邀请访问三星，也在两个半月后（一九八九年四月十三至十五日）实践了我们的访问。在韩国受到了三星诚恳的款待。十三日晚上到达首尔，住进三星开办的旅馆；十四日参观三星记忆体工厂。在此之前，我已看过不少记忆体工厂——德仪日本、德仪美国、还有好几个日本记忆体公司。的确，与我看过德日、德仪美的工厂相比，三星的工厂毫不逊色。三星的工程师和经理人相当熟谙他们的工作，也都相当诚恳回答我们的问题。

十五日上午，李健熙在一个布置华丽的大会客室接见我们。也有几位三星经理人员陪同。我们人见，在我脑海中一瞬即逝的印象是：有点像古代中国边疆酋长进贡时拜见中国皇帝。

李健熙问我们：怎幺样，你们看到了吧？记忆体需要很大资本、很多人才。我已不记得我们的详细答复，总之是谢谢他，也说了些很佩服三星成就之类的话。当天下午我们就登机回台北。

这次访问，丝毫没有改变施董事长、史副院长对记忆体的看法。施董事长的德碁计画木已成舟，他在与李健熙早餐（一月）及访问三星（四月）中间，已在二月对经济部长报告要成立德碁。看了三星后，他还是继续积极进行德碁的开办。

史副院长在与李早餐前，已开始在心中酝酿以记忆体为载具、以五年内衍生记忆体公司为目标的"次微米计画"。访问三星后，他更积极对经济部倡议"次微米计画"。终于在一年多后，一九九零年七月，此计画正式开始。

In any case, Shi, Shi, and I accepted Lee Kun-hee's invitation to visit Samsung, and two and a half months later (April 13-15, 1989), we fulfilled our visit. We received warm hospitality from Samsung in Korea. On the evening of the 13th we arrived in Seoul and checked into a Samsung-operated hotel. On the 14th we toured Samsung's memory fab. Before this, I had seen quite a few memory fabs—TI Japan, TI U.S., and several Japanese memory companies. Indeed, compared to the TI Japanese, TI U.S. fabs I had seen, Samsung's fab was in no way inferior. Samsung's engineers and managers were highly knowledgeable about their work and answered our questions quite frankly.

On the morning of the 15th, Lee Kun-hee received us in a magnificently decorated boardroom. Several Samsung managers also accompanied him. A fleeting impression in my mind was: somewhat like a frontier tribal chief paying tribute to the Chinese emperor in ancient times.

Lee Kun-hee asked us: Well, did you see? Memory requires huge capital and lots of talent. I don't remember our exact replies—in any case, we thanked him, and said things like how much we admired Samsung's achievements. That afternoon we boarded a plane back to Taipei.

This visit didn't change Chairman Shi's or Vice President Shi's views of memory at all. Chairman Shi's Tepro plan was already a done deal—between the breakfast with Lee Kun-hee (January) and the Samsung visit (April), he had already reported to the Economic Minister in February about establishing Tepro. After seeing Samsung, he continued actively pursuing Tepro's founding.

Vice President Shi, before the breakfast with Lee, had already begun brewing in his mind the "Submicron Program" with memory as its vehicle and the goal of spinning off a memory company within five years. After visiting Samsung, he became more active in advocating for the "Submicron Program" to the Ministry of Economic Affairs. Finally, over a year later, in July 1990, the program officially began.

次微米计划与投标

工研院的"次微米计画"在九零年七月正式开始。史钦泰副院长深知开发记忆体需要设计及制程人才密切配合，他亲到美国求才，找到了卢志远、卢超群两兄弟。哥哥卢志远在贝尔实验室开发制程，弟弟卢超群在IBM设计——两人在各自领域都相当杰出，把他们两人找进"次微米计画"的确会是一个不错的安排。

但是问题发生了。志远愿意受聘为电子所副所长，并主持"次微米计画"，但超群志在创业，要在台湾开一家记忆体设计公司（即是后来的钰创科技），所以只愿意做"次微米计画"的设计承办者。史副院长在没有更好选择情形下，也同意了这安排。

这安排在第一个产品的开发相当成功，四年后（九四年中期），电子所和钰创团队已成功开发4M DRAM。但这安排的另一任务却被忽略了。4M DRAM的设计可以由钰创承办，但是下一个产品呢？本来要在"次微米计画"下建立自己的设计团队，但是等到真的要做，就发现有基本的矛盾和困难。台湾的IC设计人才本来就少，遇到好的，谁雇他（她）？是电子所还是钰创？何况竞争雇设计人才的公司还不只钰创，九三年又另一家记忆体设计公司从"钰创"衍生出去。

总之，直至九四年，在世界先进衍生的前夕，隶属工研院"次微米计画"的DRAM设计工程师只有两、三位。

ITRI's "Submicron Program" officially began in July 1990. Vice President Shi Qin-tai, keenly aware that memory development required close cooperation between design and process talent, personally went to the U.S. to recruit, finding the brothers Lu Chih-yuan and Lu Chao-chun. The elder brother Lu Chih-yuan developed processes at Bell Labs; the younger brother Lu Chao-chun designed at IBM—both were quite outstanding in their respective fields. Recruiting both into the "Submicron Program" was indeed a good arrangement.

But a problem emerged. Chih-yuan was willing to serve as deputy director of the Electronics Research Institute and lead the "Submicron Program." But Chao-chun aspired to start his own business—he wanted to open a memory design company in Taiwan (which later became ProMOS Technologies)—so he was only willing to be the design contractor for the "Submicron Program." Vice President Shi, lacking a better alternative, also agreed to this arrangement.

This arrangement was quite successful in developing the first product. Four years later (mid-1994), the ERI and ProMOS teams had successfully developed 4M DRAM. But another mission of this arrangement was neglected. The 4M DRAM design could be contracted to ProMOS, but what about the next product? The original intent was to build the program's own design team under the "Submicron Program," but when it came time to actually do it, fundamental contradictions and difficulties emerged. Taiwan's IC design talent was already scarce. When you found good talent, who hired them—ERI or ProMOS? Moreover, ProMOS wasn't the only company competing to hire design talent; in 1993 another memory design company "spun off" from ProMOS.

In any case, by 1994, on the eve of Vanguard International's spin-off, the DRAM design engineers belonging to ITRI's "Submicron Program" numbered only two or three.

"无法拒绝的机会"

更巧的，九四年秋，台积电在台湾上市，上市几天后股价涨到142.5元，是原始投资成本的十几倍（见第二十二章）。不少台积电的原始团队都发了一笔不大不小的财，被"次微米计画"的团队羡慕。"他们能，为什幺我们不能？"

赶快衍一家公司！赶快量！赶快上市！赶快发财！

在"次微米计画"团队急着要量4M DRAM、工研院电子所绝对看好衍生公司的情绪下，经济部在九三年底决定以"招标"方式决定谁主导衍生公司。

第二，当初我替工研院的VLSI计画找出路，衍生了台积电，而台积电又非常成功。现在工研院又要衍生一个半导体公司，难道我拒绝参与？正因为是台积电成功，大家才会有这样的期待。

现在，又一个"无法拒绝的机会"出现。还是去吧！不负大家的期望。再募一次资，再办一个公司。

我自经济部决定要招标时，就开始物色总经理。第一人史钦泰。他是"次微米计画之父"，也是台湾资深半导体研发者，所以是当然人选。史在九三年底也答应接受此职。但九四年初，史忽然被升任为工研院院长，他就告诉我：他宁愿当工研院院长，而不来世界先进做总经理了。

记忆体梦碎

德碁比世界先进早五年开始，但它的命运与世界先进相似。世界先进虽有自主记忆体技术，但技术跟不上竞争者，所以失败。德碁没有自主记忆体技术，它的技术来源——德仪，在九零年代决定退出。幸而在九九年台积电因为自己来不急扩充产能，决定为增加产能而购并德碁。自此德碁的招牌消失，它的工厂变成台积电的晶圆七厂。

九零年代德碁和世界先进在记忆体上的努力，是台湾企图强力进入记忆体业的勇敢一搏。但"爱拼才会赢"，世界先进和德碁拼了，而没有赢。幸而有台积电这一支救兵，才不致"死伤惨重"。

当年李健熙的"忠"告："台湾出不起记忆体业需要的资本，也没有足够人才……要是真的要做记忆体，还是与三星合作"，至少有一半是对的。的确，德碁和世界先进人才、资本都不够，但是李健熙漏了另一个重点："学习曲线"！在九零年代德碁和世界先进开始做记忆体时，三星已远在"学习曲线"的前面。
